## 1. The ordinary kernel-count filtration

Let A be elementary finite-dimensional, K an actual ideal contained in
J(A), K^2=0, and C=A/K. The quotient has the same labelled vertex
algebra S and all the same simples. Square-zero makes K a canonical
C-bimodule: changing a lift of either C action changes the product
by K^2, which is zero.

Choose an S-bimodule **linear** section s:C -> A preserving the
vertices, the unit and radical augmentation. Such a vector-space
section exists; no multiplicative section is assumed. Write

\[
A=s(C)\oplus K,\qquad J(A)=s(J(C))\oplus K.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Products of two C lifts have the ordinary C product plus an error in K.
Products of a lift with K give its canonical C action, while products
of two K letters vanish.

For inflated ordinary endpoint modules X,Y, use their normalized
two-sided ordinary A bar. Its differential has two count components:
b_0 preserves the number p of K letters; b_1 replaces two C letters
with one K letter and increases p by one. Endpoint actions are
ordinary C actions and kill K. Thus the decreasing filtration F^p
by **at least p kernel letters** is a filtration of the actual full
ordinary bar.

The projection to the ordinary C bar is a genuine chain map. Its kernel
N is exactly F^1. At a total bar degree n there are at most n letters,
so the filtration is finite in every degree. For a fixed sequence of
p K letters, associated b_0 homology is the C-block multibar

\[
X\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}K
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}\cdots
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}K\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}Y,
\tag{1.2}
\]

shifted by p. There are p copies of K. Later differentials have
(p,n) -> (p+r,n-1), r>=1.

The multiplicative error is allowed throughout. This kernel-letter
filtration is for an ordinary square-zero extension and is different
from the derived negative-weight and local excursion filtrations.

## 2. Perfect tensor-nilpotent kernels give an ordinary isomorphism

Assume the canonical **actual** C-bimodule K satisfies

\[
\operatorname{pd}_{C^e}K\le D<\infty,\qquad
K^{\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}\ell}=0\text{ in }D(C^e).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Set

\[
W=\ell-1,\qquad U=(\ell-1)(D+1).
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — Eventual ordinary Tor/Ext and a uniform page bound

For every labelled simple pair,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y)\xrightarrow{\cong}
\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(R_z,L_y)\quad(n>U+1).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The comparison is already injective for n>U. Dual ordinary Ext
inflation is an isomorphism in the same high degrees.
Every quotient-edge death occurs by kernel-count page W.

No ordinary or derived section is required, and no one-sided
projectivity of K is assumed.

#### Proof

Use Section 1 for N with simple endpoints. For p>=ell, the actual
derived tensor word K^{tensor_C^L p} is zero, so (1.2) vanishes.
For 1<=p<ell, choose a finite C^e-projective resolution of actual K
of length at most D. A p-fold derived tensor word is represented
by a total C^e-projective complex in homological degrees 0,...,pD.

To check projectivity here, elementary bimodule projectives have form
Ce_i tensor_k e_jC; the tensor over C of two such terms is another
sum of elementary bimodule projectives, with multiplicity
dim e_jCe_h. After tensoring the two simple endpoints, these terms
have no extra endpoint homology. Thus (1.2) has vertical homology
only in degrees 0,...,pD, and shifted total degree at most

\[
p(D+1)\le U.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently the first page of N is confined to total degrees <=U
and counts <=W. Every subsequent page is a subquotient at those
positions. Degreewise finite convergence proves H_n(N)=0 for n>U.

The exact sequence of actual ordinary bar complexes
0 -> N -> B_A -> B_C -> 0 gives (2.3) when both H_n(N) and
H_{n-1}(N) vanish, and injectivity when H_n(N)=0. A quotient-edge
class at p=0 can die only by a differential to count r; all counts
r>W already vanish on the first page. This proves the uniform
kernel-count death-page bound.

Ordinary finite-dimensional simple Ext is dual to the corresponding
Tor and the quotient map induces the stated inflation. QED.

The strict inequality n>U+1 is essential. Section 5 realizes failure
at n=U+1=2. The result is an eventual comparison; it does not assert
a low-degree retract, augmented formality or an algebra section.

### Lemma 2.2 — Both ordinary quotient modules have bounded ambient pd

Under (2.1),

\[
\operatorname{pd}_A C\le U,\qquad
\operatorname{pd}_{A^{op}}C\le U.
\tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

Apply the same actual bar filtration to R_z tensor_A^L Ce_j.
The count-zero first page is R_z e_j in degree zero. All p>=1
terms obey the bound (2.4), and all p>=ell terms are zero.
Thus Tor^A_n(R_z,Ce_j)=0 for every right simple z and n>U.
Minimal ordinary A-projective resolutions give pd_A Ce_j<=U
for every j, hence the first inequality.

For the second inequality compute e_iC tensor_A^L L_y directly.
The count-zero term is e_iL_y in degree zero; the C^e-projective
tensor-word resolution gives the same bound for all positive counts.
All left-simple tests vanish for n>U, proving the right-module pd
bound. This is a direct right-module argument, not opposite-+ transfer.
QED.

## 3. Strict dimension descent and all-size closure

### Theorem 3.1 — A nonsplit ordinary reduction retains a bad quotient

Under (2.1), A and C have infinite global dimension simultaneously.
If C has finite global dimension g, then

\[
\operatorname{gldim}A\le g+U.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If A has finite global dimension h, then

\[
\operatorname{gldim}C\le\max(h,U+1).
\tag{3.2}
\]

When C is infinite +, A is infinite +.
If K!=0, a hypothetical bad A therefore gives a bad C with
strictly smaller positive integer dim_k C.

#### Proof

Every A simple is an inflated C simple because K is radical.
Resolve it by C projectives; by Lemma 2.2 each inflated term has
ambient pd at most U. A finite length-g resolution gives (3.1).
Conversely (2.3) makes every simple-pair C-Tor above max(h,U+1)
zero, proving (3.2). Together these prove equivalence of infinity.

A fixed supported infinite C-Tor tail has e_yCe_z!=0.
The ordinary quotient map is surjective on that Peirce block,
so e_yAe_z!=0. Isomorphism (2.3) lifts the same fixed pair in all
sufficiently large degrees. This proves infinite-+ transfer.

If A had infinite global dimension and were not infinite +, these
two results make C also have infinite global dimension and not
be infinite +. For nonzero K,
dim_k C=dim_k A-dim_k K<dim_k A. QED.

The theorem closes every checked finite tower of such ordinary
square-zero quotients over a justified Gap-A base class, at arbitrary
finite size. It can join C069's split radical moves and C061/C067's
eligible vertex moves in the same strictly decreasing algebra dimension.
Hypotheses must be checked anew at each step; inheritance or existence
of the next move is not asserted.

Unlike C069, there is no general inequality gldim C<=gldim A:
a nonsplit quotient can increase finite global dimension. The
isomorphism only starts above the stated cutoff.

## 4. Actual one-sided-projective kernels: cycles close without a period condition

There is a second ordinary closure in the same filtration. Assume
every actual column Ke_j is left C-projective and write

\[
Ke_j\cong\bigoplus_i(Ce_i)^{T(i,j)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

No C^e-perfectness or tensor nilpotence is needed for this subsection.

### Theorem 4.1 — Ordinary projective-column formula

The full formal series of actual ordinary Tor is

\[
\boxed{
\left(\sum_{n\ge0}\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,Ce_j)t^n\right)_{zj}
=(I-tT)^{-1}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

A cycle in the actual multiplicity graph makes A infinite +.
If T is acyclic on v vertices, actual K^{tensor_C^L v}=0,
and pd_A C<=v-1.

#### Proof

Each C-block tensor word with projective test Ce_j is degree-zero
homology, of dimension (T^p)_{zj}, shifted by p. Actual left
projectivity at each right tensor step proves this by decomposing
actual projective columns. The first page therefore has n=p.

Every possible later differential increases count p by r>=1 but
lowers degree n by one. It cannot connect n=p to n'=p+r.
Hence all pages collapse for these test modules, including the effect
of nonsplit multiplication errors. Finite convergence gives (4.2).

A repeated actual directed cycle produces a diagonal test-column
Tor class in each multiple of its length. Ordinary finite-length
detection selects a fixed y with e_yCe_j!=0, hence e_yAe_j!=0,
and infinitely many Tor^A_n(R_j,L_y). Thus A is infinite +.

If the graph is acyclic, T^v=0. Actual left-projectivity identifies
derived tensor words with ordinary projective tensor words, so this
is actual tensor nilpotence, not nilpotence of resolution terms.
Formula (4.2) gives pd_A C<=v-1. QED.

For actual right-projective K, decompose e_iK into e_jC and compute
e_iC tensor_A^L L_y directly. The series is the analogous row-matrix
inverse. Cycles give supported ordinary return and the acyclic case
bounds pd_{A^{op}} C. No opposite-transfer assumption is used.

Consequently, if actual K is also C^e-perfect, every such ordinary
square-zero extension over a Gap-A algebra C satisfies Gap A:
cyclic matrices give a new supported A tail; acyclic matrices give
the tensor nilpotence needed by Theorems 2.1--3.1.

No periodic-gcd restriction is needed in (4.2). The ordinary count
is monotone because K^2=0 and the extension error increases count.
In T10's derived many-layer model, binary negative products can
decrease count; its aperiodic counterexample remains valid.

The nonperfect acyclic case here has the stated finite ambient
quotient pd, but no supported-tail transfer or universal ordinary
comparison has yet been proved in full generality. The following
tensor-square-zero subcase has a supported bridge without perfectness.

### Theorem 4.2 — Nonperfect tensor-square-zero kernels still preserve supported infinity

Assume actual K is left C-projective and

\[
K\otimes_C K=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then C infinite + implies A infinite +. No C^e-perfectness or
ordinary section is required. More precisely, a persistent supported
C pair (R_z,L_y) forces a persistent supported ordinary A pair
with the same L_y and right simple either R_z or a composition
factor of T_z=R_z tensor_C K.

If all reverse-supported A-Tor vanishes for every n>=M, M>=1,
then all reverse-supported C-Tor vanishes for every n>=M+2.
A hypothetical bad A gives a bad C with strictly smaller algebra
dimension when K!=0. Also pd_A C<=1 and finite gldim C=g gives
gldim A<=g+1.

#### Proof

Actual left projectivity makes (4.3) an actual derived tensor square
zero and makes R_z tensor_C^L K the ordinary right module T_z.
All counts p>=2 of the kernel N in Section 1 have zero associated
homology. Its only remaining count gives, for n>=1 (and H_0(N)=0),

\[
H_n(N(R_z,L_y))\cong
\operatorname{Tor}^C_{n-1}(T_z,L_y).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Associativity of actual derived tensor gives T_z tensor_C^L K=0.
Apply the same ordinary count filtration with right endpoint
T_z instead of R_z. Every positive-count first-page term is zero,
so its actual ordinary projection is an isomorphism

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(T_z,L_y)\cong
\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(T_z,L_y)\quad\text{for every }n.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For an original C-supported pair, e_yCe_z!=0. A simple R_j
appears in T_z precisely with multiplicity T(z,j) in (4.1).
If that multiplicity is positive, the actual projective column
Ke_j contains a summand Ce_z. Restricting it by e_y gives

\[
e_yKe_j\ne0,\quad\text{hence }e_yAe_j\ne0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

This uses the actual ordinary ideal K inside A; negative cohomology
of an idempotent derived quotient does not supply such support.

The actual bar long exact sequence has the segment

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(R_z,L_y)\longrightarrow
H_{n-1}(N(R_z,L_y)).
\tag{4.7}
\]

If the middle term persists, either the original ordinary A pair
persists, or (4.4)--(4.5) force persistent ordinary
Tor^A_{n-2}(T_z,L_y). Finite composition factors select one fixed
R_j with persistent Tor^A(R_j,L_y). Support is (4.6). This is an
ordinary supported bridge even when the original quotient-edge
image vanishes; it is not a same-pair survival theorem.

For the numerical statement, all factors R_j in T_z are A-visible
by (4.6), so the ordinary finite-length inequality gives
Tor^A_m(T_z,L_y)=0 for m>=M. Equations (4.4)--(4.7) then make
Tor^C_n(R_z,L_y)=0 for n>=M+2. This holds for every C-supported
pair, while the original A pair also vanishes for n>=M.

The matrix T has T^2=0 by (4.3); (4.2) gives pd_A C<=1.
All simples are C simples, so the finite C-projective resolution
argument gives gldim A<=g+1. Thus infinite global dimension of
bad A passes to C, while the supported bridge forbids C infinite +.
The nonzero ideal strictly decreases dimension. QED.

The direct right-projective version sets U_y=K tensor_C L_y.
Its count-one kernel homology is Tor^C_{n-1}(R_z,U_y);
K tensor_C^L U_y=0 gives the all-degree ordinary comparison with
U_y. A factor L_i of U_y has positive row multiplicity B(i,y).
For e_yCe_z!=0, actual right projectivity gives e_iKe_z!=0, so

No opposite-side infinite-+ transfer is used.

Consequently every checked tower of these tensor-square-zero,

\[
\operatorname{gldim}A\le (v-1)(M+2r)+r.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The general nonperfect acyclic case with tensor index greater than

## 5. Actual connected nonsplit calibration and sharp cutoff

Let A have quiver

\[
0\xrightarrow{\alpha}1\xrightarrow{\beta}2
\xrightarrow{\delta}3,\qquad t:3\longrightarrow3,
\quad t^2=0,
\tag{5.1}
\]

with no other relations. This is a finite connected elementary
monomial algebra. A basis consists of the four vertices, the four
arrows, and

\[
\gamma=\beta\alpha,\quad \eta=\delta\beta,\quad
\theta=t\delta,\quad \zeta=\delta\beta\alpha,\quad
\iota=t\delta\beta,\quad \kappa=t\delta\beta\alpha.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus dim_k A=14. Put K=(gamma)=span{gamma,zeta,kappa}.
It is an actual radical square-zero ideal. The quotient C=A/K
has dimension 11, with the extra relation beta alpha=0.

The canonical C-bimodule is

\[
K\cong Ce_2\otimes_k e_0C.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed Ce_2 has basis e_2,delta,t delta and e_0C has basis e_0.
Map these to gamma,zeta,kappa. The displayed map preserves both
actual quotient actions. Hence D=0. Since e_0Ce_2=0,
K^{tensor_C^L 2}=0 and ell=2. The parameters are W=1,U=1.
As actual ambient modules, K is Ae_2 on the left and three copies
of e_0A on the right, so both ambient pd values are zero as well.

Nevertheless A -> C has **no unital ordinary algebra section**.
If one existed, C069's conjugation would align it with the vertices.
The alpha and beta Peirce spaces in A each have their unique arrow
lift, with no K component of those endpoint types. Their product
is nonzero gamma in A, whereas beta alpha is zero in C.
This contradicts multiplicativity.

The finite ordinary module certificates give

\[
0\to Ae_1\to Ae_0\to L_0\to0,
\qquad
0\to Ce_2\to Ce_1\to Ce_0\to L_0\to0,
\tag{5.4}
\]

with radical maps alpha and beta in algebraic product order.
These maps are exact by the displayed path bases; they are minimal.
Consequently

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^A_2(R_2,L_0)=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Tor}^C_2(R_2,L_0)=k.
\tag{5.5}

This fails the comparison exactly at n=U+1. In the kernel-count
sequence, the quotient word beta|alpha is sent on page one to
the nonzero one-kernel-letter class gamma.

At vertex 3, Omega L_3=L_3 over both A and C, by multiplication
with t in the projective Ae_3=Ce_3=span{e_3,t}. Therefore the
supported ordinary diagonal Tor is k in every degree, over every
field. The comparison there is the identity on the words t|...|t.
A is infinite +, not a counterexample.

All other left simple resolutions are finite: at vertices 1 and 2
the radical projective columns are Ae_2 and Ae_3 respectively,
and the quotient columns have the same descriptions; at vertex 0

The three-vertex path subexample, omitting vertex 3 and delta,t,

The connected calibration is a whole ordinary ideal quotient,

### 5.2 A nonsplit nonperfect calibration: supported transfer without full comparison

Let A_tri have basis e_0,e_1,t,a,b, with t:0 -> 0,

\[

\tag{5.6}
\]

For left modules, L_1 is projective over both algebras. In A_tri,
rad(Ae_0)=At plus ka, where At=span{t,b} and ka=L_1.

Consequently the supported (R_0,L_0) diagonal has ordinary Tor

A-Tor zero for n>=2 and C-Tor k for every n>=1. Its quotient

This pair is reverse-invisible because e_0Ae_1=0.

All displayed certificates hold over every field. A_tri is infinite +,
not a counterexample. It is an actual nonsplit nonperfect instance
