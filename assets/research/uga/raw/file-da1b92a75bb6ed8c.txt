\[

1. M_d=0 for d>a;
2. pd_{C^e} M_d<=D for every nonzero layer;
3. the graded C-bimodule
   \[
   \mathcal M=\bigoplus_{1\le d\le a}M_d[d]
   \quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad
   \mathcal M^{\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}\ell}=0.
   \tag{1.1}
   \]

\[
W=a(\ell-1),\qquad U=(\ell-1)(a+1+D).
\tag{1.2}
\]

If all layers vanish, the bar comparison is an isomorphism in every degree.
The displayed bounds also hold with ell=1 and U=W=0, though they are not

## 2. A bounded homology kernel

For a retained pair let

\[
N=\ker\bigl(B_H(R_z,L_y)\longrightarrow B_C(R_z,L_y)\bigr).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus N consists of bar words containing at least one negative letter.
Every operation preserves this property: m_2 preserves total negative
weight; m_r, r>=3, raises it by r-2. In particular (2.1) is a short exact
sequence of actual chain complexes. Use the existing negative-weight
filtration, with an M_d letter of weight d and homological bar degree d+1.

### Lemma 2.1 — The binary first page has a finite degree and weight range

For the binary differential b_2 on N, its homology is zero in negative
weight >W and in total homological degree >U.

#### Proof

There can be nonzero binary products of two negative letters. To compute
this first page, filter the **binary complex only** by the number p>=1 of
negative letters. Binary C products and C-actions preserve p, whereas
negative-negative products lower it by one. This is a valid finite
filtration in each total chain degree and in each fixed weight.

For an ordered list d_1,...,d_p, its associated multibar homology is

\[
R_z\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}M_{d_1}\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}\cdots
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}M_{d_p}\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}L_y,
\tag{2.2}
\]

with homological shift sum_i(d_i+1), and weight sum_i d_i. This is the
existing multibar computation of the C blocks between the negative
letters. It is a homological statement, not a support-path count.

Take a length-at-most-D projective C-bimodule resolution of each M_d.
The tensor of elementary terms has the form

\[
(Ce_i\otimes e_jC)\otimes_C(Ce_k\otimes e_lC)
\cong Ce_i\otimes(e_jCe_k)\otimes e_lC.
\tag{2.3}
\]

It is again C^e-projective. The bounded tensor complex represents the
derived tensor word and has length <=pD. Tensoring its terms first with
R_z yields right C-projectives; tensoring finally with L_y computes
(2.2). Its unshifted homology lies in degrees 0,...,pD.

All p>=ell associated terms are zero by (1.1). Every remaining term has

\[
\sum_i d_i\le ap\le W,\qquad
\sum_i(d_i+1)+pD\le p(a+1+D)\le U.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Finite, degreewise convergence of this auxiliary filtration proves both
bounds for the actual b_2 homology of N, including its negative-negative
products. QED.

The auxiliary letter-count filtration is only a computation of b_2.
It is not claimed to filter all higher operations: those can change the
number of negative letters differently. Return now to negative weight.

### Lemma 2.2 — Higher operations cannot restore unbounded kernel homology

For the full actual A_infinity bar differential,

\[
H_n(N)=0\quad\hbox{for every }n>U.
\tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

The first page of N's negative-weight spectral sequence is the binary
homology from Lemma 2.1. All its groups of total degree >U vanish.
Subsequent pages are subquotients, so the same holds on every page.

In total chain degree n there are finitely many bar letters, finitely
many weights, and finitely many basis words. The filtration is finite
degreewise, hence converges to the homology of N without a completion
or inverse-limit assumption. The associated graded of H_n(N) is zero
when n>U, proving (2.5). QED.

No parity, formality, operator finite generation or regular injective
dimension enters this argument.

## 3. Ordinary Tor/Ext bridge and a uniform screening bound

### Theorem 3.1 — Eventual ordinary Tor isomorphism

For every retained pair,

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_n:\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y)
\ \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\
\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(R_z,L_y)
\quad\hbox{for }n>U+1.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The ordinary comparison is also injective for every n>U.

#### Proof

The exact sequence (2.1) gives

\[
H_n(N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^H_n(R_z,L_y)
\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_n}\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(R_z,L_y)
\longrightarrow H_{n-1}(N).
\tag{3.2}
\]

For n>U+1 both outer terms vanish by Lemma 2.2, giving the isomorphism.
For n>U the first outer term vanishes, giving injectivity.
Use the already established ordinary A/H comparison. The projection is
the natural comparison induced by the actual A -> C quotient. QED.

Dualising finite-dimensional simple-pair complexes identifies
D Tor^A_n(R_z,L_y) with Ext^n_A(L_y,L_z). Thus the natural inflation

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^n_C(L_y,L_z)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(L_y,L_z)
\tag{3.3}
\]

is an isomorphism in the same degrees. These are ordinary groups on both
sides. No singular, stable or Tate comparison is being used.

### Theorem 3.2 — Uniform negative-weight screening-page bound

Every quotient-edge class which dies in the existing negative-weight
spectral sequence dies by a page r<=W, in the convention that m_3 raises
weight by one.

#### Proof

Lemma 2.1 makes every positive-weight first-page column above W zero.
All later pages retain those zero columns. A page-r differential from
weight zero has target weight r, so it is zero for r>W. QED.

This concerns negative weight, not the local-singular excursion filtration.
No excursion bound is inferred.

## 4. Actual 24-dimensional nonformal calibration

\[

## 7. Normal adversarial audit

   Radical nilpotence, zero binary negative products, finite algebra

   not substitute for it. Section 5 supplies an actual graph counterexample.

   remainder is not closed by this theorem.

   C^e-projective resolutions. Separate finite pd is not substituted.
4. **Binary products and filtration:** negative-negative products
   can lower the auxiliary count. This auxiliary filtration is used
   only for b_2; the full comparison uses the existing negative
   weight. No unsupported count filtration of mixed operations is used.

   weight in each degree. No completed bar construction, inverse
   limit or uniform finite global dimension of C is assumed.
6. **Index and sharpness:** N vanishes above U, so isomorphism needs
   n>U+1. The actual A24 failure at U+1=5 prevents dropping the extra
   one or changing the strict inequality.
7. **Ordinary and supported:** localisation supplies ordinary A-Tor;
   (3.2) compares it to ordinary C-Tor. A fixed reverse-supported
   C pair keeps the same support in A. No naked cycle, stable group
   or opposite transfer appears in this bridge.
8. **Section and descent:** the theorem does not construct an algebra
   section, assert formality or prove inheritance under another
   deletion. A24 has a nonzero section obstruction. The stated
   global exclusion uses vertex minimality, not an unproved new
   class-wide descent.
9. **Calibration scope:** A24 is infinite + and its screening is
   bounded; A9_four has finite gldim and finite-pd source. Neither
   is a universal counterexample or an unbounded-excursion model.
10. **Prior assets and verification:** the multibar/corridor input is
    reused, not presented as a new universal Noetherian theorem.
    All conclusions here remain self-audited.
