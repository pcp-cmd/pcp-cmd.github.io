# §3 Dangerous heuristic lines and strategies not to pursue

**Dangerous heuristic lines**

- “Persistent cross-Tor plus arrows both ways gives a Peirce return.” **Status: Mathematically incorrect as stated.** `6bd89784ef94bef3` explicitly refutes the implication. The replacement must use comparison provenance and relation-level data.
- “Finite-length devissage solves visibility.” **Status: Conflated with a stronger claim.** The verified simple-factor extraction preserves Tor recurrence, not the ordered Peirce block required for infinite-(+).
- “Two vertices and three vertices are both now closed.” **Status: Mathematically incorrect as stated.** `ee07f374726a4e16` closes the strongly connected two-vertex case, but the mutual-dumbbell three-vertex case remains open.
- “The hyper-Tor spectral sequence automatically has finite width.” **Status: Not automatic.** `54ae7bd3cfdc8bb1` assumes an explicit upper bound on nonzero higher comparison degrees.
- “Minimal-resolution support edges can be multiplied into a nonzero return.” **Status: Method shortcut, not a proof step.** `39de5538689bf91c` supplies local radical edges, while relations can still annihilate their composite.

**Strategies not to pursue**

- Do not attempt a generic cross-Tor-to-product theorem: `6bd89784ef94bef3` rules it out.
- Do not cite a blanket three-vertex closure from the existing Armenta audit: the mutual dumbbell survives.
- Do not discard higher comparison rows: they are typed secondary defects, not negligible errors.
- Do not use support graphs without radical-filtration or differential-matrix control.
- Do not erase provenance when devissing to simples: the quotient/kernel/higher-row location is the only extra structure available for recycling.
- Do not stop at the five-vertex case: the requested result is a universal recurrence–visibility mechanism.
