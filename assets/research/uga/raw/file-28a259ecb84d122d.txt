## 2. C120 — An ordinary Euler no-cancellation stopping theorem

Assume there is an integer d0>=0 such that, for EVERY ordered pair x,y
and EVERY internal degree d>d0, all n with
\(\beta^G_{xy}(n,d)>0\) have the SAME parity. The allowed parity can
depend on x,y,d. There may be several n of that parity and several
internal weights in one homological degree.

Then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gd}G<\infty\ \Longleftrightarrow\ D(t)=1,}
\tag{2}
\]
and, if D=1,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gd}G\le\max\{d0,(N-1)w\}.}
\tag{3}
\]
If gd G is infinite, put u=deg D>=1. For EVERY b>=0 there are some
x,y and a degree n with
\[
\boxed{b<n\le(b+1)w+u-1,\quad
e_yGe_x\ne0,\quad \operatorname{Tor}^G_n(R_x,L_y)\ne0.}
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently ONE fixed supported ordered pair persists infinitely often.

### Proof

If gd G is finite, Q in (1) is a polynomial matrix. Taking determinants
in C Q=I gives a polynomial inverse for D in Z[t]. Thus D is a unit;
D(0)=1 forces D=1. This direction requires no parity assumption.

Conversely, if D=1, the adjugate identity says Q=adj C. Every entry
has degree at most (N-1)w. For d>max{d0,(N-1)w}, its coefficient is
therefore zero. But (1) and the hypothesis make that coefficient either
a sum of nonnegative integer Betti numbers or the negative of such
a sum. The sum is finite because n<=d. All its summands are zero.
No nonzero graded Betti number can then have such an internal degree.
Since n<=d, every Tor degree n>max{d0,(N-1)w} vanishes. The actual
minimal S resolution terminates, proving (3) and (2).

For infinite gd, (2) forces D!=1. Apply the already proved C117 ordinary
window (4) with the exact same orientation. No invented invariant or
singular-to-ordinary substitution is used. QED.

This is a stopping theorem with an explicit polynomial degree bound.
Finite state counts, regular-target Ext, and module rectangles do not
enter it. It does NOT assert the parity hypothesis automatically.

## 5. A literal nongradable filtered Frobenius calibration

For ANY field define A16 with basis the 16 squarefree monomials x_I,
I subset{1,2,3,4}, including 1. Products of disjoint subsets are their
union. The ONLY additional nonzero overlapping products are
\[
x_i^2=\prod_{j\ne i}x_j=:m_i.
\tag{10}
\]
Every other overlapping product is zero. In particular x_i^3=x_1234
and every product of five radical factors is zero.

This is an actual associative commutative algebra. For radical triples,
three pairwise disjoint factors give the union on both sides. An overlap
can be nonzero only through the repeated singleton in (10); its product
with a third factor is nonzero only when that factor is again x_i,
giving x_1234 on both sides. All remaining overlaps give zero on both
sides. Identities supply the other associativity cases.

Its radical powers are spans of squarefree monomials of sizes at least
p (p=1..4), of dimensions15,11,5,1; J5=0. Thus
\[
\operatorname{gr}_J A16=k[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]/(x_1^2,x_2^2,x_3^2,x_4^2).
\tag{11}
\]
The tensor of the four actual dual-number minimal resolutions, with
generator degree n in each factor, is exact over k and minimal over
the tensor algebra. It proves ALL n
\[
W_n=\{n\},\quad
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^{\operatorname{gr}A16}_n(k,k)=\binom{n+3}{3},
\quad D_G(t)=(1+t)^4.
\]
Theorem (6) therefore gives these ordinary Betti dimensions also for
A16; a finite prefix is not their proof. Extracting the top monomial
coefficient gives the complement-permutation nondegenerate pairing,
so A16 is Frobenius over every field.
