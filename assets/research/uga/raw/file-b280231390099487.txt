## 1. Hypotheses, actual projectives and exact restriction

Let A be finite-dimensional elementary over any field. Let F be a
nonempty proper vertex set, f=sum_{u in F}e_u, e=1-f and B=eAe.
Assume

\[
d=\max_{u\in F}\operatorname{pd}_A L_u<\infty,
\qquad t=\operatorname{pd}_{B^{op}}(Ae)<\infty.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Here Ae is the ACTUAL (A,B)-bimodule, with RIGHT B pd in (1.1).
B is the retained CORNER, not A/AfA. No algebra homomorphism A->B
or quotient comparison is asserted. Retained physical Peirce spaces
are literally the same:

\[
e_yBe_z=e_yAe_z\quad(y,z\notin F).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Put

\[
N=d+t+1.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The exact functor E(M)=eM on LEFT modules has left derived adjoint
G(-)=Ae tensor^L_B(-). Its right derived adjoint expression is
RHom_A(Ae,-)=e(-), since Ae is LEFT A projective. These identities
will be realised by actual ordinary projective complexes, not invoked
as a singular equivalence.

## 2. Bounded ordinary defect and uniform high Ext isomorphism

### Theorem 2.1 — Ordinary high comparison for ALL finite modules (C093)

For every finite LEFT A modules M,Z the actual exact restriction map
on ordinary Yoneda Ext is an isomorphism for n>N:

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(M,Z)
\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\operatorname{Ext}^n_B(eM,eZ).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For every finite RIGHT A module R and finite LEFT A module M, its
finite-dimensional dual gives a natural ordinary isomorphism

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^B_n(Re,eM)
\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R,M)\quad(n>N).
\tag{2.2}
\]

The direction in (2.2) is B->A, dual to Ext restriction. It is NOT
an A->B quotient change-of-rings map.

#### Proof from actual ordinary projective complexes

Take the actual minimal B-projective resolution Q^bullet of eM,
in nonpositive cohomological degrees. Form

\[
T^\bullet=Ae\otimes_B Q^\bullet.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Every term is a sum of Ae_j=Ae tensor_B Be_j, j retained, hence
an actual LEFT A projective. It need not be minimal or exact;
we do NOT call it a projective resolution of M. Its homology is

\[
H^{-i}(T^\bullet)=\operatorname{Tor}^B_i(Ae,eM),
\quad H^{-i}(T^\bullet)=0\ (i>t).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Let P^bullet be the actual minimal A-projective resolution of M.
Projectivity of Q gives a comparison Q->eP resolving the identity
on eM. Adjunction gives a chain map T->P representing the canonical
counit epsilon:G(eM)->M. Let C_M be its cone. Applying e gives a
quasi-isomorphism Q->eP, so EVERY cohomology module of C_M is
annihilated by e and has only F LEFT simple factors. Also

\[
H^j(C_M)=0\quad(j<-t-1\text{ or }j>0).
\tag{2.5}
\]

Finite-length ordinary devissage from (1.1) gives pd_A H^j(C_M)<=d
for each of the finitely many nonzero cohomology modules. This is a
bounded GLOBAL defect. Resolving those modules and using the finite
truncation triangles gives a bounded LEFT A projective representative
of C_M in degrees [-N,0]. Equivalently, direct ordinary Ext devissage
in those triangles gives

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(A)}(C_M,Z[n])=0\quad(n>N)
\tag{2.6}
\]

for EVERY finite Z. In fact a cohomology module at degree -s contributes
ordinary Ext^{n-s}_A(H^{-s}(C_M),Z), zero when n>d+s, and s<=t+1.
This proves (2.6) with the displayed N, not an unspecified cutoff.

Apply ordinary RHom_A(-,Z) to T->M->C_M. The two adjoining defect
terms vanish in degrees n and n+1 when n>N. Thus

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(M,Z)
\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D(A)}(T,Z[n])
\cong\operatorname{Ext}^n_B(eM,eZ).
\tag{2.7}
\]

The last identification is the literal chain adjunction
Hom_A(Ae tensor_B Q,Z)=Hom_B(Q,eZ). Under it, composition with
epsilon is exactly the restriction map E on ordinary Ext: e epsilon
resolves the identity. So (2.1) identifies the natural map, not merely
group dimensions. All derived notation here is an ordinary-projective
calculation with a bounded perfect error; no singular/Tate/stable
comparison is used.

For finite R put Z=D R, where D=Hom_k(-,k). The natural identity
e(DR)=D(Re) and ordinary tensor–Hom duality give
D Tor^A_n(R,M)=Ext^n_A(M,DR), and the analogous identity over B.
Dualising (2.1) proves (2.2). QED.

### Corollary 2.2 — Deleted RIGHT simples are forced finite-pd

Take Z=L_u for u in F. Then eZ=0, so Ext^n_A(M,L_u)=0 for EVERY
finite M and n>N. By ordinary finite-dimensional duality,

\[
\operatorname{pd}_{A^{op}}R_u\le N\quad(u\in F).
\tag{2.8}
\]

Thus (1.1) deletes only vertices finite-pd on BOTH sides. It cannot
lower the number of infinite RIGHT labels. In particular, if a
LEFT-finite vertex has infinite RIGHT pd, then for the complementary
corner the module Ae cannot have finite RIGHT B pd. This conclusion
follows from an ordinary all-module comparison, not side transfer.

## 3. Exact infinite labels, supported return and strict dimension descent

### Corollary 3.1 — Retained ordinary labels and finite pd bounds

For every retained LEFT label y, the A and B simple pds are either
both infinite or both finite. If finite a=pd_A L_y, b=pd_B L_y, then

\[
a\le\max\{b,N\},\qquad b\le\max\{a,N\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed high Ext to all A simple targets is (2.1); deleted targets have
zero e-image. Conversely all B simple targets occur as eL_z for
retained z. Testing those actual simple targets proves both bounds.

The same finite/infinite equivalence and the same inequalities hold
for every retained RIGHT R_z by (2.2), testing all LEFT simples and
using that deleted sources have e-image zero. Deleted labels are
finite on both sides by (1.1) and (2.8). Hence, as labelled sets,

\[
I_L(B)=I_L(A),\qquad I_R(B)=I_R(A).
\tag{3.2}
\]

These sets lie entirely in the retained vertices. No opposite-+
theorem or inference from missing tests enters. Finite pds strictly
above N are in fact equal by (3.1). Small finite pds need not agree.

### Corollary 3.2 — Global infinity equivalence, uniform bound and badness descent

The exact functor e(-) is essentially surjective on finite B modules:
for X use the actual finite A module Ae tensor_B X, whose restriction
is B tensor_B X=X. Therefore (2.1) implies, whenever finite,

\[
\operatorname{gd}A\le\max\{\operatorname{gd}B,N\},\qquad
\operatorname{gd}B\le\max\{\operatorname{gd}A,N\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

In particular gd A is infinite iff gd B is infinite; this also follows
from (3.2). Any ordinary B supported persistent simple pair has the
SAME supported A tail by (1.2) and the high Tor isomorphism (2.2).
A bad A therefore gives a bad B. The nonempty deleted identity
sum f ensures

\[
\dim_k B<\dim_k A.
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is a legitimate well-founded global descent at ANY source count.
The left/right infinite counts are preserved, not decreased. C088's
opposite-count drop is a different mechanism with different hypotheses.
One may remove a finite vertex by this corner move even when its
projective cover contains infinite factors and no C088 block exists.

Consequently a vertex- or dimension-minimal bad algebra has NO proper
finite-LEFT complement satisfying (1.1). This excludes the entire
perfect-corner branch of that global alternative, rather than merely
making a hypothetical counterexample more elaborate.

### Corollary 3.3 — Checked towers close and comparison bounds compose

For a sequence of such actual checked corners, the ordinary Ext
restriction maps compose and the dual Tor maps compose in the
reverse direction. All are isomorphisms for n>max_i N_i; every retained
physical pair is unchanged. No perfection inheritance is asserted:
each actual corner module and finite complement must be checked.

Each nonempty deletion lowers dimension and vertex number, so at most
v-1 moves are possible from v vertices. If the terminal algebra has a
proved ordinary return (for example C080 or C090), it lifts throughout
the tower. If it has finite gd b, then

\[
\operatorname{gd}A\le\max\{b,N_1,\ldots,N_r\}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

This supplies a uniform proved bound for the checked tower class.
It does not claim automatic existence of such a tower in every A.
