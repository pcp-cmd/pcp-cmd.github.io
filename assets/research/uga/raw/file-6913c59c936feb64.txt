# Run — A finite obstruction cutoff forces ordinary Tor survival

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Researcher:** one main Codex researcher  
**Evidence:** complete proofs and normal adversarial self-audit; not independently verified  
**Global status:** Universal Gap A remains OPEN. No novelty claim is made.

This continuation starts from T5, including its odd-negative,
projective-bimodule even-Tor bridge. The present mechanism removes both
of those restrictions under a stated coefficient-dimension hypothesis.
It constructs the actual augmented A_infinity section required by C060
and therefore returns to ordinary Tor in every degree.

The principal application is: if the ordinary quotient has left and
right regular injective dimension at most one, and every negative
cohomology layer has finite C^e-projective dimension, then every
ordinary quotient Tor class lifts as a split summand. Mixed higher
operations, both negative parities and unbounded negative degrees are
allowed. This excludes a whole perfect-coefficient deletion class in a
global vertex-minimal counterexample. It does not show that an arbitrary
quotient is one-Gorenstein or that arbitrary coefficients are perfect.

## 1. Fixed notation and existing assets

Let q be the retained connective derived quotient at x, C=H^0(q), and
S the retained separable vertex algebra. Work with its strictly unital,
augmented, S-relative minimal model

\[
H=C\oplus\bigoplus_{d\ge1}M_d[d],\qquad M_d=H^{-d}(q).
\]

All degrees are cohomological: |m_r|=2-r and a morphism component s_r
has degree 1-r. Each M_d is finite-dimensional for an actual
finite-dimensional deletion; no uniform dimension or pd bound is
needed. Write R=rad C. Normalized S-relative Hochschild cochains

\[
\mathcal C^n(C,M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{S^e}(R^{\otimes_S n},M)
\]

compute HH^n(C,M)=Ext^n_{C^e}(C,M). The relative bar resolution has
terms C tensor_S R^{tensor n} tensor_S C; these are C^e-projective
because S is separable. The usual normalized differential is

\[
(\delta a)(c_1,\ldots,c_{n+1})=
c_1a(c_2,\ldots,c_{n+1})
\sum_{i=1}^n(-1)^i a(c_1,\ldots,c_ic_{i+1},\ldots,c_{n+1})
(-1)^{n+1}a(c_1,\ldots,c_n)c_{n+1}.
\]

The internal shift of M only changes the common suspension sign, not
whether this cohomology vanishes.

We reuse the strict projection p:H -> C, the derived-localisation
comparison for retained simples, and C060's section/bar retraction.
We do not re-prove them. The morphism and suspended coalgebra
conventions agree with [Keller, Sections 3.4 and 3.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9910179).
The arity recursion needed here is proved below, including its
coefficient and index, rather than inferred from a generic formality
slogan.

## 2. The actual section obstruction

### Lemma 2.1 — The next obstruction lies in HH^n(C,M_{n-2})

Suppose components s_1,...,s_{n-2}, with s_1 the inclusion of C,
satisfy the A_infinity morphism equations through arity n-1, where
n>=3. They can be extended through arity n if and only if a
particular cocycle

\[
\mathfrak o_n\in\mathcal C^n(C,M_{n-2})
\]

is a coboundary. The correcting component is

\[
s_{n-1}:R^{\otimes_S(n-1)}\longrightarrow M_{n-2},
\]

of internal degree 2-n. This is the obstruction of the morphism,
including all already chosen corrections; it need not be the raw
pure-input m_n.

#### Proof

Use the suspended reduced tensor coalgebras B(C), B(H), with
differentials b_C,b_H of degree one. The chosen components determine
a coalgebra map F through the required lengths. Temporarily set the
next and later components to zero and define its defect

\[
\mathcal D=b_HF-Fb_C.
\]

It is an F-coderivation, because both b's are coderivations and F is
a coalgebra map. Its corestrictions below length n vanish by the
assumed equations. Its first possible corestriction, at length n,
unsuspends to a normalized n-cochain of degree 2-n. Connectivity
therefore places its value in M_{n-2}.

The elementary identity

\[
b_H\mathcal D+\mathcal D b_C=0
\tag{2.1}
\]

follows by expansion and b_H^2=b_C^2=0. Its corestriction at length
n+1 is the Hochschild equation delta(o_n)=0. Here minimality kills
all unary terms. The only remaining ways to use the first defect
are binary left/right action by s_1(c), or a binary C contraction
inside its inputs. Higher target operations or a component s_r
with r>=2 would need at least n+2 inputs and cannot contribute.
This accounts for every term and yields exactly the displayed
normalized Hochschild differential, up to the harmless common
suspension sign.

Changing F's component at length n-1 by a cochain a adds delta(a)
to its defect at length n, with the same suspension convention.
It does not change any smaller equation: a unary term would be
needed at length n-1, and both minimal models have b_1=0.
Thus solve delta(a)=-o_n and take it for s_{n-1}. Conversely that
equation is forced by an extension through arity n. QED.

This proof permits nonzero negative-negative binary products and
all mixed higher operations. They enter o_n through the already
chosen components. They do not alter the differential on its
first unresolved coefficient.

### Theorem 2.2 — Vanishing coefficients force a full augmented section

If

\[
\boxed{HH^{d+2}(C,M_d)=0\quad\text{for every }d\ge1,}
\tag{2.2}
\]

there is a strictly unital, augmented S-relative A_infinity section
s:C -> H with s_1=id_C and p s=id_C.

#### Proof

The arity-two equation holds since m_2 on C is its ordinary
product. Apply Lemma 2.1 for n=3,4,... . At each step (2.2) solves
the only next equation, and future components preserve every
earlier equation. Choose normalized S-relative correcting
cochains, so units and primitive labels are preserved.

There is no infinite-sum convergence assumption. On a bar word of
length l, the coalgebra map uses finitely many ordered partitions
of that word, and only components s_1,...,s_l occur. Each has the
correct total degree. Thus the recursive components define an
actual map of direct-sum conilpotent bar coalgebras.

Every s_r with r>=2 has negative output, so p kills it. The linear
component is the identity on C. Consequently p s=id as an
A_infinity morphism, not merely on degree-zero cohomology. QED.

### Corollary 2.3 — Ordinary supported Tor retracts in every degree

Under (2.2), for every retained pair and every n>=0,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(R_z,L_y)
\text{ is a split summand of }\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y).}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Proof: apply C060 to the section constructed in Theorem 2.2.
The bar maps satisfy B(p)B(s)=id, and the retained derived quotient
computes ordinary A-Tor. Reverse support in C lifts to A
separately through the ordinary quotient. QED.

This is a constructed section, stronger than T5's even-degree
surjectivity. It does not make H formal: mixed operations and
negative multiplication can remain.

## 3. A dimension bound that actually terminates the obstruction tower

### Lemma 3.1 — Finite pd does not enlarge a coefficient's injective bound

For a finite-dimensional ring E with id_E E<=D, every finite E-module
M of finite projective dimension satisfies id_E M<=D.

#### Proof

Take a finite projective resolution of M. Each projective term
has injective dimension at most D. For
0 -> K -> P -> X -> 0 and i>D the long exact sequence gives

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^i_E(Z,X)
\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i+1}_E(Z,K).
\]

Iterate along that finite resolution, ending at a projective
module whose corresponding Ext vanishes. This holds for every
Z, proving the claimed injective bound. QED.

The finiteness of the resolution and the regular injective bound
are both used. Finite pd alone over an arbitrary C^e does not
supply any such uniform bound.

### Theorem 3.2 — Uniform finite arity test for the section

Assume id_{C^e} C^e<=D<infinity and pd_{C^e} M_d<infinity
for each d>=1. A full section C -> H exists if and only if the
morphism equations admit a compatible solution through arity D.
For D<=2 this condition is automatic.

#### Proof

Lemma 3.1 gives id_{C^e} M_d<=D independently of its pd.
Therefore

\[
HH^n(C,M_{n-2})=0\qquad(n>D).
\]

Once the finitely many equations 3<=n<=D are solved, Lemma 2.1
extends that same solution at every later arity. Necessity follows
by truncating an actual section. For D<=2 there is no equation in
this finite range. QED.

This is a uniform bound on **section obstruction arity**, under
the stated regular and coefficient hypotheses. It is not an
operation-arity bound, excursion-depth bound, screening-page
bound, or finite-degree Tor certificate. Already chosen lower
components must solve the equations together; testing individual
raw m_n values is insufficient.

If M_1,...,M_{D-2} are zero (an empty condition for D<=2), the
low-arity equations hold with all these components zero. Thus
the full section is automatic. This supplies a closure mechanism
for a stated deep-negative perfect-coefficient class as well.

## 4. Removing both parity and projectivity in one-Gorenstein quotients

### Lemma 4.1 — The enveloping regular bound

If id_C C<=g_l and id_{C^op} C<=g_r, then

\[
\operatorname{id}_{C^e}C^e\le g_l+g_r.
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Tensor over k finite injective resolutions of the two regular
modules. The total complex resolves C tensor_k C^op and has
length at most g_l+g_r. The tensor of two finite injectives is
injective over the tensor algebra: its vector-space dual is the
tensor of two projectives and hence projective. This proves
(4.1). QED.

This elementary ordinary-dimension calculation is also
[Bergh--Jorgensen, Lemma 2.1](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/30556).
Only that lemma is used from the paper. No Tate groups or
singularity-category comparison enters this proof.

### Theorem 4.2 — One-Gorenstein perfect coefficients give full Tor survival

Assume

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{id}_C C\le1,\quad
\operatorname{id}_{C^{op}}C\le1,\quad
\operatorname{pd}_{C^e}H^{-d}(q)<\infty\ \ (d\ge1).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then the section of Theorem 2.2 exists and the all-degree ordinary
Tor retraction (2.3) holds. In particular C infinite + implies
A infinite +.

#### Proof

Lemma 4.1 gives D<=2. Lemma 3.1 gives id_{C^e} M_d<=2 for
every d, so HH^{d+2}(C,M_d)=0 for all d>=1. Apply Theorem 2.2
and Corollary 2.3. QED.

The negative homology can have both parities and unbounded degree.
Its individual finite projective dimensions need not be given by
a uniform bound. The higher operations and multiplication among
negative classes need not vanish.

### Corollary 4.3 — Perfect local-trivial screening is excluded over this quotient

For e_xAe_x=k, the actual retained model is C plus M[1], with the
only possibly nonzero pure higher operation kappa in HH^3(C,M).
If C has both regular injective dimensions at most one and
pd_{C^e} M is finite, kappa is a coboundary. Every ordinary
quotient Tor class retracts to A. Thus an infinite-+ ordinary
quotient cannot be completely screened.

This covers positive finite bimodule pd, not only T5's
C^e-projective coupler. In this two-stage case the normalized
model becomes augmented formal; in the general many-layer
Theorem 4.2 only a section is asserted.

### Corollary 4.4 — Global minimal-counterexample branch exclusion

Let A be a global vertex-minimal Gap-A counterexample, and x an
infinite-pd vertex. Its deletion cannot satisfy (4.2).

Proof: C058 gives gldim C infinite. Global vertex minimality
makes C infinite +. Theorem 4.2 supplies a supported ordinary
tail in A, a contradiction. QED.

This retains all source and support hypotheses and does not
use the quarantined opposite-side transfer. Using right regular
injective dimension in an explicitly stated hypothesis is not
a transfer of an infinite-+ witness.

## 5. Adversarial audit and useful boundary calibrations

1. The obstruction index is n with coefficient H^{2-n}=M_{n-2};
   the correction is s_{n-1}. In the two-stage model this is
   exactly HH^3(C,M_1), agreeing with the existing kappa asset.
2. The obstruction includes mixed higher operations evaluated on
   earlier corrections. Replacing it with raw pure m_n after
   nonzero lower corrections would be incorrect.
3. The cocycle proof uses the actual identity (2.1), not a graph,
   support recurrence or an unsupported formality assertion.
4. A later component affects its first next equation and not the
   previously solved ones. Direct-sum conilpotence handles the
   infinite number of components without completion.
5. Normalized S-relative cochains preserve the augmentation,
   strict units and primitive labels. Their cohomology agrees
   with ordinary Hochschild Ext because the base is separable.
6. The injective estimate is for the coefficient variable of Ext.
   Finite projective dimension of that variable is converted
   only using the stated finite regular injective bound.
7. C^e-perfect means finite projective dimension of each actual
   bimodule homology layer. Separate finite pd of its two sides
   is insufficient. The nine-dimensional C063 calibration has
   self-injective C but a coupler of infinite C^e-pd, so it
   does not satisfy (4.2) and creates no contradiction.
8. Theorem 4.2 does not assume a parity pattern and is not a
   consequence of T5's positive-weight parity degeneration.
9. The section need not formalize the whole many-layer algebra.
   Only its ordinary degree-zero input morphism equations have
   been solved.
10. The finite arity test bounds possible section obstructions;
    it does not bound the first nonzero screening page or every
    higher-operation arity. Individual low equations must be
    compatible.
11. The global exclusion uses global vertex minimality and
    quotient infinity from the actual anchor. No unconditional
    class theorem for all algebras satisfying one deletion's
    hypotheses is asserted unless the quotient is already
    infinite +.
12. Derived sections are not claimed to inherit under further
    ordinary vertex deletion. T4 proved that inheritance only
    for ordinary sections.
13. This is an all-degree symbolic proof. No new bounded
    computation was needed or represented as independent
    evidence; unchanged computations were not rerun.
14. Neither external source verifies the project conclusion.
    Both are conventional inputs checked at their exact scope.
    No independent verification or novelty is claimed.

## 6. Remaining closure work

This checkpoint supplies an actual all-degree ordinary Tor bridge
from a computable homological condition and a uniform finite
obstruction cutoff. It excludes perfect-coefficient local-trivial
screening over a one-Gorenstein quotient and a many-layer
perfect-coefficient class without parity restrictions.

An arbitrary vertex-minimal counterexample's quotient is not
known to have those injective bounds, and its negative homology
need not be C^e-perfect. Therefore this does not close general
Target B, the nonperfect local-trivial branch, Target A, or all
supported-weight screening.

If the regular enveloping injective bound is larger, only the
finitely many compatible low equations remain in the section
test; solving or forcing them remains a mathematical task.
If there is no finite regular bound or a negative layer is
nonperfect, this argument supplies no uniform cutoff. In those
cases the ordinary returns after complete quotient-edge
screening, and a genuine well-founded reduction, remain the
high-value targets. Further invariant refinement alone has
not acquired priority.
