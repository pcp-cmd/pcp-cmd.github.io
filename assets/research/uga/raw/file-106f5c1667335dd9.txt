## 2.4 Primitive-cohomology obstruction theorem

Let

\[
\mathscr H=H^*(\mathscr E)
\]

be the localized dual Rees cohomology category.

Let

\[
\mathscr I
=
\bigoplus_{n>0}\mathscr H^n
\]

be its positive-degree ideal, and define its graded indecomposable quotient

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak Q
=
\mathscr I/\mathscr I^2.
}
\]

Thus, endpointwise,

\[
\mathfrak Q^n(b,a)
=
\frac{
\mathscr H^n(b,a)
}{
\displaystyle
\sum_c
\sum_{\substack{i+j=n\\i,j>0}}
\mathscr H^i(b,c)\,
\mathscr H^j(c,a)
}.
\tag{Q}
\]

### Theorem

Assume the high localized Rees homology is \(V\)-torsion.

If there exists \(N\) such that

\[
\mathfrak Q^n=0
\qquad
(n>N),
\]

then the exact \(t\)-torsion orders in the high Rees-homology tail are uniformly bounded.

### Proof

If \(\mathfrak Q^n=0\) for \(n>N\), every positive-degree cohomology class of degree \(n>N\) is a finite sum of compositions of strictly lower positive degrees.

Induction on degree shows that the full cohomology category \(\mathscr H\) is generated, over \(\mathscr H^0\), by

\[
\bigoplus_{1\le n\le N}\mathscr H^n.
\]

There are finitely many objects, and each \(\mathscr H^n(b,a)\) is a finitely generated \(V\)-module, since it is the cohomology of a finite free \(V\)-module in that degree.

Choose finite homogeneous \(V\)-generating sets for all degrees \(0,\ldots,N\) and all endpoint pairs.

Composition by these finitely many actual cohomology classes gives finitely many homogeneous actual \(V\)-linear operators on

\[
\bigoplus_{a,b,n}\mathscr H^n(b,a).
\]

The identity morphisms supply finitely many seeds.

Thus the dual cohomology tail satisfies the already VERIFIED localized finite-state theorem.

Since the high \(\mathscr H^n\) are \(V\)-torsion, their \(t\)-orders are uniformly bounded.

The universal-coefficient torsion transfer then gives the same uniform bound, up to the fixed one-degree shift, for the original localized Rees homology \(G_h^{ab}\).

\(\square\)

### Minimal-counterexample consequence

The assumed quotient witness plus ambient eventual vanishing forces unbounded screening torsion.

Therefore a surviving minimal counterexample must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak Q^n\neq0
\quad
\text{for infinitely many }n.
}
\tag{P}
\]

This is strictly stronger than saying merely that the cohomology is infinite-dimensional.

It says that arbitrarily high degrees contain genuinely new **composition-indecomposable actual cohomology classes**.
