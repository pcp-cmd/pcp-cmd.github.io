# Run — Ordinary split radical quotients give strict dimension descent

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Researcher:** one main Codex researcher  
**Evidence:** complete elementary proof and normal adversarial self-audit;
not independently verified  
**Global status:** Universal Gap A remains OPEN. No novelty claim is made.

After T8, the attempt to force general derived tensor nilpotence has no
complete proof. Resolution-term cycles cannot supply it, by C068. This
continuation changes the global reduction: an ordinary split radical
quotient can decrease algebra dimension without deleting a vertex or
introducing another derived invariant.

Its two required properties are proved directly: supported ordinary
quotient Tor is a split summand of ambient Tor, and infinite global
dimension passes to the quotient if the actual ambient kernel has finite
projective dimension on either stated side. Every such nonzero reduction
strictly decreases the positive integer dim_k A.

This supplies a terminating all-size class theorem and can be combined
with the existing vertex-deletion bridges. It does not say that every
hypothetical counterexample admits one of these reductions.

## 1. Align an arbitrary ordinary section with the vertex algebra

Let A be elementary finite-dimensional over k, with fixed primitive
idempotents e_1,...,e_v, and K an ideal contained in J(A). Put B=A/K,
write pi:A -> B for the ordinary quotient, and suppose it has a unital
k-algebra section s. Radical containment ensures B has exactly the
same labelled simples and primitive vertex images bar e_i.

The section need not initially satisfy s(bar e_i)=e_i.
Let f_i=s(bar e_i) and

\[
u=\sum_i e_i f_i.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Then pi(u)=1, so u belongs to 1+K and is invertible, since K is
nilpotent. Orthogonality gives

\[
u f_j=e_j f_j=e_j u.
\]

Thus the conjugated section s'(b)=u s(b)u^{-1} satisfies

\[
s'(\bar e_i)=e_i,\qquad \pi s'=\mathrm{id}_B.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Also J(B)=J(A)/K, and s'(J(B)) is contained in J(A): the preimage
of J(B) under pi is J(A). Consequently pi and s' preserve the
ordinary vertex augmentation. No extra vertex-preserving assumption
on the initial section is necessary.

### Lemma 1.1 — Split ordinary Tor with the same Peirce pair

For every labelled right/left simple pair and every n>=0,

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^B_n(R_z,L_y)
\quad\hbox{is a split summand of}\quad
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y).
\tag{1.3}
\]

#### Proof

Use normalized relative bars over S=sum_i k e_i. The maps on radical
letters induced by pi and s' are chain maps, since both preserve S,
augmentation and ordinary products. Their composite on B's bar is
the identity. Endpoint simple modules have the same S actions,
so the retract holds separately for every (z,y). Passing to homology
proves (1.3). QED.

If e_y B e_z is nonzero, (1.2) embeds it into e_y A e_z. A fixed
ordinary supported B tail therefore gives a fixed supported A tail.
This does not require a section of a derived quotient, a local corner,
coefficient perfectness, or an opposite-side infinite-+ transfer.

It also follows that gldim B<=gldim A: if all ordinary A simple-pair
Tor above a finite degree vanishes, (1.3) gives the same for B.

## 2. One-sided ambient finite pd preserves global dimension infinity

### Lemma 2.1 — A finite-pd quotient cannot lose all global infinity

For any K contained in J(A), without a section assumption, suppose

\[
d=\operatorname{pd}_{A^{op}}K<\infty.
\tag{2.1}
\]

If g=gldim B is finite, then

\[
\operatorname{gldim}A\le g+d+1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

The exact right-module sequence 0 -> K -> A -> B -> 0 gives
pd_{A^{op}} B<=d+1. Each B-projective right module is a direct
summand of a direct sum of B, so has ambient right pd<=d+1.

Every simple right A-module is inflated from B, because K is contained
in the radical. Resolve it by B-projectives in length at most g.
After restriction this is still an exact sequence of right A-modules,
all its terms having ambient pd<=d+1. The elementary finite-resolution
inequality gives pd_{A^{op}} R_z<=g+d+1 for every z.

Hence Tor^A_n(R_z,L_y)=0 for all simple pairs and all n>g+d+1.
The top of a nonzero term in a minimal left-simple projective
resolution is detected by some R_z tensor that term. Thus all left
simples also have pd<=g+d+1. For a finite-dimensional algebra this
bounds its global dimension, proving (2.2). QED.

The left-sided version is proved directly: if pd_A K=d is finite,
resolve each left A-simple by B-projectives and use pd_A B<=d+1.
The right version did not assume a theorem transferring infinite +
to the opposite algebra.

### Theorem 2.2 — The ordinary reduction preserves global infinity

Under the split radical quotient of Section 1, finite actual ambient
pd of K on either one stated side gives

\[
\operatorname{gldim}A<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{gldim}B<\infty.
\tag{2.3}
\]

When finite, with d the chosen ambient one-sided pd,

\[
\operatorname{gldim}B\le\operatorname{gldim}A
\le\operatorname{gldim}B+d+1.
\tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Section 1 gives the lower inequality and the forward implication.
Lemma 2.1 gives the upper inequality and the reverse implication.
QED.

No finite pd on the other side, K^2=0, finite C^e-pd or tensor
nilpotence is assumed. The finite pd is that of the actual ideal
over the current ambient A, not merely over B.

### Literature comparison at its exact scope

[Giatagantzidis, Theorem 2.14](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12978v1#S2.SS3)
gives a split radical quotient theorem for finitistic and global
dimension, under finite right ambient kernel pd and an additional
left-pd or square-zero alternative. The elementary proof above only
addresses global dimension, using the common simple modules, and
does not need that alternative. No extension of that paper's
finitistic-dimension theorem is asserted or used here.

## 3. Strict descent, class closure and a quantitative bound

### Theorem 3.1 — A bad algebra passes to a strictly smaller bad quotient

Suppose K is nonzero and satisfies Sections 1--2. If A were a
Gap-A counterexample, B would be one too, and

\[
\boxed{\dim_k B=\dim_k A-\dim_k K<\dim_k A.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

A has infinite global dimension. Theorem 2.2 makes B's global
dimension infinite. If B had a supported ordinary infinite tail,
Lemma 1.1 would lift it to A with the same reverse-supported pair.
Thus B is not infinite +. Formula (3.1) follows from the nonzero
kernel. QED.

This is a well-founded strict descent, not merely a change in a
counterexample's structure.

### Corollary 3.2 — All-size closure under finite-pd split radical extensions

If B satisfies Universal Gap A and A -> B satisfies Sections 1--2,
then A satisfies Universal Gap A. Iterate through any finite tower
of such actual split radical quotients. The final algebra's ordinary
supported tail retracts through the whole tower, and (2.3) preserves
global infinity throughout.

In particular, the entire class obtained by finitely many such
extensions from an erasure-stable algebra of C061 satisfies Gap A
at arbitrary finite size. There is no vertex-number ceiling.
Other already justified Gap-A base classes can be substituted.

A full reduction chain of nonzero kernels cannot be infinite:
dimension strictly decreases at every step and remains at least
the number of simple vertices. Hypotheses must be checked at
each actual step; they are not declared to inherit automatically.

### Corollary 3.3 — Supported vanishing yields a finite gldim bound

Let A=A_0 -> A_1 -> ... -> A_r=B be such a tower, with nonzero
radical kernels K_i and chosen finite ambient one-sided dimensions
d_i. All quotients have v labelled vertices. Suppose B has the
all-vertex ordinary sections of C061, and suppose every
reverse-supported A simple-pair Tor vanishes for every n>=M,
where M>=1. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gldim}A
\le(v-1)M+\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(d_i+1).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Composed ordinary bar retracts give the same supported all-degree
vanishing in B: its reverse support embeds into A's support.
C061 gives gldim B<=(v-1)M. Apply (2.4) backwards at each step.
QED.

The terminal all-vertex section condition, all reverse-supported
pairs, all n>=M, and actual finite ambient kernel pd are retained.
This is a class-specific bound, not a finite-window certificate.

### Combining the global moves

A bad algebra also passes to a smaller bad algebra through the
existing ordinary-section infinite-pd vertex deletion of C061,
or a C067 deletion satisfying its exact finite-corridor hypotheses.
The same positive integer dim_k A strictly decreases for all these
moves. Thus they can be used in one checked reduction chain
without proving that their separate hypotheses inherit.

This statement unifies actual reductions; it does not prove an
available reduction exists at every nonterminal bad algebra.
That existence is the remaining universal closure issue.

## 4. Actual one-sided calibration outside the square-zero/left-pd alternative

First take the four-dimensional algebra E with vertices y,x,
radical basis c:y->y and b:x->y, and all radical products zero.
Its quotient by K_E=k b is the split ordinary algebra
k[c]/(c^2) times k at x.

As a right E-module, K_E is e_x E=k e_x via e_x -> b, so is
projective. As a left E-module it is L_y. The column E e_y has
radical k c isomorphic to L_y, giving Omega L_y=L_y and infinite
left pd. This proves actual one-sided projectivity without
assuming left finite pd.

Let T be the six-dimensional path algebra of the line 0->1->2,
with arrows alpha,beta and gamma=beta alpha. Its radical has
right-module decomposition

\[
J(T)\cong e_0T\oplus e_1T,
\tag{4.1}
\]

given by e_0 -> alpha and e_1 -> beta; the latter also sends
alpha to gamma. Thus J(T) is right-projective, while
J(T)^2=k gamma is nonzero.

Now set

\[
A=E\times T,\qquad K=K_E\times J(T).
\tag{4.2}
\]

A has dimension ten and five vertices; K has dimension four.
The ordinary quotient B has dimension six and is
k[c]/(c^2) times k^4, with its explicit vertex/radical section.
The actual ideal K is right-projective over A, has infinite
left pd, and K^2 is nonzero.

Thus (4.2) satisfies Theorem 2.2 with d=0 while neither the
left-finite-pd nor square-zero alternative holds. The retained
ordinary y bar words c|...|c give a supported Tor class in every
degree in B, and their ordinary section supplies the same tail
in A. This is infinite +, not a counterexample.

All these statements follow from the displayed basis maps over
every field. No bounded experiment or new backend execution is
needed for the elementary finite certificates.

## 5. Normal adversarial audit and remaining research

1. K is contained in the actual radical. Otherwise the quotient
   may lose simples carrying global infinity; the finite-simple
   resolution argument fails. A direct-product projection deleting
   an infinite-gldim factor illustrates that failure.
2. The ordinary section is actual and unital. Equation (1.1)
   aligns its idempotents, so the retract is separately augmented
   at each original simple pair.
3. Support is checked in the ordinary quotient and lifted through
   that section. No support cycle is treated as homology.
4. The finite pd concerns actual K over actual A on the stated
   side. Finite pd of resolution terms or of K over B is insufficient
   for the proof as stated.
5. Only one side is needed for global dimension. The proof compares
   finite bounds through ordinary simple-pair Tor, without an
   infinite-+/opposite transfer. No finitistic theorem is claimed.
6. The lower global-dimension bound uses the ordinary bar retract;
   it is not assumed for arbitrary nonsplit radical quotients.
7. Every nonzero reduction lowers dimension. Reusing a representation
   or deleting a label without the infinity and Tor comparisons is
   not a descent move.
8. Each step in a mixed chain needs its own exact hypotheses.
   No new automatic inheritance of sections, perfectness or tensor
   nilpotence is asserted.
9. The numerical bound uses the terminal all-vertex C061 condition,
   not its weaker infinite-pd-only qualitative condition.
10. This is a substantive class closure, but it does not settle the
    general reduction-existence problem. Target A, local-trivial
    nonprojective/non-nilpotent screening and the unbounded
    higher-output Target-B frontier remain open.

The next high-priority task is a theorem forcing one of these actual
ordinary reductions or an ordinary supported tail in every remaining
irreducible case. Additional descriptions of that irreducible residue
without such a mechanism have low priority.
