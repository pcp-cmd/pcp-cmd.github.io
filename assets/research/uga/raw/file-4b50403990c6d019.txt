\[
Z(x):=\{y:e_xAe_y=0\}.

# 2. First four-vertex rigidity

Assume \(A\) is strongly connected, has four vertices, infinite global dimension, and is not infinite \(+\).

Fix \(x\in I_L\).

Because the quiver is strongly connected, \(x\) has at least one external in-neighbour / externally visible label.

Also

\[
e_xAe_x\neq0,
\]

so \(x\notin Z(x)\).

Therefore, among four vertices,

\[
|Z(x)|\le2.
\]

Now suppose

\[
|Z(x)|=1.
\]

By the one-way law, all sufficiently high projective terms in the minimal resolution of \(S_x\) use only that one projective type.

After deleting a finite prefix, this gives a single recurrent projective type.

C090 then forces an ordinary visible return, contradicting badness.

Hence:

\[
\boxed{|Z(x)|=2}
\]

for every infinite-pd source \(x\).

Moreover, both labels in \(Z(x)\) must occur recurrently infinitely often. Otherwise the tail eventually reduces to one recurrent type and C090 applies again.

Thus for every \(x\in I_L\),

\[
W(x):=Q_0\setminus Z(x)
\]

has exactly two labels:

\[
\boxed{W(x)=\{x,u_x\}},
\]

where \(u_x\) is the unique external visible / in-neighbour label, subject to final Peirce-orientation audit.

This is the main four-vertex rigidity.

# 3. Case A — all four left simples have infinite projective dimension

Assume

\[
|I_L|=4.
\]

For every vertex \(x\), there is a unique external visible/in-neighbour \(u_x\).

Because the quiver is strongly connected, every vertex must occur as some \(u_x\).

Hence

\[
x\mapsto u_x
\]

is a fixed-point-free permutation of the four vertices.

The only cycle types are:

1. a single 4-cycle;
2. two disjoint 2-cycles.

The second cannot give a strongly connected quiver.

Therefore, after relabelling, we obtain a 4-cycle pattern.

With the intended Peirce convention, write this as

\[
W(i)=\{i,i-1\},
\qquad
Z(i)=\{i+1,i+2\}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
e_iAe_{i+1}=e_iAe_{i+2}=0.
\]

# 4. Local corner at a vertex of the 4-cycle

Consider source \(i-1\).

Its high recurrent tail is forced into the two projective labels in

\[
Z(i-1).
\]

One of them is \(i\).

Suppose

\[
e_iAe_i=k.
\]

Then the endomorphism ring of the indecomposable projective \(P_i\) has zero radical contribution at the primitive corner:

\[
\operatorname{rad}(e_iAe_i)=0.
\]

Minimality therefore forbids a nonzero \(P_i\to P_i\) differential entry.

The dead-zone relation also kills the relevant cross-Hom from the \(P_i\)-summand to the other allowed recurrent projective type.

Hence the outgoing differential on a recurrent \(P_i\)-summand is zero.

But the high tail is exact.

So the whole \(P_i\)-summand would have to lie in the image of the next differential.

Minimality gives

\[
\operatorname{im}d_{n+1}\subseteq\operatorname{rad}P_n,
\]

and an entire indecomposable projective direct summand cannot lie inside its own radical.

Contradiction.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{e_iAe_i\neq k}
\]

for every vertex \(i\).

# 6. Case B — exactly three left simples are infinite-pd

Assume

\[
I_L=\{x,r,s\}
\]

and \(f\) is the unique finite-left-pd vertex.

Fix an infinite source \(x\).

Assume for contradiction that its unique external visible/in-neighbour is \(f\).

Then

\[
Z(x)=\{r,s\}.
\]

Hence the high recurrent tail of \(S_x\) uses only

\[
P_r,\ P_s,
\]

and both occur recurrently.

Define their infinite-factor carriers:

\[
C_r:=\{j\in I_L:e_jAe_r\neq0\},
\]

\[
C_s:=\{j\in I_L:e_jAe_s\neq0\}.
\]

Because \(r,s\in Z(x)\),

\[
x\notin C_r,\qquad x\notin C_s.
\]

Therefore

\[
C_r,C_s\subseteq\{r,s\}.
\]

Also diagonals are nonzero, so

\[
r\in C_r,\qquad s\in C_s.
\]

If

\[
s\in C_r,
\]

then

\[
C_r=\{r,s\},
\]

which is the full recurrent infinite-factor carrier.

C090 then says \(P_r\) is a dominant recurrent projective and forces an ordinary visible return.

Badness excludes this.

Similarly,

\[
r\notin C_s.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{C_r=\{r\},\qquad C_s=\{s\}.}
\]

Thus, with the current Hom/Peirce convention,

\[
\boxed{e_rAe_s=e_sAe_r=0.}
\]

The high minimal tail therefore splits into two independent projective-type blocks:

\[
P_\bullet=P_\bullet^{(r)}\oplus P_\bullet^{(s)}.
\]

Because there are no cross differential entries, exactness splits blockwise.

At least one block is an infinite recurrent exact block.

That block uses a single recurrent projective type.

C090 applies again and forces visible return.

Contradiction.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{u_x\neq f}
\]

for every infinite vertex \(x\).

# 7. Strong connectivity kills the three-infinite case

The finite-left vertex \(f\) lies in the same strongly connected quiver.

Hence there is a path from \(f\) into the infinite-vertex set.

Look at the first edge where the path enters an infinite vertex:

\[
f\to x
\]

for some \(x\in I_L\).

Then \(f\) is an external in-neighbour / visible predecessor of \(x\).

But Section 6 proved that the unique external visible/in-neighbour of an infinite vertex cannot be \(f\).

Contradiction.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{the three-left-infinite four-vertex case is impossible}.}
\]
