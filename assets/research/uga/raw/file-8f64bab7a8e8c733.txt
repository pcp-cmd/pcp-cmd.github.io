## 1. Two quantifier errors in the previous freeze

Theorem 13.1 requires **every edge** of an already established bar cycle to
have nonzero image in the primitive quotient. One primitive edge alone does
not suffice. Section 18, item 2 loses this quantifier.

Theorem 16.1 assumes `(P_infinity)^(-d)=0` for all `1 <= d <= D`. Absence of
primitive edges on selected high-degree necklaces, or even vanishing of the
entire primitive quotient in sufficiently negative degrees, does not imply
that hypothesis for the low degrees. Section 18, item 3 loses this condition.

The valid statements are therefore sufficient criteria and a conditional
generation theorem. They do not establish an exhaustive two-branch reduction
of Target B to degree-zero arrow-rooted Massey trees.

### 1.1 One primitive negative edge can occur on a boundary necklace

Here is a finite connective graded algebra, regarded as a minimal augmented
A-infinity algebra with only `m_2` nonzero. Take vertices 1,2,3 and arrows

\[
a:1\to2,\quad b:2\to3,\quad c:3\to1,
\qquad |a|=|b|=|c|=-1.
\]

Products are in algebraic order: `ba` walks first `a`, then `b`. Impose
`ac=cb=0` and retain `d=ba`. A basis is the three idempotents and `a,b,c,d`.
There are no reduced diagonal classes; the shortest necklaces have length
two, using `d:1->3` and `c:3->1`. We have

\[
D_\infty=kd,\quad P_\infty=k\bar a\oplus k\bar b\oplus k\bar c.
\]

The minimal necklace word `[d|c]` is a cycle, since `dc=b(ac)=0`, and contains
the primitive negative edge `c`. Nevertheless

\[
b_{\rm bar}[b|a|c]=\pm[d|c],
\]

because the other possible contraction is `ac=0`. Hence it is a boundary.
The sign is a unit under either consistent suspension convention, including
characteristic two. This does not refute Theorem 13.1: `d` is decomposable.
It refutes the one-edge paraphrase. No claim that this example itself is an
ordinary finite-dimensional-algebra derived quotient is needed for this
logical test of the A-infinity assertion.

## 2. Explicit idempotent derived quotient with a low-degree primitive seed

This example is an **actual** derived quotient of an ordinary five-dimensional
algebra. It calibrates the high-degree-versus-all-degree error. It is not a
counterexample to Universal Gap A or an example of unbounded excursion depth.

Let `A` have vertices 1,2, arrows `a:1->2`, `b:2->1`, and relation `ba=0`,
again in algebraic product order. Put `t=ab`. Then

\[
A=\operatorname{span}_k\{e_1,e_2,a,b,t\},\qquad
J=\operatorname{span}_k\{a,b,t\},\qquad J^3=0.
\]

The only nonzero radical product is `ab=t`.

### 2.1 Ambient global dimension is exactly two

For left modules `P_i=Ae_i`,

\[
P_1=\langle e_1,a\rangle,\quad
P_2=\langle e_2,b,t\rangle.
\]

`JP_1=ka` is `L_2`. The radical `JP_2=<b,t>` is isomorphic to `P_1` via
`e_1 -> b`, `a -> t`. Thus the minimal resolutions are

\[
0\to P_1\to P_2\to L_2\to0,
\]
\[
0\to P_1\to P_2\to P_1\to L_1\to0.
\]

The second resolution splices the first into the cover of `L_1`.
All differentials have radical image, so `pd L_2=1`, `pd L_1=2`, and
`gldim A=2`. In particular ordinary Tor is zero in every degree above two.

### 2.2 A strict model of the derived quotient

Delete `e=e_2` and set `f=e_1`. Braun–Chuang–Lazarev, Theorem 2.14 and
Proposition 9.6, give the model

\[
B=A\langle h\rangle/(he=eh=h),\qquad |h|=-1,\quad dh=e.
\]

Their paper uses homological grading; the displayed grading is cohomological.
Over a field the flatness/left-properness hypothesis applies. The retained
corner `Q=fBf` is a model for the remaining vertex of the derived quotient.
The blocks with an `e` endpoint are contractible by multiplication by `h`,
so their removal does not remove derived homology.

For `n>=1`,

\[
Q^{-n}=\operatorname{span}_k
\{b h g_1 h\cdots h g_{n-1}h a:g_i\in\{e,t\}\},
\qquad Q^0=kf.
\]

The negative complex is the shifted unnormalized two-sided bar complex of
`k` with itself over `Gamma=eAe=k[t]/(t^2)`. Its map from degree -1 to degree
zero is multiplication `ba=0`. Bar normalization removes tensors containing
an internal `e`, leaving one basis vector

\[
v_n=b h(t h)^{n-1}a
\]

in each degree -n. The normalized differential is zero: `bt=ta=t^2=0`.
Consequently `H^(-n)(Q)=k[v_n]` for every `n>=1`.

Put `u=bha`. It is closed and

\[
u^n=b h(ab)h\cdots(ab)h a=v_n.
\]

The dg algebra map `k<u> -> Q`, with `|u|=-1` and zero differential, therefore
induces an isomorphism in every degree. Equivalently the normalization
projection sends `v_n` to `u^n` and all internally degenerate words to zero;
the connecting product is always `ab=t`, so this projection is multiplicative.
Thus

\[
\boxed{Q\simeq k\langle u\rangle,\qquad |u|=-1.}
\]

With one generator this is the associative polynomial algebra. It is not
being interpreted as a graded-commutative polynomial algebra; the odd
degree does not impose `u^2=0`.

### 2.3 Primitive quotient, tree degrees, and complete high-degree screening

Choose the formal minimal model `H=k<u>`. Here

\[
H^0=k,\quad R=0,\quad
D_\infty=u^2k[u],\quad P_\infty=ku.
\]

So `(P_infinity)^(-d)=0` for every `d>=2`, while negative homology is unbounded.
Every `u^n` is generated by a binary tree on the negative leaf `u`, with
Massey excess zero. There are no nonzero degree-zero radical leaves at all.

Moreover the one-edge necklace `[u]` is non-boundary, but every `[u^n]` for
`n>=2` is a boundary:

\[
b_{\rm bar}[u|u^{n-1}]=\pm[u^n].
\]

In fact the two-cell semifree `H`-module resolution with generator `v` of
degree -2 and `dv=u` resolves `k`. Its augmentation kernel is paired by
`u^jv -> +/-u^(j+1)`. After tensoring with `k` the differential vanishes,
giving

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^Q_n(k,k)=
\begin{cases}k,&n=0,2,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]

This agrees with the ambient minimal resolution. It also shows why one
non-boundary primitive necklace does not establish a persistent Tor tail.

The local `Gamma` classes have explicitly bounded excursion screening. In
ambient bar notation, for every `m>=1` set

\[
\alpha_m=[b|\underbrace{t|\cdots|t}_{m}|a],\qquad
\beta_m=[b|a|b|\underbrace{t|\cdots|t}_{m-1}|a].
\]

All contractions of `beta_m` are zero except its `ab=t` joint, so
`partial beta_m=+/-alpha_m`. The former has one retained internal vertex,
the latter none. Thus every such local class is killed from excursion
filtration one. This is a bounded-screening calibration, not Branch U.

## 3. Replacement lemma: generation by all primitive seeds

Fix a strictly unital, minimal, connective, `S`-augmented A-infinity model
`H`, where `S=k^q`, `C=H^0` is finite dimensional, and `R=rad C`.
Work in this chosen model; no invariance of `P_infinity` under a change of
minimal model is asserted. Define

\[
D_\infty=\sum_{r\ge2}\operatorname{im}m_r,\qquad
P_\infty=\bar H/D_\infty.
\]

For each `d>=0` choose a homogeneous `S`-bimodule lift `V_d` of
`(P_infinity)^(-d)`. In degree zero, `V_0` lifts `R/R^2`.

### Theorem 3.1 — Mixed-primitive generation

Every homogeneous element of `bar H` is a finite linear combination of
evaluations of finite planar rooted A-infinity trees whose leaves lie in
the spaces `V_d`. For an evaluated tree of negative degree -d,

\[
\boxed{d=\sum_{\ell\in\operatorname{Leaves}(T)}d_\ell
       +\sum_{v\in\operatorname{Int}(T)}(r(v)-2).}
\]

Thus negative degree can come from repeated negative primitive leaves,
Massey excess, or both. Vanishing of primitives only above degree `d_0`
restricts the leaf alphabet to `V_0,...,V_(d_0)`; it does not remove its
negative-degree letters.

#### Proof

First `D_infinity` in degree zero is `R^2`, by connectiveness and
`|m_r|=2-r`. Since `R` is nilpotent, `R=V_0+R^2` implies that `R` is generated
by finite products of elements of `V_0`.

Now fix `d>0` and put `M_d=H^(-d)`. The retained degree decomposition says

\[
D_\infty\cap M_d\subseteq RM_d+M_dR+G_d,
\]

where `G_d` is the `C`-bimodule generated by operation values whose negative
inputs have magnitude strictly less than d. Indeed an input of magnitude d
can occur only in `m_2` with the other reduced input in `R`, because
`d=sum d_j+r-2`. Hence

\[
M_d=V_d+RM_d+M_dR+G_d.
\]

Let `W_d` be the `C`-bimodule generated by `V_d+G_d`. On `M_d/W_d`,

\[
\overline M_d=R\overline M_d+\overline M_dR.
\]

If `R^l=0`, the ideal of `C^e` generated by left and right radical action
has power `2l-1` zero: in any product of that many actions, at least l
occur on one side. Iterating the last equality therefore gives
`overline M_d=0`. This is also the Nakayama argument used in the old Run.

Thus `M_d=W_d`. Induction on d replaces every smaller-degree negative input
by its already obtained finite tree expression. The `C`-bimodule actions
are binary products; radical elements expand into products of `V_0` and
semisimple idempotents merely select the Peirce components. Strict unitality
allows unit vertices to be removed. This proves the generation statement
without selecting a single term from a sum of operation images.

Finally each operation contributes degree `2-r(v)`, giving the displayed
degree formula. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — Primitive support generates all support

Draw an edge `i->j` when `e_j V_d e_i` is nonzero for some d. Every nonzero
Peirce class of `bar H` has a nonzero tree term, and the leaves of that term
form a composable path in this primitive support graph with the same
endpoints. Therefore the reduced support of `H` is contained in the path
closure of the primitive support graph. In particular a derived support
necklace forces a cycle in primitive support.

This is only a support statement. It is not a Tor-return or non-boundary
statement, and it does not identify a primitive-support cycle with a
minimal necklace in the full support of H.

### Corollary 3.3 — Acyclic bounded primitive support bounds negative homology

Assume `V_d=0` for `d>d_0`, and that the primitive support graph is acyclic.
If `q>=2`, then

\[
\boxed{H^{-d}=0\quad\text{for }d>(q-1)(d_0+1)-1.}
\]

For `q=1`, acyclicity means there are no primitive edges, and `bar H=0`.
If all negative primitives vanish, the sharper conclusion is that a nonzero
negative class requires `q>=4` and has `d<=q-3`.

#### Proof

In a nonzero evaluated tree, its L ordered primitive leaves trace an actual
directed path. Acyclicity gives `L<=q-1`. If I is the number of internal
vertices, the tree identity `sum_v(r(v)-1)=L-1` gives

\[
\operatorname{mex}(T)=L-1-I\le L-1.
\]

Theorem 3.1 yields `d<=L d_0+L-1`, proving the first bound. If `d_0=0` and
the output is negative, `I>=1`, so `d=mex(T)<=L-2<=q-3`; a positive excess
also requires `L>=3`. QED.

Unbounded negative homology with bounded primitive degrees therefore forces
cyclic primitive support. The five-dimensional example shows that this
cycle can be generated by a low-degree negative seed and binary products.
It cannot be dismissed by an arrow-only argument.

## 4. One-edge primitive detection and Peirce visibility

For a minimal model H as above, every one-letter word `[eta]` is a bar
cycle. Its homological degree is `d+1` when `eta` has degree -d. Project the
length-one component to `P_infinity`. Every boundary projects to zero,
since each length-one boundary component is a sum of operation outputs.

### Theorem 4.1 — Peircewise primitive lower bound

For every retained pair `(z,y)` and `d>=0`,

\[
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^{Q_x}_{d+1}(R_z,L_y)
\ \ge\ \dim e_z(P_\infty)^{-d}e_y.
\]

To see this, choose linearly independent representatives of a basis of the
right side. Any boundary linear combination of their one-letter cycles
would project to a nontrivial relation in that basis. The representatives
therefore give independent homology classes. A choice of representatives
is used; a canonical map from the quotient into Tor is not claimed.

For retained simples the **derived** quotient comparison is

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^{Q_x}_n(R_z,L_y)
\cong\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y).
\]

This is the derived-localisation comparison already recorded in the
Supported-Derived-Weight Run, Section 1. Its external input is BCL:
the quotient is localisation at `1-e_x`, and restriction identifies its
derived module category with the full subcategory of local A-modules
(Corollary 4.29 and Section 9). The retained simples are local because
`e_x` annihilates them. Full faithfulness gives the Ext comparison; duality
of the degreewise finite simple bar complexes gives the Tor comparison.
Alternatively, use `Q_x tensor_A^L Q_x ~= Q_x` and associativity of derived
tensor products. The ordinary quotient `A/Ae_xA` is not substituted here.

Consequently, if A is not infinite + and N is a uniform degree above which
all reverse-supported simple-pair Tor groups vanish, then

\[
\boxed{e_z(P_\infty)^{-d}e_y=0
\quad\text{if }d+1\ge N\text{ and }e_yAe_z\ne0.}
\]

In particular all diagonal primitive tails are bounded. An unbounded
primitive tail can occur only on a fixed Peirce-invisible pair after a
subsequence. This conclusion does not use the quarantined opposite-
invariance step or the finite-head theorem.

## 8. Further continuation: formal quotient excludes unbounded excursion depth

### 8.1 Exact passage/bar identification

Let `e=e_x`, `f=1-e`, `Gamma=eAe=k e + bar Gamma`. Normalize the retained
corner of the strict BCL model by removing negative words containing an
internal `e`. These words form an acyclic dg ideal: they are the usual
two-sided bar degeneracies, and multiplying passages preserves any such
internal degeneracy. The quotient `Q_norm` is therefore a model for the
retained corner of the derived quotient, with

\[
Q_{\rm norm}^0=fAf,\qquad
Q_{\rm norm}^{-n}=fAe\otimes_k
\bar\Gamma^{\otimes_k(n-1)}\otimes_k eAf
\quad(n\ge1).
\]

Its augmentation ideal has degree-zero part `fJf`. Its differential
multiplies across the internal e-vertex joints; for a degree -1 passage its
map to degree zero is ambient multiplication. Its multiplication concatenates
passages by multiplying their touching endpoint factors.

For retained endpoints `(z,y)`, expand each reduced bar letter of `Q_norm`
into its ambient radical passage. A degree-zero letter expands into one
radical factor. A degree -n letter expands into n+1 radical factors with
all n internal vertices x. Conversely every ambient bar word partitions
uniquely at its retained internal vertices. This gives an isomorphism of
graded vector spaces

\[
e_zB(Q_{\rm norm})e_y\cong B^A(z,y),
\qquad \text{bar length}=\epsilon_x+1
\]

in positive total degree; the length-zero vertex terms match as well.

It is a chain isomorphism. To fix signs, let a suspended letter have degree
`|q|-1`, use `b_1(sq)=-s(dq)` and
`b_2(sq|sq')=(-1)^(|q|-1)s(qq')`, with the tensor Koszul signs. Expansion
then gives the ordinary bar signs `(-1)^i` for removing the i-th internal
ambient vertex. The `b_1` terms remove precisely x vertices; `b_2` terms
remove retained vertices. There are no additional contractions. Hence the
excursion filtration is exactly the reduced-bar length filtration shifted
by one under this isomorphism.

### Theorem 8.2 — Formality exclusion for Target B

Assume `Q_x` is formal as an augmented `S` dg algebra on the retained
vertices. Then its excursion spectral sequence collapses at `E^2`.
Every zero-excursion class that dies is hit by `d_1`; in particular unbounded
excursion screening is impossible.

#### Proof

Use the exact filtered identification above. Augmented formality permits
the minimal augmented A-infinity model `H=H^*(Q_x)` to have only `m_2`
nonzero. The bar map of an augmented A-infinity quasi-isomorphism from
this model to `Q_norm` does not increase bar length; its leading component
is the tensor power of its linear quasi-isomorphism. Thus it induces an
isomorphism of the length-filtration spectral sequences from `E^1` onward.
This can equally be applied to a formality zigzag.

In `B(H)` there is no length-preserving differential, and its only
differential lowers length by one. Every `E^2` class has a representative
in one bar length killed by `b_2`, which is already a cycle for the full
differential. Thus all subsequent differentials vanish. Connectiveness
bounds bar length in each total degree, so no completion/convergence issue
occurs. Transfer the collapse back through the filtered isomorphism. QED.

This does not assume that finite-dimensionality bounds higher arity. It
requires actual **augmented formality**, not merely a guessed zero list of
higher operations or an unbounded dg algebra's graded cohomology ring.
Nor is collapse at `E^2` being identified with negative-weight collapse.

The five-dimensional example meets the formality hypothesis and the
explicit chains in Section 2.3 realize its `d_1` screenings in every local
degree. The theorem is independent of the opposite-side transfer gate.
