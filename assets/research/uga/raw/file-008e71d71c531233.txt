## 1. Reused inputs and the actual ordinary filtration

Let A be a finite-dimensional elementary k-algebra, K an actual radical
ideal with K^2=0, and C=A/K. Assume K is left C-projective. All actions
on K are canonical. Write

\[
Ke_j\simeq\bigoplus_i(Ce_i)^{T(i,j)}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Use C072's actual ordinary bar filtration, not a derived-idempotent
cohomology substitute. An S-bimodule linear radical section need not be an
algebra section. The differential preserves kernel count or increases
it by one; K^2=0 prevents its decrease. For ordinary right C-module X
and ordinary left C-module Y let N(X,Y) be the kernel of the ordinary
bar projection B_A(X,Y)->B_C(X,Y).

Set F(X)=X tensor_C K. Actual left projectivity makes F exact and gives

\[
[F(R_z):R_j]=T(z,j).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus actual derived tensor words reduce to ordinary F^p(X), and the
positive-count first page of N is

\[
E^1_{p,n}= \operatorname{Tor}^C_{n-p}(F^p(X),Y),\qquad p\ge1.
\tag{1.3}
\]

This is the existing ordinary bar construction. It includes all nonsplit
multiplication errors. Every fixed total degree has finite filtration,
so the convergence used below is ordinary degreewise convergence.

Assume T is acyclic. Let q be its longest directed path length, with
the graph convention j->i when T(i,j)>0. Then q<=v-1 and C072 gives
K^{tensor_C^L(q+1)}=0 and pd_A C<=q.

For each z let h(z) be the longest path ending at z. If T(z,j)>0,
h(j)<=h(z)-1. Define

\[
P(z)=\{j:(T^p)(z,j)>0\text{ for some }p\ge0\}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The p=0 term includes z. This is an actual F-composition carrier,
not a graph-to-homology assertion.

## 2. A uniform ordinary migration theorem

### Theorem 2.1 — Finite F-height controls all ordinary quotient Tor

Fix a left C-simple L_y and M>=1. If

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_j,L_y)=0
\quad(n\ge M,\ j\in P(z)),
\tag{2.1}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(R_z,L_y)=0
\quad(n\ge M+2h(z)).}
\tag{2.2}
\]

More generally take any finite-dimensional X, its simple-factor carrier
and all F-reachable factors. With h(X) the maximum of their initial
simple heights, the same hypothesis on all those ordinary A simples
implies Tor^C_n(X,L_y)=0 for n>=M+2h(X).

Consequently an infinite ordinary C tail at (R_z,L_y) forces an infinite
ordinary A tail at some fixed (R_j,L_y), j in P(z). The right endpoint
can change; reverse support of the final pair is not asserted.

#### Proof

Prove the module version by induction on h=h(X). Exactness of F shows
that all factors of F^p(X) have height at most h-p and are reachable
from factors of X. F^p(X)=0 for p>h.

If h=0, F(X)=0, every positive-count first-page term is zero and the
actual ordinary projection is an isomorphism in every degree. Finite
composition-series Tor inequalities give the asserted vanishing.

For h>0 the induction hypothesis gives

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^C_m(F^p(X),L_y)=0
\quad(m\ge M+2(h-p)),\quad1\le p\le h.
\tag{2.3}
\]

In (1.3) put m=n-p. Every positive column is therefore zero when
n>=M+2h-1: indeed n-p>=M+2(h-p) for every p>=1. Finite convergence
gives H_n(N(X,L_y))=0 in that range.

The actual bar exact sequence gives

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(X,L_y)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(X,L_y)\longrightarrow H_{n-1}(N(X,L_y)).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Both outside terms are zero for n>=M+2h. This proves (2.2).

For the qualitative statement, if all reachable ordinary A simple tails
were finite, the finite set P(z) would have a common M, contradicting
the infinite C tail. Hence one fixed reachable pair persists. QED.

### Explicit descent interpretation

If the original A pair does not persist, (2.4) forces an infinite
positive-count N tail. Its finitely many counts and (1.3) give an
infinite C tail for some F^p(R_z), p>=1. Finite ordinary composition
factors select R_j with h(j)<=h(z)-p. Repeat if its ordinary A pair
does not persist. Height strictly decreases, so at most h(z) such
migrations occur. The terminal tail is ordinary A-Tor, not stable,
Tate or singular-category information.

This describes a genuine well-founded migration of ordinary tails.
It does not yet preserve the physical reverse Peirce support needed
for Universal Gap A.

### Corollary 2.2 — Global infinity transfers without perfectness

If T is acyclic, then

\[
\operatorname{gldim}A=\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{gldim}C=\infty.
\tag{2.5}
\]

For finite gldim A=a and finite gldim C=c, respectively,

\[
\operatorname{gldim}C\le a+2q,\qquad
\operatorname{gldim}A\le c+q.
\tag{2.6}
\]

#### Proof

If A has finite global dimension a, all ordinary simple-pair Tor
vanishes for n>=a+1. Theorem 2.1 applied to every z,y gives C-Tor
vanishing for n>=a+1+2q, hence the first bound.

For the other bound reuse C072's proved pd_A C<=q. Every A simple
is an inflated C simple. Its finite C-projective resolution of length
at most c has all terms of ambient pd<=q, so its A-pd is at most c+q.
The two bounds prove (2.5). QED.

Neither C^e-perfectness, a section, nor stable projective-type bimodule
summands is needed. A nonzero K strictly decreases algebra dimension.
However (2.5) alone does not make a bad A yield a bad C: C may supply
a supported witness whose visibility is lost in the migration.

### Direct right-projective version

If K is right C-projective, use G(Y)=K tensor_C Y, which is exact.
Write e_iK=sum_j(e_jC)^{B(i,j)}; then [G(L_y):L_i]=B(i,y).
Assume B is acyclic and define the height as the longest G-factor
path beginning at y. Its height strictly drops on positive G steps.

The same actual ordinary filtration has first page
Tor^C_{n-p}(R_z,G^p(Y)). Induction in the left argument gives the
M+2h(Y) bound and a fixed ordinary A pair (R_z,L_i) in the G-reachable
carrier. The actual test-row formula bounds pd_{A^{op}} C by q,
and direct right-simple resolutions give (2.5)--(2.6).
No opposite-+ transfer is used.

## 3. The exact supported gap

When the original C pair is supported, e_yCe_z!=0. Every factor R_j
of F(R_z) has T(z,j)>0, so actual Ke_j contains Ce_z and

\[
e_yKe_j\ne0,\qquad e_yAe_j\ne0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is C074's physical visibility mechanism at the first step.
At later steps a factor of F^p(R_z) gives a projective summand of
K^{tensor_C p}, not a product inside A. Physical K^2=0, and it is
invalid to replace (3.1) by visibility of every T-reachable endpoint.

Theorem 2.1 requires ordinary A vanishing on the entire reachable
carrier. Non-infinite-+ only supplies vanishing on reverse-supported
pairs. It does not supply (2.1) at every reachable endpoint.

The next closure problem is therefore precise: force a supported
ordinary A tail before visibility is lost, or replace the migration
by an actual eligible ordinary reduction that preserves non-infinite-+.
No general solution to that problem is proved here.

## 4. An actual connected nonperfect first-module counterexample family

For every r>=1 put m=4r+3. Start with the oriented line
0->1->...->m-1, denote its arrow ideal by J_line, and impose all
line paths of length four equal to zero. Add a vertex star, a loop
t at star, and c:star->0. Impose t^2=0 and every radical product
involving c equal to zero. Call the resulting connected algebra A_r.

Let K_r be the actual ideal spanned by line paths of lengths two
and three, and C_r=A_r/K_r. C_r is radical-square-zero. A literal
basis gives

\[
\dim A_r=16r+9,\quad \dim C_r=8r+8,\quad \dim K_r=8r+1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Products are concatenation up to length three, idempotent actions,
and zero otherwise. All relations are monomial, so this is an
associative finite algebra over every field.

### Projectivity, actual nilpotence, nonsplitting and nonperfectness

For line source j<=m-3,

\[
K_re_j\simeq C_re_{j+2};
\tag{4.2}
\]

send the top to the length-two path and its radical arrow, when
present, to the length-three path. All other columns are zero.
Thus K_r is actually left C_r-projective. Its matrix has T(j+2,j)=1,
so its actual derived tensor nilpotence index is ell=2r+2 and q=2r+1.

Nevertheless K_r^2=0 as a physical A_r ideal. These two uses of
square zero are different: its tensor square is nonzero.

There is no ordinary section A_r->C_r. After standard vertex
alignment, every adjacent arrow has a unique lift, whose product
with the next arrow is the nonzero length-two path in A_r; C_r
requires it to be zero.

There is no stable projective-type bimodule splitting. The length-two
path starting at 1 is in the Ce_3 column. Right multiplication by
the arrow 0->1 gives the length-three path starting at 0, in the
radical of the Ce_2 column. The type-3 projector acts as identity
on the first whole column and zero on the second whole column,
so it does not commute with the actual right action.

The actual right module e_2K_r is R_0. Over C_r the right projective
e_0C_r has radical kc=R_star. The right projective e_star C_r has
radical kt=R_star, with multiplication by t having equal image
and kernel kt. Thus pd_{C_r^{op}} R_0=infinity, proving K_r
nonperfect over C_r^e. The same right periodic certificate works
over A_r. This is a genuine nonperfect higher-index calibration.

The original source L_star also has infinite A_r-pd:
rad(A_re_star)=L_star direct sum L_0. The t-word ordinary bar gives
Tor^{A_r}_n(R_star,L_star)=k in every degree. Hence this connected
family is already infinite +, not a Gap-A counterexample.

### All-degree ordinary path resolution certificate

The line simples are unaffected by the added incoming source.
For an A_r line simple L_y the nonzero minimal projectives are

\[
P_{2s}=A_re_{y+4s},\qquad P_{2s+1}=A_re_{y+4s+1},
\tag{4.3}
\]

when the index is below m. The maps alternate multiplication by
the relevant length-one and length-three paths. The kernel of a
length-one map is the length-three top at y+4s+4; the kernel of
a length-three map is the whole next radical. Images equal these
literal kernels. At the right endpoint the resolution terminates.

Over C_r, the minimal projectives are C_re_{y+n}; consecutive
length-one maps have radical image and radical kernel. Thus

\[
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^{A_r}_n(R_z,L_y)=
\begin{cases}
1&z-y=4s,\ n=2s,\\
1&z-y=4s+1,\ n=2s+1,\\
0&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\quad
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^{C_r}_n(R_z,L_y)=
\begin{cases}1&n=z-y\ge0,\\0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\tag{4.4}
\]

These assertions hold in all degrees and all characteristics by
the literal resolutions, not by rank extrapolation.

### The first ordinary kernel module does not represent N

Take z=m-1=4r+2 and y=0. The original ordinary A pair is zero
in every degree, while C Tor is k in degree 4r+2 alone. The actual
bar exact sequence therefore gives

\[
H_n(N(R_z,L_0))=
\begin{cases}k&n=4r+1,\\0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Left-radical quotient of K_r kills all length-three paths. Right
radical action on the remaining length-two tops is zero, so
F(R_z)=R_{4r}. Its ordinary A Tor is

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^{A_r}_n(F(R_z),L_0)=
\begin{cases}k&n=2r,\\0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Hence the proposed higher-index identity
H_n(N(R_z,L_y))=Tor^{A}_{n-1}(F(R_z),L_y) is false. Its predicted
nonzero degree is 2r+1; the actual degree is 4r+1, separated by
an arbitrarily large 2r.

This refutes the attempted representation by a single first-kernel
ordinary module, even with actual left projectivity, actual tensor
nilpotence, a connected nonsplit quotient and genuine global infinity.
It does not refute an eventual supported bridge or Theorem 2.1.
The mismatched original pair is reverse-invisible. The family
is monomial and already belongs to existing Gap-A closure assets;
no new monomial closure is claimed.
