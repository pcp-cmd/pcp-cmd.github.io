## 1. Reused inputs and conventions

Let A be elementary finite-dimensional over a field k, with radical J,
left simples L_y and right simples R_z. An infinite-+ witness is a fixed pair

\[
e_yAe_z\ne0,\qquad
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y)\ne0
\quad\text{for infinitely many }n.
\tag{1.1}
\]

This is the reverse Peirce condition, not the other path direction.

For deletion of e=e_x, put f=1-e, C=A/AeA. Let q be the connective
retained derived quotient, augmented over S=sum_{i!=x}ke_i.
Reuse the derived-localisation comparison audited in T1:

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^q_n(R_z,L_y)
\cong\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y)\quad(y,z\ne x).
\tag{1.2}
\]

This does not assert that A -> C is a homological epimorphism.
Its literature input is
[Braun--Chuang--Lazarev, Corollary 4.29 and Section 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.01146).

Also reuse UGA-C058: if the entire minimal resolution of X uses columns
Au for e_xAu=0, the same complex is its minimal C-projective resolution,
and pd_A X=pd_C X.

Consequently, if A is non-infinite-+ and pd_A L_x is infinite, then
gldim C is infinite. High resolution terms of L_x use only Ae_i with
e_xAe_i=0; a sufficiently high nonzero syzygy X has infinite pd over both
A and C. This left-sided proof does not use opposite transfer.

## 2. A section gives an ordinary, visible Tor bridge

Choose a strictly unital, augmented minimal S-relative A_infinity model
H of q, with H^0=C and |m_r|=2-r. Connectivity makes the projection
p:H -> C a strict A_infinity morphism: m_2 preserves degree, whereas
every m_r with r>=3 has negative output, including on degree-zero inputs.

### Theorem 2.1 — Split Tor bridge

Suppose there is a strictly unital, S-augmented A_infinity morphism

\[
s:C\longrightarrow H,\qquad s_1:C\longrightarrow H^0=C
\text{ equal to the identity}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For every retained pair y,z and n>=0,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(R_z,L_y)
\text{ is a split summand of }\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y).}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Every reverse-supported infinite Tor witness of C therefore lifts to A.

#### Proof

On degree-zero inputs, |s_r|=1-r forces negative output for r>=2.
Thus p s is strictly the identity: the linear term is the identity and
all other Taylor coefficients vanish. The maps retain Peirce endpoints.

The direct-sum reduced relative bar construction gives chain maps

\[
B(p)B(s)=1_{B(C)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Their evaluation on one finite word involves only finitely many
partitions, so no completion or convergence assumption is hidden.
Taking the (z,y) Peirce summand and homology gives the split injection
from Tor_C to Tor_H. The minimal-model equivalence and (1.2) identify
the latter with ordinary Tor_A.

Finally e_yCe_z is a quotient of e_yAe_z. A nonzero reverse Peirce
component in C gives the required component in A. This separately
checks visibility as well as survival. QED.

The local-trivial two-stage asset already proves this mechanism when
kappa=0. Theorem 2.1 removes its amplitude restriction: H may have
unbounded negative cohomology and mixed higher operations.
It does not assert that every derived quotient admits a section.

### Proposition 2.2 — Pure degree-zero criterion, mixed operations allowed

Let R=rad C. A sufficient condition for (2.1), in one strictly augmented
minimal model, is

\[
m_r(R^{\otimes_S r})=0\quad\text{for every }r\ge3.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Operations with negative inputs need not vanish. More precisely, (2.1)
is equivalent to the existence of an adapted minimal model satisfying
(2.4), related to H by an augmented change of coordinates whose linear
term is the identity.

#### Proof

Under (2.4), the ordinary inclusion C -> H is a strict A_infinity
morphism, so Theorem 2.1 applies.

Conversely, given s, extend its reduced higher Taylor coefficients to
bar H by making them zero on any word with a negative-degree input,
and put G_1=1_H. This defines an invertible cofree bar-coalgebra map G:
the higher components strictly lower length, so its inverse is a finite
triangular calculation on each word. Augmentation and Peirce labels are
preserved; degree also gives B(p)G=B(p).

For the coderivation b of H set b'=G^{-1}bG. On bar C, G is B(s).
The section equation therefore gives

\[
b'B(\mathrm{incl})=B(\mathrm{incl})b_C.
\]

C is a strict A_infinity subalgebra for b', and its pure higher
operations vanish. Minimality and m_2 are unchanged by G_1=1.
No model-invariance claim about primitive or higher-output quotients
is being made. QED.

This is an actual chain retraction, not a bounded-arity page argument.

### Corollary 2.3 — Global formal-deletion exclusion

In a vertex-minimal counterexample to Universal Gap A, the retained
derived quotient at any vertex x with pd_A L_x infinite cannot admit
(2.1). In particular it cannot be formal as an augmented algebra over S,
or have only mixed higher operations in an adapted model.

#### Proof

Section 1 gives gldim C infinite, without opposite transfer.
Vertex minimality makes C infinite +. Theorem 2.1 would make A
infinite +, a contradiction. Augmented formality provides a model
with only m_2, which satisfies (2.4). QED.

This excludes formal deletions from the global minimal-deletion problem,
including bounded and local-trivial screening. It strengthens the scope
of T1's formal exclusion from Branch U. It does not exclude arbitrary
non-formal quotients or close all of Target A or Target B.

## 3. A section in the ordinary multiplication table

### Proposition 3.1 — Ordinary corner section

If B=fAf -> C=B/(fAeAf) has a unital algebra section sigma fixing the
retained idempotents, the split ordinary Tor comparison (2.2) holds
independently of formality.

#### Proof

Let chi_x:A -> k be the simple character at x and D=C x k. The maps

\[
\pi:A\longrightarrow D,\qquad a\longmapsto(a\bmod AeA,\chi_x(a)),
\]

\[
\iota:D\longrightarrow A,\qquad(c,\lambda)\longmapsto
\sigma(c)+\lambda e_x
\tag{3.1}
\]

are unital algebra maps preserving the full vertex algebra S_A, and
pi iota=1_D. The two image summands are orthogonal. Elements of rad C
have nilpotent images, hence zero image in the product of fields A/J;
augmentation is also preserved.

The ordinary S_A-relative bar complexes are chain retracts. For retained
endpoints the D bar complex equals the C bar complex, since the extra
k factor has zero radical and cannot occur in a positive-length retained
word. This proves (2.2) using ordinary bar homology. QED.

Alternatively sigma followed by B -> q is a strict dg section.
This is the actual B inclusion, not the generally nonmultiplicative
projection a -> faf from A.

## 4. A presentation condition supplies sections at every deletion

Write A=kQ/I with I admissible. For each vertex x, let rho_x be the
path-algebra endomorphism killing e_x and every path visiting x and
fixing every path avoiding x. Its image is the full-subquiver path
algebra after deleting x; it is nonunital as a map to kQ.

Assume

\[
\rho_x(I)\subseteq I\quad\text{for every vertex }x.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This concerns actual relations, not the support graph. It suffices to
check the erasures of an ideal generating set, since rho_x is
multiplicative.

A sufficient condition is that each defining relation is a linear
combination of parallel paths with the same set of visited vertices.
Common left/right multiplication preserves equality of those sets.
Every monomial presentation satisfies (4.1). Nonmonomial relations
such as ell_{i+1}a_i-a_i ell_i^{d_i} also satisfy this sufficient
condition: both paths visit {i,i+1}, regardless of their lengths.

### Lemma 4.1 — Strict section and closure under further deletion

Each rho_x descends to an idempotent endomorphism delta_x of A with

\[
\ker\delta_x=Ae_xA,\qquad\delta_x(1)=1-e_x.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Its image lies in fAf and is isomorphic to C, providing the ordinary
section of Proposition 3.1. The smaller quotient C again satisfies (4.1).

#### Proof

In kQ the kernel of rho_x is exactly the ideal generated by e_x:
a path visits x exactly when it factors through e_x.
Stability of I makes the map descend. If delta_x(a)=0, represent a by
p in kQ. Then rho_x(p) belongs to I; hence modulo I, a is represented
by p-rho_x(p), a member of the ideal generated by e_x.
The converse inclusion is immediate. Idempotence gives the
image/quotient isomorphism and section.

The erasures commute on paths. For y!=x, rho_y preserves I and
the ideal of paths visiting x, so descends to C as its vertex erasure.
This proves closure. QED.

## 5. A genuine terminating descent

### Theorem 5.1 — Arbitrary-size Gap A under (4.1)

Every elementary finite-dimensional algebra with an admissible
presentation satisfying (4.1) satisfies

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gldim}A=\infty
\ \Longrightarrow\ A\text{ is infinite }+.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

Suppose a counterexample in this class exists and choose one with the
fewest vertices. Choose x with pd_A L_x infinite.
UGA-C058 gives gldim C infinite. Lemma 4.1 keeps C in the same class
and removes exactly one vertex. Minimality makes C infinite +.
Proposition 3.1 lifts its visible ordinary witness to A, a contradiction.

Equivalently a bad A gives a bad C: the split Tor comparison passes
non-infinite-+ to C, while the actual-tail comparison passes infinite
global dimension to C. The number of vertices strictly decreases.
A one-vertex endpoint cannot be bad: a nonsemisimple finite-dimensional
local algebra has a nonterminating minimal free resolution of its simple,
and nonzero diagonal Tor in every degree. Thus no infinite descent
is possible. QED.

This closes the whole class, irrespective of its local screening case.
The monomial case is already known and is used as a calibration, not
claimed as a new algebra class; see
[Armenta, Sections 1 and 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20849v1).
The same-section mechanism beyond monomial presentations is proved here,
but its novelty has not been independently checked.

### Theorem 5.2 — Quantitative termination bound

Let A satisfy (4.1), with v>=1 vertices. If M>=1 and

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y)=0
\quad(e_yAe_z\ne0,\ n\ge M),
\tag{5.2}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gldim}A\le(v-1)M.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

Induct on v. For v=1, (5.2) forces the local algebra to be semisimple,
so its global dimension is zero.

For v>=2, fix any source x. Proposition 3.1 passes (5.2), with the
same M, to C=A/Ae_xA: reverse support in C implies reverse support
in A, and every C Tor is a summand of the corresponding A Tor.
Induction gives gldim C <= (v-2)M.

In the minimal resolution P of L_x, (5.2) gives e_xP_n=0 for all n>=M.
These terms use only Ae_i with e_xAe_i=0. For X=Omega_A^M L_x,
UGA-C058 identifies its entire tail with a minimal C-resolution, giving
pd_A X=pd_C X. If X!=0,

\[
\operatorname{pd}_A L_x
\le M+\operatorname{pd}_C X
\le M+(v-2)M.
\]

If X=0 the bound holds as well. This applies to every source x,
and bounds gldim A. QED.

The bound is uniform within this class for fixed v and the all-source
disappearance threshold M. It is not a screening-page bound, not a
finite-window test, and not applicable to arbitrary presentations.

## 6. Calibration: path-length grading is insufficient

Take arrows a:1->2, b:2->1 and a loop c at 1. Kill all length-three
paths and impose, among quadratic paths,

\[
ba=c^2,\qquad ab=ac=cb=0.
\]

The basis is e1,e2,a,b,c,t, with t=ba=c^2!=0. These are consistent
bound-quiver relations, and A has a path-length grading.

Erasing vertex 2 sends ba-c^2 to -c^2, nonzero in A, so (4.1) fails.
For this deletion,

\[
fAf=k[c]/(c^3),\qquad C=k[c]/(c^2).
\]

There is no ordinary algebra section: every lift c+alpha c^2 has square
c^2!=0. This calculation works in every characteristic.

This does not contradict Gap A or Theorem 2.1. Here e_2Ae_2=k,
and Ae_2 tensor_k e_2A -> A is injective: its four tensor basis
elements map to e2,a,b,t. The reused derived-quotient cohomology
formula therefore gives q equivalent to C. A derived A_infinity
section exists and the Tor bridge applies.
An ordinary corner section is sufficient, not necessary, for the bridge.

Thus an arbitrary admissible presentation, a length grading, or an
unverified choice of path representatives cannot replace (4.1).

## 10. Continued advance — ordinary sections descend without a chosen presentation

The presentation condition in Section 4 is sufficient, but is not needed
for the following structural closure theorem.

### Lemma 10.1 — Sections pass to other primitive deletion quotients

Suppose the ordinary corner quotient at y has a label-preserving unital
section. It gives an idempotent, generally nonunital endomorphism

\[
\delta_y:A\longrightarrow A,\qquad
\ker\delta_y=Ae_yA,\quad
\delta_y(e_i)=e_i\ (i\ne y),\quad\delta_y(e_y)=0.
\]

For x!=y, delta_y preserves Ae_xA and induces an idempotent endomorphism
on C_x=A/Ae_xA with kernel C_x e_y C_x. Hence the ordinary corner
quotient at y in C_x also has a label-preserving section.
No commutation of the original sections is assumed.

#### Proof

Because delta_y fixes e_x, it sends Ae_xA into Ae_xA, so the induced map
is defined. If delta_y(a) belongs to Ae_xA, then

\[
a=(a-\delta_y(a))+\delta_y(a)\in Ae_yA+Ae_xA,
\]

since a-delta_y(a) belongs to the kernel of delta_y.
The reverse inclusion in the induced kernel follows because the y
idempotent is killed. Idempotence and the other fixed labels give
the image/quotient section, with image in the retained corner. QED.

### Theorem 10.2 — Closure when ordinary sections exist at all infinite-pd vertices

If every vertex y with pd_A L_y infinite has a label-preserving
ordinary corner section

\[
A/Ae_yA\longrightarrow (1-e_y)A(1-e_y),
\tag{10.1}
\]

then A satisfies Universal Gap A. No condition is imposed at
finite-pd vertices, and no path-presentation hypothesis is required.

#### Proof

Suppose A is a counterexample with (10.1). Choose x of infinite pd.
The real-tail comparison gives gldim C_x infinite. The section at x
and Proposition 3.1 give a split Tor comparison for every retained
simple pair, so C_x is also non-infinite-+.

If a retained L_y has infinite pd over C_x, its nonterminating minimal
resolution has some right simple Tor nonzero in infinitely many degrees.
The split comparison implies pd_A L_y infinite. Thus y was already
an infinite-pd vertex of A and has a section there. Lemma 10.1 passes
that section to C_x. The structural hypothesis is therefore inherited.

Define p(B) to be the number of left simple B-modules of infinite pd.
The same Tor comparison shows

\[
1\le p(C_x)\le p(A)-1.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Iterating would give an infinite strictly decreasing sequence of positive
integers. This is impossible. When p=1, the next quotient has p=0,
contradicting its already proved infinite global dimension.
This proves the theorem. QED.

This is a well-founded descent in the number of infinite-pd simples,
not a claim that arbitrary deletion preserves or reduces that number.
The split ordinary Tor comparison is essential at each step.
Sections need not commute, and finite-pd sources need no sections.

### Corollary 10.3 — The earlier bound applies beyond erasure presentations

If every primitive corner quotient of A has an ordinary label-preserving
section, Lemma 10.1 makes this condition deletion-closed. The proof of
Theorem 5.2 then applies verbatim and yields gldim A <= (v-1)M under
all reverse-supported vanishing for every n>=M.

For this quantitative assertion sections at every vertex remain required;
the weaker infinite-pd-only hypothesis of Theorem 10.2 does not by itself
justify this numerical bound. If A has finite global dimension, that
weaker hypothesis is vacuous.
