## I. Canonical obstruction

Let \(A\) be an elementary finite-dimensional algebra over **any** field \(k\), with radical \(J\), \(S=A/J=k^N\), left simples \(L_y=Ae_y/Je_y\), and right simples \(R_x=e_xA/e_xJ\). Set

\[
\beta_{xy}(n)=\dim_k\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_x,L_y)
=\dim_k\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(L_y,L_x),\qquad
C_{yx}=\dim_k e_yAe_x.
\]

The target is a **fixed** pair \((x,y)\) with \(C_{yx}>0\) and \(\beta_{xy}(n)>0\) infinitely often. This is reverse Peirce support. In our left-module convention an arrow \(y\to x\) lies in \(e_xJe_y/J^2\), while \(\operatorname{Hom}_A(Ae_r,Ae_s)=e_rAe_s\).

The global failure condition is the existing condition

\[
(Z_b):\quad C_{yx}>0\Longrightarrow\beta_{xy}(n)=0\quad(n>b).
\]

For a real minimal left resolution \(P_\bullet(L_y)\), its multiplicity of \(Ae_x\) in degree \(n\) is exactly \(\beta_{xy}(n)\). Therefore

\[
\omega_n:=\sum_{x,y}C_{yx}\beta_{xy}(n)
=\operatorname{tr}(C B_n)
=\sum_y\dim_k e_yP_n(L_y),\qquad B_n=(\beta_{xy}(n)).
\tag{1}
\]

Thus \((Z_b)\) is precisely \(\omega_n=0\) for every \(n>b\). The coefficients are nonnegative; no cancellation is hidden in this scalar test. This is the existing supported aggregate, not a new invariant.

Infinite global dimension already forces some left simple to have infinite pd. Its minimal resolution has a nonzero term in **every** degree. Tensoring by \(S\) kills all minimal differentials, so \(\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(S,S)\ne0\) in every degree. Finite \(N^2\) pigeonhole then fixes a pair returning infinitely often. **Fixing the pair is already solved. The missing step is reverse visibility.**

The stronger pointwise implication

\[
\beta_{xy}(n)>0\text{ infinitely often}\Longrightarrow C_{yx}>0
\tag{2}
\]

is **FALSE**, even for a radical-square-zero two-vertex algebra; see Appendix B. Consequently a valid universal proof must use the **whole** badness condition, possibly changing the persistent pair. It cannot merely apply visibility to whichever pair pigeonhole first selects.

## IV. Main-route theorem skeleton

Write \(A=H/I\) with \(H=kQ\), \(F\) the arrow ideal and \(F^\ell\subseteq I\subseteq F^2\). This is the elementary bound-quiver presentation, not an assumption that \(F=\operatorname{rad}H\). Define \(I^0=H\) and

\[
K_{2r}=\frac{I^r\cap F I^{r-1}F}{F I^r+I^rF}\quad(r\ge1),\qquad
K_{2r+1}=\frac{F I^r\cap I^rF}{I^{r+1}+F I^rF}\quad(r\ge0).
\]

The safe skeleton is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{gd}A=\infty
&\xRightarrow{\text{PROVED: actual minimal simple resolution}}
 \operatorname{Tor}^A_n(S,S)\ne0\text{ for every }n\\
&\xRightarrow{\text{LITERATURE THEOREM: Bongartz formula, exact Tor scope}}
 K_n\ne0\text{ for every }n\\
&\xRightarrow{\text{PROVED: finite }N^2\text{ pigeonhole}}
 \exists(x,y):e_xK_ne_y\ne0\text{ infinitely often}. 
\end{aligned}
\]

From the last line, \(e_yAe_x\ne0\) for **that same pair** is a **FALSE** arrow (Appendix B). Replace it by the single global arrow

\[
\boxed{\omega_n=0\text{ eventually}\quad
\xRightarrow{\text{CURRENTLY UNKNOWN / EXACTLY UGA}}
K_n=0\text{ eventually}.}
\]

Concretely, \((Z_b)\) says that for all \(C_{yx}>0\) and all sufficiently large \(r\),

\[
e_x(I^r\cap F I^{r-1}F)e_y
=e_x(F I^r+I^rF)e_y,
\tag{3}
\]

\[
e_x(F I^r\cap I^rF)e_y
=e_x(I^{r+1}+F I^rF)e_y.
\tag{4}
\]

The unknown is whether these simultaneous equalities on **every visible block**, with a common eventual range, imply the equalities on **all** blocks eventually. If they do, \(K_n=0\) eventually, hence ordinary simple Tor vanishes eventually and \(\operatorname{gd}A<\infty\) (**PROVED ordinary minimal-resolution implication**), contradicting the first line. Conversely UGA immediately implies this eventual-total-vanishing statement. Thus the reduction has one exact open arrow and does not disguise a proof gap as a new theorem.

## Appendix A. Complete ordinary derivation and orientation audit of the ideal formulas

The elementary quiver presentation is admissible; this gives \(F/I=J\) and \(H/F=S\). \(H\) is hereditary even when \(Q\) has cycles. Every left ideal of \(H\) is projective, so for every \(r\ge0\)

\[
P_{2r}=I^r/I^{r+1},\qquad
P_{2r+1}=I^rF/I^{r+1}F
\]

is projective as a left \(A\)-module: each is \(A\otimes_H\) the corresponding projective left ideal. Inclusions in

\[
\cdots\subset I^2F\subset I^2\subset IF\subset I\subset F\subset H
\]

induce a projective resolution \(\cdots\to P_2\to P_1\to A\to S\to0\). Exactness follows by taking the two consecutive quotients of each nested triple; its even and odd kernels are respectively

\[
\ker d_{2r}=I^rF/I^{r+1},\qquad
\ker d_{2r-1}=I^r/I^rF.
\]

At \(r=0\) the first formula includes the augmentation kernel (F/I). For \(n\ge1\), right exactness gives

\[
\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(S,S)
\simeq\ker\big(S\otimes_A\ker d_{n-1}\longrightarrow S\otimes_AP_{n-1}\big).
\]

For \(n=2r\), the domain and target are

\[
\frac{I^r}{F I^r+I^rF}
\longrightarrow
\frac{I^{r-1}F}{F I^{r-1}F+I^rF}.
\]

The map is induced by inclusion, so its kernel is precisely the displayed \(K_{2r}\). For \(n=2r+1\) the map is

\[
\frac{I^rF}{F I^rF+I^{r+1}}
\longrightarrow
\frac{I^r}{F I^r+I^{r+1}},
\]

whose kernel is \(K_{2r+1}\). The denominators lie in their stated intersection numerators because \(I\subset F^2\). All constructions preserve the outside idempotents, so these are \(S\)-bimodule isomorphisms and

\[
e_xK_ne_y\simeq\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(R_x,L_y).
\tag{5}
\]

Orientation anchors: \(K_1=F/F^2\); the arrow \(y\to x\) contributes to \(\operatorname{Tor}_1(R_x,L_y)\), since \(Ae_x\) covers that head of \(Je_y\). Also \(K_2=I/(FI+IF)\). Visibility is the **reverse** block \(e_y(H/I)e_x\), not \(e_x(H/I)e_y\). Plain vector duality identifies \(5\) with the dual of ordinary \(\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(L_y,L_x)\); no unexamined dual-bimodule convention is needed.

The canonical resolution may be far from minimal. Nonzero \(I^r\), a nonzero \(P_n\), or a path overlap do not imply \(K_n\ne0\). The formulas compute **homology after the relevant intersection/sum quotient**, not a projective-term count. Field perfection is unnecessary for these Tor calculations; Huber–Kalck explicitly record this in Remark 2.3\(2\). No opposite-\(+\) comparison is used.

## Appendix B. Actual two-vertex disproof of the same-pair visibility arrow

Take vertices \(0,1\), an arrow \(a:0\to1\), a loop \(x:1\to1\), and \(A=kQ/F^2\). The basis is \(e_0,e_1,a,x\), with all radical products zero. Its **actual** minimal kernels are

\[
\Omega L_0=L_1,\qquad \Omega L_1=L_1.
\]

Indeed \(JP_0=ka\simeq L_1\), \(JP_1=kx\simeq L_1\). Thus

\[
\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(R_1,L_0)=k\quad(n\ge1),\qquad e_0Ae_1=0.
\]

For \(I=F^2\), both parity formulas reduce to \(K_n=F^n/F^{n+1}\). The class \(x^{n-1}a\in e_1K_ne_0\) is the canonical ideal representative of the same persistent invisible pair. This is an admissible finite algebra over any field, so the pointwise bridge is conclusively false.

Nevertheless \(\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(R_1,L_1)=k\) for every \(n\ge1\), and \(e_1Ae_1\ne0\). **UGA holds in this algebra.** It breaks the proposed same-pair theorem, not UGA. Even total high visible vanishing for the one source \(L_0\) cannot replace the global condition on all sources.

## Appendix D. Trace does give finite identities, but not an automatic contradiction

The localized ILP hypothesis is **\(e\)-boundedness**: every sufficiently high projective term has top annihilated by \(e\). It is not the assertion that the whole module has finite pd. The \(e\)-trace of these high terms vanishes, so its alternating sum is finite and independent of the lifts. Additivity requires the modules in the exact sequence to be \(e\)-bounded. Finite injective dimension of the simple supported by a primitive \(e\) supplies this boundedness for all modules (with the appropriate module-side convention), and leads to the radical-triviality statement in ILP Theorem 1.5. Strong no loop concludes \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(S_e,S_e)=0\) when that simple has finite pd or finite id, not a supported persistent ordinary tail.

Under \((Z_b)\), each \(L_y\)'s minimal resolution is \(e_y\)-bounded, since actually \(e_yP_n(L_y)=0\) at high degree. Thus it is wrong to say trace can never be used on an infinite resolution. The gap is different: for **one fixed** \(e_y\), \((Z_b)\) does not control the high \(e_y\)-tops of every other source and intermediate module needed in a filtration. Using its own idempotent for each simple does not prove the common-\(e_y\) additivity hypotheses. No trace class known to be nonzero follows merely from the remaining invisible ordinary Tor.

For a hub \(L_u\) with a finite resolution \(Q_\bullet\) of length \(d\), lifts \(h:P_\bullet\to Q_\bullet\), \(g:Q_\bullet\to P_\bullet\) of zero maps of simples satisfy the null-homotopy trace identity

\[
\operatorname{tr}_{HS}(h_0g_0)
=\operatorname{tr}_{HS}(g_0h_0)
=\sum_{i=1}^d(-1)^{i+1}\operatorname{tr}_{HS}(h_ig_i).
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed \(hg\) lifts the zero endomorphism of \(L_u\), and the alternating trace of its null-homotopy telescopes on the **finite** \(Q_\bullet\). Cyclicity permits computing the degree-zero class on either projective. This is the mechanism of Armenta Lemma 10.9; his following Remark 10.10 explicitly records that this identity alone does not give a contradiction.

Check \(7\) on the frozen Saorín/N7 actual algebra: \(a:0\to1,b:1\to0\), \(aba=0\). Basis

\[
e_0,e_1,a,b,p=ba,q=ab,r=bab,
\]

with nonzero radical products \(ba=p,ab=q,bq=pb=r\). Its infinite resolution of \(L_0\) has \(P_0=Ae_0\), \(P_i=Ae_1\) for \(i\ge1\), \(d_1=\rho a\), \(d_i=\rho q\) for \(i\ge2\). The real finite-pd hub resolution is

\[
0\to Ae_0\xrightarrow{\rho b}Ae_1\to L_1\to0.
\]

## Appendix F. Why Artin–Rees / ideal-power finiteness is not automatic

The path algebra need not be Noetherian. For the one-vertex two-loop quiver, \(H=k\langle x,y\rangle\), even \(A=H/F^2\) is finite-dimensional and admissible. The left ideals

\[
L_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m}Hxy^jx
\]

strictly increase: \(xy^{m+1}x\) has none of \(xy^jx\), \(j\le m\), as a suffix, so it cannot lie in the monomial left ideal \(L_m\). Thus \(H\) is not left Noetherian; the analogous prefix argument gives the right boundary. A positively graded Rees ring with degree-zero quotient \(H\) cannot automatically be Noetherian either.

Nor does \(J^\ell=0\) in \(A\) imply \(I^r=0\) in \(H\); for instance \(I=F^2\) in this example has nonzero powers in every degree. The relevant obstruction is the **intersection modulo the sum**, not the presence of powers or cycles. A proposed Artin–Rees use must provide its actual ring, filtration, module, finiteness and two-sided compatibility hypotheses. This appendix refutes automatic availability, not all possible specialized Artin–Rees applications.
