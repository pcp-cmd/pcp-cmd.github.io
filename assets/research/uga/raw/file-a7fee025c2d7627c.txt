## 1. Exact hypotheses and statement — C101

nonempty proper set of primitive labels, S=sum_{f in F} L_f,

\[
d=\max_F\operatorname{pd}_A L_f<\infty,
\qquad r=\max_F\operatorname{pd}_{A^{op}} R_f<\infty.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus S has LEFT pd at most d and LEFT injective dimension at most r,
by ordinary finite-dimensional duality. Put

\[
E=\operatorname{Ext}^*_A(S,S),\quad
q=\max\{p:E^p\ne0\},\quad w=\max(q-1,0).
\tag{1.2}
\]

E uses actual ordinary Yoneda postcomposition. E^0=k^F;
E^{>0}=rad E and q<=min(d,r). Assume that this finite-dimensional
ordinary algebra E has finite global dimension g. The ungraded bound
g also bounds the lengths of its homogeneous minimal resolutions:
graded projective covers exist, are ungraded projective covers, and
a homogeneous kernel which is ungraded projective is graded projective.

### Theorem 1.1

For EVERY finite LEFT A-module M, the actual counit cone

\[
C_M=\operatorname{Cone}(Ae\otimes_B^{\mathbb L}eM\longrightarrow M)
\tag{1.3}
\]

has cohomology only in [-a_L,0], where

\[
a_L=r+gw.
\tag{1.4}
\]

All its factors lie in F. In particular

\[
\operatorname{pd}_{B^{op}} Ae\le\max(a_L-1,0).
\tag{1.5}
\]

The direct RIGHT proof gives a_R=d+gw and
pd_B eA<=max(a_R-1,0). ALL finite modules have natural ordinary

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(M,Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{Ext}^n_B(eM,eZ),
\qquad
\operatorname{Tor}^B_n(Re,eM)\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R,M)
\quad(n>N),
\tag{1.6}
\]

where

\[
N=d+r+g\max(q-1,0).
\tag{1.7}
\]

The bound is sufficient; sharpness is not asserted. Replacing q by
min(d,r) gives a weaker bound without measuring the top F Ext degree.

Both retained infinite-pd label sets are EXACTLY the old sets. For a

with the analogous max(N,other gd) bounds.

## 2. Realize a terminating ordinary E resolution

Write G(X)=sum_n Hom_{D(A)}(X,S[n]), a graded LEFT E-module. For
M in degree zero, G(M) has degrees only 0,...,r and is finite.

Take its actual minimal homogeneous E-projective resolution. Let K_0=G(M),
P_i -> K_i be its projective covers, and K_{i+1} the kernels. Since
gd(E)=g, K_g is projective and the last cover P_g -> K_g is an isomorphism.
If a kernel vanishes earlier, take subsequent objects zero. We use only
g+1 covers. This is the terminating measure, not a support-graph cycle.

Every P_i is a finite sum of E-indecomposable projectives generated in
degrees sigma. The following elementary degree bounds are crucial:

\[
i\le\sigma\le r+iq\qquad(0\le i\le g).
\tag{2.1}
\]

At i=0 generators are degrees 0,...,r. For i>0 minimality puts
K_i inside rad P_{i-1}=E^{>0}P_{i-1}, so its least possible degree is
at least i. The highest degree in P_{i-1} is at most r+iq. Its kernel
and all minimal generators have no higher degrees. This proves (2.1).
If q=0, E is semisimple and g=0; no positive-degree induction is needed.

G(L_f[sigma]) is the E-projective for f generated in degree sigma.
For any bounded X, a homogeneous map G(L_f[sigma]) -> G(X) is specified
by the image of that generator, equivalently a morphism
X -> L_f[sigma] in D(A). Finite direct sums give the same assertion.
This is ordinary Yoneda representation; no formal dg-model assumption is used.

Set X_0=M. Suppose G(X_i)=K_i. Realize P_i -> K_i by

\[
X_i\xrightarrow{u_i}S_i\longrightarrow X_{i+1}
\longrightarrow X_i[1],
\qquad S_i=\bigoplus L_f[\sigma].
\tag{2.2}
\]

The map on G is onto in EVERY degree. The actual triangle long exact
sequence consequently gives G(X_{i+1})=ker(P_i -> K_i)=K_{i+1}.
These X_i are bounded complexes because there are finitely many finite
S_i. Finally

\[
G(X_{g+1})=0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The stopping assertion concerns genuine E syzygies, including their
relations and kernels. Nilpotence of Yoneda products alone does not
make K_g projective and would not justify (2.3).

## 3. Actual minimal resolutions identify the counit

Take a bounded-above actual MINIMAL A-projective complex Q representing
X_{g+1}, with finite projectives in each degree. It may initially have
an infinite negative tail. Minimality makes Hom_A(Q,S) have zero
differentials, since S is semisimple. Equation (2.3) therefore says:
NO term of Q has an A e_f summand for f in F. Every term is in add(Ae).
This inference uses actual minimality, not singular/Tate information.

Applying the exact functor e(-) to (2.2), eS_i=0 gives

\[
eX_{g+1}\simeq eM[g+1].
\tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently eQ[-g-1] is an actual B-projective complex resolving eM.
It is minimal: differentials between retained projectives lie in
e(rad A)e=rad B. A bounded-above minimal complex with cohomology only
in degree zero has no positive-degree terms, since a highest positive
projective would have to be hit surjectively by a radical differential.
Thus this is an ordinary minimal B-projective resolution, not a
complex declared exact after deleting finite-pd images.

Term by term Ae tensor_B eQ is Q. The resulting map Q[-g-1] -> M
obtained by composing the rotated triangles in (2.2) has e-image the
identity of eM. By actual derived adjunction it is the counit map.
It follows that X_{g+1}[-g-1] is Ae tensor_B^L eM, with the correct map.

Rotate and shift (2.2): setting T_i=X_i[-i], there are triangles

\[
T_{i+1}\longrightarrow T_i\longrightarrow S_i[-i]
\longrightarrow T_{i+1}[1].
\tag{3.2}
\]

The octahedral axiom, or literal iterated cones, makes C_M a finite
extension of S_i[-i] for 0<=i<=g. Each such object is a sum of
L_f[sigma-i]. By (2.1)

\[
0\le\sigma-i\le r+i(q-1)\le r+g\max(q-1,0).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the actual cone has amplitude [-a_L,0] and F cohomology. Replacing
each L_f by its finite A-projective resolution further represents the
cone by a projective complex in [-a_L-d,0]. This is an actual bounded
repair. It proves (1.4) without any convergence assertion for an
unbounded spectral sequence.

For i>=1, H^{-i-1}(C_M)=Tor^B_i(Ae,eM). Every finite B-module X occurs
as eM, using the ordinary finite A-module M=Ae tensor_B X. Therefore
these Tor groups vanish above max(a_L-1,0), proving (1.5).
