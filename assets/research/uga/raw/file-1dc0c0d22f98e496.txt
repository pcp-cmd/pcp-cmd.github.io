# Rees torsion, the screening corridor, and cycle-lift obstructions

2026-09-27. Project-derived results, with full proofs below. The statements have been self-audited; their precise independent review scope is recorded separately in MRS. No novelty claim is made. Universal Gap A remains open.

## 1. Fixed setup and the question

The primary project concerns arbitrary finite-dimensional basic complex algebras, with no bound on the number of vertices. This note uses the two-stage setup established in [Derived deletion, Sections 2–4](Derived-Deletion-Screening-Normal-Form.md). The arguments here also work over a field whenever the indicated split relative bar model exists.

Let \(C\) be finite-dimensional and elementary, \(S=\bigoplus_i ke_i\), \(J=\operatorname{rad}C\), and let \(M\) be a finite-dimensional \(C\)-bimodule. Fix a strictly unital augmented minimal model
\[
H=C\oplus M[1],\qquad m_2,\quad m_3=\kappa,\qquad m_{\ge4}=0.
\]
For fixed simples \(R_a,L_b\), write its relative bar complex as \(B=\bigoplus_{n,p}B_{n,p}\), where a \(J\)-letter contributes homological degree one, an \(M\)-letter contributes degree two, and \(p\) counts \(M\)-letters. Thus
\[
0\le p\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\quad
b_2:B_{n,p}\to B_{n-1,p},\quad
b_3:B_{n,p}\to B_{n-1,p+1}.
\]
All spaces in a fixed homological degree are finite-dimensional. The identities are \(b_2^2=b_3^2=b_2b_3+b_3b_2=0\).

With \(q=n-2p\), the decreasing filtration by \(p\) gives
\[
E_1^{p,q}=H_q(W_p),\qquad
W_p=R_a\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}M^{\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}p}\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}L_b,
\quad
d_r:(p,q)\longmapsto(p+r,q-2r-1).
\tag{1}
\]
For \(p=0\), \(W_0=R_a\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}L_b\). There are no incoming differentials into that column. The limiting edge is the image of
\[
\epsilon_n:H_n(B,b_2+b_3)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(R_a,L_b).
\tag{2}
\]
In a derived deletion with \(eAe=k\), the source of (2) is \(\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_a,L_b)\). We never identify ordinary \(A\to C\) with a homological epimorphism.

## 2. The screening corridor

**Theorem 1.** Suppose \(M\) has a finite \(C^e\)-projective resolution of length \(d\). For \(p\ge1\),
\[
E_1^{p,q}=0\quad\text{unless}\quad 0\le q\le pd.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently a nonzero outgoing differential from \(E_r^{0,n}\) must satisfy
\[
\boxed{\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{d+2}\right\rceil
\le r\le \left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** Choose a projective bimodule resolution \(P\to M\), supported in degrees \(0,\ldots,d\). Its terms are summands of finite sums of \(Ce_u\otimes_k e_vC\). Each term of \(P^{\otimes_Cp}\) is \(C^e\)-projective: the tensor of two displayed elementary bimodules is
\[
Ce_u\otimes_k(e_vCe_w)\otimes_k e_zC.
\]
The complex lies in degrees \(0,\ldots,pd\). It computes the derived tensor power because the factors are bounded complexes of left and right projectives. After tensoring on the left by \(R_a\), its terms remain right \(C\)-projective; the final tensor with \(L_b\) therefore computes \(W_p\). Its homology has exactly the amplitude claimed in (3). The target of a differential leaving \((0,n)\) on page \(r\) has coordinates \((r,n-2r-1)\). Nonzero terms on later pages are subquotients of their first-page positions, so \(0\le n-2r-1\le rd\), equivalent to (4). \(\square\)

Finite projective dimension on each of the two sides separately is not the bimodule hypothesis of this theorem.

## 3. Which class is Rees torsion?

Use a central variable \(t\), of homological degree zero, and set
\[
\mathcal B=(B\otimes_k k[t],D=b_2+t b_3),\qquad
\mathcal H_h=H_h(\mathcal B).
\tag{5}
\]
Assign auxiliary weight \(p-j\) to \(t^jB_{n,p}\). The differential preserves this weight. Modulo \(t\), the differential is \(b_2\); its Bockstein spectral sequence, with the auxiliary weight retained, is (1). Equivalently, the same correction equations compute both spectral sequences.

**Theorem 2 (page equals exponent, target degree).** A nonzero \(d_r\) leaving total degree \(n\) yields an element of exact \(t\)-torsion order \(r\) in \(\mathcal H_{n-1}\). In particular, the torsion element is in the target degree; the disappearing source class need not itself lift to Rees homology.

**Proof.** A page-\(r\) representative consists of \(z_s\in B_{n,p+s}\), \(0\le s<r\), satisfying
\[
b_2z_0=0,\qquad b_2z_s=-b_3z_{s-1}\quad(1\le s<r).
\]
Put \(Z=\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}t^sz_s\) and \(w=b_3z_{r-1}\). The two anticommutation identities give \(Dw=0\), and direct expansion gives
\[
DZ=t^rw.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(t^r[w]=0\) in \(\mathcal H_{n-1}\). If \(t^{r-1}[w]=0\), write \(DY=t^{r-1}w\). Then \(D(Z-tY)=0\) is a polynomial lift of the original \(z_0\), forcing the outgoing \(d_r\) of its page-\(r\) class to vanish. This contradicts the chosen nonzero \(d_r\). For the filtration interpretation one takes the auxiliary-homogeneous component of \(Y\); this gives exactly the required filtration shifts. When \(p>0\), this argument does not exclude a later incoming differential and does not claim permanent survival. Therefore the order is exactly \(r\). \(\square\)

There is also a useful count interpretation. Localize at \((t)\), a discrete valuation ring. A complex of finite free modules bounded below splits degree by degree into one-term free blocks and two-term elementary blocks
\[
k[t]_{(t)}z\xrightarrow{\,t^r\,}k[t]_{(t)}w.
\tag{7}
\]
To see this, diagonalize a differential by Smith reduction. Nonzero diagonal entries force the adjacent differentials to vanish on those summands by \(D^2=0\) and torsion-freeness, so those blocks split off; proceed upwards in degree. Each positive \(r\) block contributes one page-\(r\) differential and one target-degree torsion summand of length \(r\). This counts torsion summands, not arbitrary named representatives on earlier pages.

Define \(\tau(h)\) as the largest exact \(t\)-torsion order in \(\mathcal H_h\), or zero if there is no such torsion. Combining Theorems 1 and 2 gives, whenever a zero-column class of degree \(n\) dies,
\[
\tau(n-1)\ \ge\ r\ \ge\
\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{d+2}\right\rceil.
\tag{8}
\]
Each individual \(\mathcal H_h\) is finitely generated over \(k[t]\). This alone gives no uniform bound as \(h\) grows.

## 4. Uniform torsion kills high-degree screening

**Theorem 3.** Retain \(M\in\operatorname{perf}(C^e)\) with projective dimension \(d\). Suppose there are \(h_0,N\ge0\) such that
\[
t^N T_t\mathcal H_h=0\quad(h\ge h_0),
\qquad
T_tV=\{v:t^rv=0\text{ for some }r\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Then the edge map (2) is surjective whenever
\[
n-1\ge h_0,\qquad n>(d+2)N+1.
\tag{10}
\]

**Proof.** A dying zero-column class in such a degree would have death page \(r\le N\) by Theorem 2 and (9), but \(r\ge\lceil(n-1)/(d+2)\rceil>N\) by (4). Thus no zero-column class can die. The filtration is finite in each total degree, so its limiting edge equals the entire zero column. This proves surjectivity. \(\square\)

**Noetherian criterion.** Condition (9) follows if the actual tail \(\bigoplus_{h\ge h_0}\mathcal H_h\) is a finitely generated module over a left Noetherian operator ring containing central \(t\), with the given \(t\)-action. Its \(t\)-power torsion is a submodule by centrality, hence has finitely many generators. The maximum of their annihilating exponents gives \(N\).

The Noetherian property and finite generation concern the whole tail and its actual operators. They have not been proved for general derived deletions. A formal cycle in a resolution graph does not supply them. This is a conditional theorem, not a proof of Universal Gap A.

**Supported-return consequence.** If \(C\) has a persistent pair with \(e_bCe_a\ne0\), conditions of Theorem 3 force persistent \(\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_a,L_b)\). The quotient map ensures \(e_bAe_a\ne0\). Thus a minimal vertex counterexample in this perfect-bimodule branch cannot also satisfy (9).

## 5. The precise cycle-lift complex

Work either at a fixed pair or in the direct sum of all pairs, with maps preserving both endpoint labels. Let \(\operatorname{End}^{L,\ell}(B)\) consist of graded \(k\)-linear maps taking \(B_{n,p}\) to \(B_{n+L,p+\ell}\). These are maps of the relative bar vector spaces; no unproved \(C^e\)-linearity is implied. Define
\[
\partial_iU=b_iU-(-1)^L U b_i\quad(i=2,3).
\]
Then \(\partial_2\) has bidegree \((-1,0)\), \(\partial_3\) has bidegree \((-1,1)\), and their squares and anticommutator vanish.

For a homogeneous candidate of auxiliary shift \(\ell\),
\[
U(t)=\sum_{m\ge0}t^mU_m,\qquad
U_m\in\operatorname{End}^{L,\ell+m}(B),
\]
the chain-map equations are exactly
\[
\partial_2U_0=0,\qquad
\partial_2U_{m+1}=-\partial_3U_m.
\tag{11}
\]
If the equations through \(U_m\) hold, the next obstruction is
\[
[\partial_3U_m]\in
H_{L-1}\bigl(\operatorname{End}^{*,\ell+m+1}(B),\partial_2\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]
It is a cycle because \(\partial_2\partial_3=-\partial_3\partial_2\) and \(\partial_3^2=0\). Its vanishing is exactly the existence of the next lift. The obstruction may depend on the preceding choices.

A prescribed bigraded map on \(H(B,b_2)\) can be lifted to \(U_0=i\sigma p\) using a filtration-preserving vector-space contraction. Existence of that prescribed map with meaningful return/generation properties is an additional step. A finite graph cycle or an infinite subsequence with matching labels is not such a map.

If all equations are solved, the expression \(U(t)\) is locally polynomial: for a fixed input in \(B_{n,p}\), its \(m\)-term vanishes when \(p+\ell+m>\lfloor(n+L)/2\rfloor\). Thus it defines an actual \(k[t]\)-linear chain operator without passing to formal power series. This rectification does not prove finite generation of its homology module.

## 6. A concrete obstruction to primitive rank-one transport

**Theorem 4.** For positive integers \(r<s\), every \(k[t]\)-linear map
\[
f:k[t]/(t^r)\longrightarrow k[t]/(t^s)
\]
has image in \(t^{s-r}k[t]/(t^s)\). In particular its reduction to the target modulo \(t\) is zero.

**Proof.** Write \(f(1)=a(t)\bmod t^s\). The relation \(t^rf(1)=0\) says \(t^s\mid t^ra(t)\); hence \(t^{s-r}\mid a(t)\). \(\square\)

For isolated cyclic target channels of elementary Rees blocks (7), a proposed operator transporting a primitive generator to a primitive generator while the exponent strictly grows is therefore impossible. In a pair of blocks of exponents \(r,r+\ell\), \(\ell>0\), the chain-map equation on coefficients reads \(b=\pm t^\ell a\). Thus the target transport vanishes modulo \(t\).

The hypothesis is about actual cyclic homology channels and their induced map. Rank-one numerical probes or a resolution graph do not supply isolated summands or this primitive transport. This theorem locates a genuine obstruction under explicit hypotheses; it does not show that a nonzero obstruction creates supported return or strict descent.

## 7. Exact calibration in the existing eight-dimensional deletion

Use the already audited example with \(C=k[u]/(u^3)\), \(M=k\), and \(\kappa(u,u,u^2)=\kappa(u,u^2,u)=1\). Write \(x=su\), \(y=su^2\), \(m=sM[1]\). Their homological degrees are \(1,1,2\), and internal weights \(1,2,4\).

Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
z_0&=xyxyxy,\\
z_1&=-xymxx-mxxxy-mxyxx,\\
w&=mxm-mmx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The tensor words in (13) are not commutative monomials. Exact bar expansion gives
\[
b_2z_0=0,\quad b_2z_1=-b_3z_0,\quad
b_3z_1=w,\quad Dw=0,\quad
D(z_0+tz_1)=t^2w.
\tag{14}
\]
Here \(z_0,z_1\) have total degree six; \(w\) has degree five. The calculation independently checks \(tw\) is not a boundary: in auxiliary weight one and internal weight nine, a possible preimage is in \(B_{6,1}\); the exact simultaneous linear equations \(b_2v=0,\ b_3v=w\) are inconsistent. There are no higher \(p\) terms in this degree and weight. Consequently \([w]\in\mathcal H_5\) has exact order two.

The computation is over \(\mathbb Q\), with exact rational elimination; it extends to \(\mathbb C\). It checks the calibration and signs, not the general theorems. ## 8. What remains

The central remaining question is whether a finite-dimensional derived deletion can sustain the linearly growing torsion required by (8) without supported return or strict descent. The following are separate missing steps:

- construct a meaningful actual cycle shift on first-page homology;
- solve or interpret its successive obstructions;
- prove that actual operators generate the whole relevant homology tail;
- handle bimodules with infinite \(C^e\)-projective dimension, including finite projective dimension on each side separately.

The present results neither exclude those vertical cases nor convert the first nonzero End-complex obstruction into a returning Peirce class. The earlier finite-generation, prefix-perfectness and generic-point bridges remain open.
