## 2. The actual quotient preserves all high ordinary Tor

The real short exact sequence is

\[
0\longrightarrow F_n\longrightarrow X_n
 \longrightarrow V_n\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For EVERY right A-module R and EVERY r>d_n+1, its ordinary long exact
sequence gives the SAME-degree isomorphism induced by the projection:

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^A_r(R,X_n)
 \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Tor}^A_r(R,V_n)
 =\bigoplus_j\operatorname{Tor}^A_r(R,L_j)^{b_{j,n}}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Both Tor_r(R,F_n) and Tor_(r-1)(R,F_n) vanish at this strict cutoff.
Ordinary projective dimension shifting through the ORIGINAL exact
resolution of L_y gives, in the same range,

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^A_{n+r}(R,L_y)
 \cong\bigoplus_j\operatorname{Tor}^A_r(R,L_j)^{b_{j,n}}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

No differential or repeated image is deleted from the original complex.
The module quotient and its real exact sequence supply ALL exactness.
The corresponding first-variable ordinary Ext map is
Ext^r_A(V_n,Z) -> Ext^r_A(X_n,Z), an isomorphism for every target Z
and the same r>d_n+1. No singular or stable identification is used.

X_n is infinite-pd. Since F_n has finite pd, (2.1) implies V_n has
infinite pd. Otherwise both outer modules have finite pd and so does
X_n. Therefore some L_j occurring in V_n has infinite LEFT pd.

For this ONE fixed event n, (2.3) with every simple detector R_q implies

\[
T_y=\bigcup_{j:b_{j,n}>0}T_j.
\tag{2.4}

Finite-pd simple factors have empty T_j. Every infinite constituent
gives nonempty T_j subset T_y. This statement is about ACTUAL ordinary
recurrent projective types, not a smaller composition/image carrier
whose projective cover might expand as in C091.

The finite value d_n suffices for(2.4) independently at that event.

This is why pointwise finite error pd is sufficient here, unlike the
