# §0 Mathematical verdict

**Not solved.** Universal Gap A and the broader recurrence–visibility / corner–defect theory remain open, but dead-zone descent, five-vertex reduction, corner-to-ambient Tor comparison, and finite-length simple extraction now have verifier-accepted components; the central unresolved interface is Peirce compatibility under the extra structure of a vertex-minimal Gap-A failure.

**Closed components.** A high minimal syzygy tail supported on an idempotent corner forces that corner to have infinite global dimension (`59a12876ab799b9e`), and the dead-zone Hom hypothesis implies this support condition (`5da47c5107901438`). Strong connectivity yields the strict size bound (`4ac1cd635b7cb0e8`), while corner Tor can be transferred and decomposed into ambient persistent simple-pair alternatives under explicit acyclicity hypotheses (`78ea06837fb5f0cb`, `7d0a95b907de372f`).

**Viable proof architecture.** The live route is descent to a smaller recurrent corner, exact separation of direct lifting from comparison error, then defect recycling using minimal-counterexample dead-zone constraints and bilateral recurrence rather than generic Peirce multiplication.

**Main blocker.** Persistent cross-Tor and nonzero cross pieces do not force their product to be nonzero: `6bd89784ef94bef3` gives a two-vertex counterexample with persistent cross-Tor but (gAfAg=0). Thus general idempotent algebra is insufficient; the missing theorem must use the structural conditions of a vertex-minimal Gap-A failure.

**Highest-priority unresolved bridge.** Prove that, in a vertex-minimal Gap-A failure, every persistent simple pair arising from the corner-comparison error either occupies an ambient nonzero Peirce position or generates a strictly smaller secondary dead-zone recurrence to which the descent can be iterated.

**Method failure vs. proposition failure.** The generic cross-Tor-to-product route is a method failure, verifier-confirmed by `6bd89784ef94bef3`; this does not establish proposition failure for universal Gap A.

**Calibration caveat.** Do not read the verifier-accepted reduction package as a near-complete proof: Peirce compatibility is the load-bearing theorem and remains unmatched.

**Current best proof skeleton**

1. Assume a vertex-minimal finite-dimensional bound-quiver algebra (A) has infinite global dimension and is not infinite ((+)).
2. Use the exact one-way/syzygy-return theorem to obtain the Hom dead-zone condition.
3. Apply `5da47c5107901438` to obtain an infinite-global-dimension corner Δ=fAf.
4. Use `4ac1cd635b7cb0e8` to make Δ strictly smaller; for five vertices, `073459eead09a970` reduces to the three-vertex closure.
5. By vertex minimality, obtain an infinite-(+) witness in Δ.
6. Under the valid comparison acyclicity, transfer the witness via `78ea06837fb5f0cb` and decompose it using `7d0a95b907de372f`.
7. If the extracted simple pair has the compatible ambient Peirce block, obtain an ambient infinite-(+) witness.
8. Otherwise define its Peirce-zero displacement as a secondary defect and descend again.
9. Prove that finite vertex count plus bilateral (A/A^{op}) recurrence prevents infinite strict defect descent or a periodic derangement cycle.
10. Conclude that an ambient compatible witness must occur, contradicting failure of infinite ((+)).

**Central missing lemma.** Let (A=kQ/I) be vertex-minimal among finite-dimensional basic algebras with infinite global dimension that are not infinite ((+)). Let (f) be a dead-zone idempotent obtained from an infinite-projective-dimension simple, and suppose an infinite-(+) witness in (fAf) enters the ambient comparison package and yields one of the persistent simple pairs of `7d0a95b907de372f`. Then either the pair has a nonzero compatible ambient Peirce block, or there is a new proper idempotent (f'Af') of infinite global dimension together with a persistent recurrence whose support is strictly smaller in a well-founded invariant. The lemma must exclude the generic counterexample `6bd89784ef94bef3` by using vertex minimality and the origin of (f) as a syzygy dead zone.

# §3 Dangerous heuristic lines and strategies not to pursue

**Dangerous heuristic lines**

- “Persistent cross-Tor plus arrows both ways gives a Peirce return.” **Status: Mathematically incorrect as stated.** `6bd89784ef94bef3` explicitly refutes this. The replacement must exploit dead-zone origin and vertex minimality.
- “Finite-length dévissage solves visibility.” **Status: Conflated with a stronger claim.** `cde27e1302bea231` selects a fixed simple pair but says nothing about the required Peirce block.
- “The five-vertex reduction closes Gap A.” **Status: Conditional only.** `073459eead09a970` still assumes the three-vertex theorem and provides a corner witness, not ambient lifting.
- “Tor-acyclic induction is automatic for a corner.” **Status: Not automatic.** `78ea06837fb5f0cb` states it as an explicit hypothesis.
- “A repeated support state gives a nonzero path product.” **Status: Method shortcut, not a proof step.** Relations can kill the product even when each local cross piece is nonzero, as `6bd89784ef94bef3` illustrates.

**Strategies not to pursue**

- Do not prove a generic cross-Tor-to-product theorem: it is false by `6bd89784ef94bef3`.
- Do not erase the provenance of the three alternatives in `7d0a95b907de372f`: their composition-factor locations are the only route to extra structure.
- Do not stop at three- or five-vertex closure: extract the invariant that makes defect descent terminate.
- Do not assume Tor-acyclic induction; prove it or promote its failure to a new persistent defect.
- Do not use support graphs without relation-level multiplication or radical-filtration control.
- Do not treat an internally supplied low-vertex theorem as verified until it enters this fact graph.
