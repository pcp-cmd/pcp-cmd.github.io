## 2. Exact objects, orientations, and the actual graded minimal resolution

Let

\[
A=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{w}A_d,\qquad A_0=S=k^N,\qquad
J=\bigoplus_{d>0}A_d,
\]

be an ACTUAL positive grading of the elementary finite-dimensional algebra, over any field. The algebra need not be generated in degree1. Let \(w=\max\{d:A_d\ne0\}\). Set

\[
C_{yx}(t)=\sum_{d\ge0}\dim_k(e_yA_de_x)t^d,\qquad
D(t)=\det C(t)\in\mathbb Z[t],\quad D(0)=1.
\]

Rows are physical target vertices; columns are primitive projective sources. For the left simple \(L_y\) in degree0, write its ACTUAL minimal graded projective resolution as

\[
P_n(L_y)=\bigoplus_{x,d}(Ae_x)\langle d\rangle^{\beta_{xy}(n,d)},\qquad
B_n(t)_{xy}=\sum_d\beta_{xy}(n,d)t^d.
\]

Here \(\langle d\rangle\) places a primitive generator in degree \(d\). Forgetting the grading gives an actual minimal ordinary resolution, since its differentials still have radical image. Consequently

\[
\sum_d\beta_{xy}(n,d)=
\dim_k\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_x,L_y).
\tag{1}
\]

Reverse visibility is \(C_{yx}(1)=\dim e_yAe_x>0\), exactly as in C115. It is not \(C_{xy}(1)\).

**Generator-degree bounds.** Every nonzero generator of \(P_n\) has

\[
n\le d\le nw.
\tag{2}
\]

Proof: \(P_0\) has degree0 generators. In a minimal cover the new syzygy lies in \(JP_{n-1}\), so its minimum degree increases by at least1. A module contained in \(P_{n-1}\), whose highest component has degree at most \((n-1)w+w\), cannot have a top lift above \(nw\). Choose homogeneous top lifts for its real projective cover. Induction proves both inequalities, without uniform pd or module-state assumptions.

For each fixed internal degree \(q\), \(P_n[q]=0\) once \(n>q\). The degree-\(q\) resolution is thus a finite exact sequence. Taking its integer Euler characteristic gives the degreewise formal identity

\[
C(t)Q(t)=I,\qquad
Q(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^nB_n(t)=C(t)^{-1}
\quad\text{in }\operatorname{Mat}_N(\mathbb Z[[t]]).
\tag{3}
\]

This is an ordinary dimension calculation from actual minimal resolutions. The sum is coefficientwise finite; it is not an analytic evaluation at \(t=1\).

## 3. The direct ordinary logarithmic-derivative bridge

Jacobi's determinant identity applied over \(\mathbb Q[[t]]\) to the INTEGER matrix (3) yields

\[
\frac{D'(t)}{D(t)}
=\operatorname{tr}\big(C'(t)C(t)^{-1}\big)
=\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^n
 \sum_{x,y}C'_{yx}(t)B_n(t)_{xy}.
\tag{4}
\]

The derivative is of the polynomial of integer dimensions. It is NOT a derivative of structure constants in \(k\), and is never reduced modulo \(\operatorname{char}k\). Each coefficient in \(C'_{yx}B_{n,xy}\) is a nonnegative integer. If such a coefficient is nonzero, then (1) is nonzero and \(C_{yx}(1)>0\): an actual ordinary SUPPORTED pair is present. Different \(n\)'s can cancel in (4); a nonzero coefficient nonetheless certifies at least one real supported summand.

Assume \(D\ne1\) and put \(u=\deg D\ge1\), necessarily \(u\le Nw\). Write

\[
\frac{D'}D=\sum_{m\ge0}g_mt^m,\qquad
D=1+c_1t+\cdots+c_ut^u.
\]

The \(g_m\) are integers, by formal inversion of a polynomial with constant term1, and satisfy

\[
g_m+c_1g_{m-1}+\cdots+c_ug_{m-u}=0\qquad(m\ge u).
\tag{5}
\]

The series is not a polynomial: otherwise \(D'=DG\) with polynomial \(G\), impossible for nonconstant \(D\) in characteristic0 since \(\deg D'<\deg D\). These calculations take place in \(\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Q\), whatever the original field.

There cannot be \(u\) consecutive zero coefficients anywhere at nonnegative indices. Such a block beginning at \(L\ge0\), together with (5) starting at \(L+u\), would make every later coefficient zero, contrary to the preceding paragraph.

### Theorem 3.1 — Supported ordinary Tor in a uniform window

For EVERY integer \(b\ge0\), some pair \((x,y)\) and some \(n\) satisfy

\[
\boxed{
b<n\le(b+1)w+u-1,\qquad
e_yAe_x\ne0,\qquad
\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_x,L_y)\ne0.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Proof: choose a nonzero \(g_m\) among the \(u\) consecutive indices
\((b+1)w,\ldots,(b+1)w+u-1\). In (4), every term of homological degree \(n\le b\) has polynomial degree at most \(w-1+nw\le(b+1)w-1\), by (2), so none contributes to \(g_m\). A nonzero contribution must have \(n>b\). Since \(\deg B_n\) begins at \(n\) and \(\deg C'\) begins at a nonnegative integer, \(n\le m\). A nonzero summand gives the stated actual supported pair. This proves (6).

As \(b\) tends to infinity, finite \(N^2\) pigeonhole fixes ONE supported pair nonzero infinitely often. Its individual degrees need not occupy every window; the window assertion is for the supported aggregate. In particular gd A is infinite, and the entire ACTUAL positive-graded nonconstant-\(D\) branch of UGA is closed.

**Equivalent contrapositive boundary.** Full supported cutoff \(Z_b\) forces \(D=1\) for every actual positive grading. This is useful only because the nonconstant branch has just been excluded with ordinary witnesses and a stopping window; \(D=1\) itself is not closure.

## 4. Whole arbitrary-size classes, including higher Loewy selfinjective algebras

### 4.1 Actual positive-graded selfinjective algebras

Assume A is also selfinjective. Each indecomposable \(Ae_i\) has one simple socle \(L_{\nu(i)}\), with \(\nu\) a permutation. Let \(w_i\) be the highest nonzero graded degree of \(Ae_i\). That highest component is annihilated by all positive elements, so lies in the simple socle. It is therefore one-dimensional, at exactly row \(\nu(i)\), and every other entry of column \(i\) has degree strictly less than \(w_i\).

In the determinant, the coefficient of \(t^{\sum_iw_i}\) comes from the UNIQUE permutation \(\nu\), with coefficient \(\operatorname{sgn}\nu=\pm1\). Consequently

\[
\deg D=\sum_iw_i.
\tag{7}
\]

If A is not semisimple, this degree is positive. Theorem3.1 closes the WHOLE positive-graded nonsemisimple selfinjective class, at arbitrary finite vertex count and arbitrary Loewy length, over any elementary field. Its explicit window uses \(w=\max_iw_i\), \(u=\sum_iw_i\). The conclusion is one fixed ordinary supported pair, possibly off-diagonal; it is NOT the stronger EVERY-simple diagonal assertion of C108 in its cube-zero class.

No selfinjectivity of an associated graded algebra is presumed. Nongraded selfinjective cases are not closed by this argument.

### 4.2 Actual Koszul algebras

For a Koszul algebra, \(\beta_{xy}(n,d)=0\) unless \(d=n\). If \(D=1\), the inverse \(C^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}C\) is polynomial. Coefficient \(t^n\) in (3) is then \((-1)^n(\beta_{xy}(n,n))\), with no cross-homological cancellation. Thus all simple Betti matrices vanish eventually and gd A is finite. Therefore infinite-gd ACTUAL Koszul algebras have \(D\ne1\), and Theorem3.1 closes this whole class over any field. Section5 below gives a stronger diagonal result and extends it to radical-associated-graded Koszul algebras with an actual ordinary comparison.

### 4.3 Actual positive multigradings

If A has a finite \(\mathbb N^s\)-grading with degree0 equal to S and every radical component of nonzero total degree, use its standard multigraded Cartan determinant. If that integer polynomial is nonconstant, choose positive integer weights separating its finitely many distinct exponent vectors; for example sufficiently large successive powers of one integer distinguish the bounded coordinates. The specialized polynomial remains nonconstant and defines an ACTUAL positive integer grading of A. Theorem3.1 applies. No grading is manufactured on A unless its actual multiplication respects it.

## 5. Exact ordinary associated-graded transfer under degree separation

Let A be elementary finite-dimensional, with a FINITE descending multiplicative filtration by two-sided ideals

\[
F^0A=A,\qquad F^1A=J,\qquad F^{h+1}A=0,\qquad
F^pA\,F^qA\subseteq F^{p+q}A.
\]

Let \(G=\operatorname{gr}_F A\), so \(G_0=S\), and let

\[
W_n=\{d:\operatorname{Tor}^G_n(S,S)_d\ne0\}.
\]

These are the ACTUAL ordinary bigraded Tor groups of G. Assume the exact separation condition

\[
\max W_{n-1}<\min W_n
\tag{8}
\]

whenever both adjacent groups are nonzero. This is a substantive hypothesis, not inferred from finite algebra dimension, nonconstant determinant, graph cycles or quadratic relations.

### Lemma 5.1 — All higher ordinary bar differentials vanish

The normalized S-relative ordinary bar complex for \(\operatorname{Tor}^A(S,S)\) has degree-n term \(J^{\otimes_S n}\). Filter a tensor by the sum of its factors' F-orders. Its degree-n filtration lies between weights \(n\) and \(nh\), so it is finite in each degree. Multiplication preserves or increases weight, and its leading differential is exactly the ordinary bar differential for G. Outside idempotents are retained.

The resulting convergent ordinary spectral sequence has

\[
E^1_{n,d}=\operatorname{Tor}^G_n(S,S)_d,\qquad
d_r:E^r_{n,d}\longrightarrow E^r_{n-1,d+r}\quad(r\ge1).
\tag{9}
\]

Under (8), an outgoing target has weight greater than every possible weight in homological degree \(n-1\); an incoming class starts in \(W_{n+1}\) and reaches still larger weight, above every possible weight in degree n. Thus BOTH incoming and outgoing differentials vanish, on every page. The finite degreewise filtration gives

\[
\operatorname{gr}_F\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_x,L_y)
\simeq\operatorname{Tor}^G_n(R_x,L_y)
\quad\text{as weight-graded vector spaces}.
\tag{10}
\]

In particular ordinary Betti dimensions agree for EVERY pair and EVERY degree. A general weight-splitting is not canonical; (10) is an associated-graded identification, not an invented algebra map \(G\to A\) or an unchecked Tor inflation map.

The physical Peirce support is also exact:

\[
\dim e_yAe_x=\sum_p\dim e_yG_pe_x.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus supported persistence, every chosen-side simple pd label, and global infinity agree under (8). Any established UGA closure for G transfers to A through actual ordinary groups. This is a precise repair of the old associated-graded lifting guard; without (8) or another actual comparison it remains invalid.

### Lemma 5.2 — Ordinary Yoneda generation transfers

Dual ordinary bar cochains carry the finite degreewise filtration and cup product compatible with tensor deconcatenation. The corresponding associated ordinary Ext algebra after the degeneration in (9) is the ordinary graded Ext algebra of G, preserving homological degrees and vertex labels.

If that algebra is generated in homological degrees at most \(d\), lift a finite basis in degrees0 through d to ordinary Ext_A. For any ordinary class, subtract a polynomial in the lifted classes with the same leading filtered symbol; continue through its FINITE degreewise filtration. This terminates and proves generation of ordinary Ext_A in homological degrees at most d. There is no use of singular/Tate Ext, finite-state propagation or an assumed projective-map comparison.

The existing project ordinary word theorem now gives, under diagonal cutoff n>b (in particular under full Z_b),

\[
\operatorname{gd}A\le Nb+(N-1)d.
\tag{12}
\]

The stopping theorem is reused from N-Vertex-Main-Route; it is not a new FG theorem for arbitrary A.

### Corollary 5.3 — Radical-associated-graded Koszul closure, with a diagonal

Take \(F^pA=J^p\) and assume the ACTUAL \(G=\operatorname{gr}_J A\) is Koszul. Then \(W_n=\{n\}\) when nonzero, so (8) holds. The ordinary Ext algebra of G is generated in degree1: in internal weight n its ordinary bar cycles are the intersection of every adjacent quadratic kernel in \((G_1)^{\otimes_S n}\), with NO incoming weight-n boundary. Ordinary degree-one cup words restrict from the whole tensor dual onto the dual of that cycle space. Koszul purity says this is all its degree-n Ext. This is the same genuine lowest-weight cup argument reused in C110, without extending C110's cube-zero core proof.

Lemma5.2 gives ordinary degree-one generation for A. If gd A is infinite, ordinary degree-one words of arbitrarily large length exist. Expanding vertex endpoints, finite vertex repetition gives nonzero self-Ext subproducts of arbitrarily large degree, and then fixes one diagonal by finite pigeonhole. Hence

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gd}A=\infty\Longrightarrow
\exists i:\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_i,L_i)\ne0
\text{ infinitely often}.}
\tag{13}
\]

Under a common diagonal cutoff n>b, (12) with d=1 gives
\(\operatorname{gd}A\le Nb+N-1\). This closes the ENTIRE radical-associated-graded Koszul class at arbitrary size/Loewy length over ANY elementary field. No positive grading of A itself is required. No claim that all quadratic algebras, all finite algebras, or all selfinjective algebras have Koszul radical graded algebra is made.

More generally, under (8) plus proved degree-d generation for Ext_G, (12) applies. Without that generation, (10)–(11) still transfer any already-closed supported-return class, including a nonconstant-Cartan G.
