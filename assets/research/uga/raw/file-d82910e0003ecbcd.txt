# 6. First-Defect Edge Survival Lemma — what is actually true

Assume temporarily that

\[
H_q(K)=0
\qquad
(r<q<2r).
\]

Consider the Künneth spectral sequence for

\[

At total degree \(2r\), the extremal term

\[

}
\]

appears.

Because all positive rows strictly below \(r\) vanish and all rows strictly between \(r\) and \(2r\) are assumed absent, there is no earlier positive-row source capable of killing this extremal tensor term through the obvious low-page channels.

Thus one gets an edge-survival statement:

\[
\boxed{

\text{ contributes a surviving class in }
H_{2r}(K\otimes_S^{\mathbf L}K)

\tag{6.1}

whenever the actual tensor product is nonzero and the stated row gap holds.

This is the legitimate “epsilon of room” output.

# 7. Why edge survival does not bootstrap \(K\)

The crucial failure is:

\[
H_{2r}(K\otimes_S^{\mathbf L}K)\neq0
\]

does **not** imply

\[
H_{2r}(K)\neq0.
\]

The comultiplication

\[
\Delta:K\to K\otimes_S^{\mathbf L}K
\]

is constrained by counit identities and behaves like a split coaction / monomorphism-type map.

It is not a surjection onto all tensor-square homology.

Therefore a class in

\[
M\otimes_SM
\]

may lie entirely outside the image of

\[
\Delta_*H_*(K).
\]

Hence the implication

\[
\boxed{
\text{surviving self-interaction in }K\otimes K
\Longrightarrow
\text{next defect in }K
}
\]

is invalid.

This is the precise failure of the most natural multiplicative-projection bootstrap.
