## 2. An ordinary pd-one Hom window, without a bounded cone

### Lemma 2.1

For a bounded-above complex C whose finite cohomology modules H_i in
degrees -i all have ambient pd<=1, and for any finite LEFT target Z,
there is, for n>=1, a vector-space exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(H_{n-1},Z)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{D(A)}(C,Z[n])
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_A(H_n,Z)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

In particular the right Hom term is bounded by the middle ordinary
derived Hom dimension. The cohomology may occupy infinitely many degrees.

#### Proof with finite ordinary truncations

For the fixed target Z[n], the portion of C in degrees <=-n-1 has
zero maps to Z[n], as does its shift by one, by the ordinary derived
category truncation orthogonality. Therefore only the finite truncation
with cohomology in [-n,0] matters. Its Postnikov connecting maps between
different cohomology degrees lie in Ext^r between cohomology modules
with r>=2; those groups are zero because the source has pd<=1.
Induction splits this FINITE truncation into its shifted cohomology
modules (a choice, not a canonical splitting). Only shifts n and n-1
can map to Z[n], giving (2.1) and its natural edge sequence.

Equivalently the ordinary hyper-Ext spectral sequence has only columns
zero and one and no possible higher differential. For each total degree
it is a finite window. No convergence of an unrestricted infinite
direct sum, singular/Tate comparison or bounded GLOBAL amplitude is
assumed. QED.

Apply this to C_M. For retained q, Hom_A(H_i,L_q)=0 since H_i has only
F factors, so

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(A)}(C_M,L_q[n+1])
\cong\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(H_n,L_q).
\tag{2.2}
\]

For f in F, the literal chain adjunction gives
Hom_{D(A)}(Ae tensor^L_B eM,L_f[j])=0 in EVERY degree: the Hom complex
is Hom_B(Q,eL_f)=0. The counit triangle hence gives

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(M,L_f)
\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D(A)}(C_M,L_f[n]).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Lemma 2.1 consequently implies

\[
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_A(H_n,L_f)
\le\dim\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(M,L_f).
\tag{2.4}
\]

All these are ordinary projective/Ext calculations. No local map is
deleted. In particular (2.1) does NOT imply C_M has bounded cohomology.

## 3. Actual injective kernels provide physical alignment

### Lemma 3.1 — Weighted finite-head inequality

If H is a finite LEFT module supported in F, its pd is <=1. Take
its ACTUAL minimal ordinary resolution

\[
0\longrightarrow P_1\xrightarrow{\iota}P_0
\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

P_0=sum_{f in F}(Ae_f)^{h_f}, with h_f=dim Hom_A(H,L_f). For any
retained q the multiplicity of Ae_q in P_1 is
dim Ext^1_A(H,L_q). Since iota is an actual INJECTION, applying e_u
gives an injection of finite vector spaces. Thus for EVERY vertex u,

\[
D(u,q)\dim\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(H,L_q)
\le\sum_{f\in F}D(u,f)\dim\operatorname{Hom}_A(H,L_f).
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is proved by actual projective-cover multiplicities and exactness.
The cover P_0 may have infinite-pd composition factors. None of them
is discarded and their pds are not inferred from H. The crucial
ordinary fact is the injection P_1->P_0, which fails at the first
kernel map of a longer resolution.

## 4. Same-degree ordinary supported Tor bridge (C094)

### Theorem 4.1 — Arbitrary-size weighted corner comparison

For retained q, any finite LEFT A module M, EVERY vertex u and n>=1,
write beta_q^A(M,n)=dim Tor^A_n(R_q,M). Then

\[
\boxed{
D(u,q)\,\beta_q^B(eM,n)
\le D(u,q)\,\beta_q^A(M,n)
  +\sum_{f\in F}D(u,f)\,\beta_f^A(M,n).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

In particular for retained simple y and u=y,

\[
\boxed{
D(y,q)\,\beta^B_{qy}(n)
\le D(y,q)\,\beta^A_{qy}(n)
  +\sum_{f\in F}D(y,f)\,\beta^A_{fy}(n).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

The counit triangle and chain adjunction give a sequence

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(M,L_q)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^n_B(eM,L_q)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{D(A)}(C_M,L_q[n+1]).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore the middle dimension is at most the first dimension plus
dim Ext^1_A(H_n,L_q), by (2.2). Multiply by D(u,q), use the actual
injection inequality (3.2), then use (2.4) for each f. Ordinary
finite-dimensional Tor–Ext duality gives (4.1), then (4.2). QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — An actual supported persistent B pair returns in A

Suppose e_yAe_q nonzero and beta^B_qy(n)>0 in infinitely many n.
Equation (4.2) forces infinitely many positive supported A contributions
in the finite set of pairs

\[
(R_q,L_y),\quad (R_f,L_y)\ (f\in F,\ D(y,f)>0).
\tag{4.4}
\]

A fixed pair among them has an ordinary persistent Tor tail. The
SOURCE y is unchanged, while the detector may become f. All contributions
occur in the SAME degree n and carry their own physical support.

No algebra projection, ordinary section, corner perfection or finite
Tor sampling is required. This is a full supported ordinary return
mechanism for the entire pd<=1-complement class, not a necessary
condition on a hypothetical counterexample.

### Corollary 4.3 — A uniform supported cutoff with ZERO loss

If every supported A simple pair has Tor zero for n>M, then every
supported B simple pair is zero for n>M as well. Indeed every weighted
A term on the right of (4.2) is then zero. The same M works; an
initial rough M+1 bound is unnecessary. This statement applies to
all source/detector pairs, not one selected local source.

## 5. Global infinity, exact chosen-side labels and automatic descent

Sum (4.1) over ALL vertices u. With c_q=dim Ae_q>0 it gives

\[

\le c_q\beta_q^A(M,n)+\sum_{f\in F}c_f\beta_f^A(M,n).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For a retained simple L_y of finite A pd a, all terms on the right

\[

\tag{5.2}

\[

I_L(B)=I_L(A)\text{ on the retained labels}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

No LEFT infinite label was deleted. LEFT finite/infinite labels are
exactly preserved; retained finite pds cannot increase. Thus any

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gd}B\le\operatorname{gd}A

\tag{5.4}

A\text{ bad}\Longrightarrow B\text{ bad},\qquad
