## 1. Actual projective-type decomposition

Let K be a radical square-zero ideal of the elementary finite algebra A,
and C=A/K. Assume its canonical actual C-bimodule has a direct sum

\[
K=\bigoplus_{i=1}^v K_i
\quad\text{as }C\text{-bimodules},\qquad
{}_C K_i\cong(Ce_i)^{r_i}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Here r_i>=0 and K_i=0 is permitted. The **same actual subspace** K_i
is stable under both C actions. It need not be Ce_i tensor a right
vector module with scalar action; right actions inside its left
endomorphism algebra are allowed.

This is stronger than left projectivity alone. The left-module
Krull--Schmidt decomposition of K need not be right-action stable.
Nor is K_i assumed to equal the raw Peirce row e_iK.

Write the actual left-projective column matrix

\[
Ke_j\cong\bigoplus_i(Ce_i)^{T(i,j)}
\tag{1.2}
\]

and use edges j -> i when T(i,j)>0. The summand K_i supplies
exactly row i: right vertex projections make K_i e_j left-module
direct summands of K_i, so their projective types remain Ce_i.
Every nonzero K_i therefore has a nonzero incoming row.

## 2. Construct an eligible ordinary ideal at a sink

### Lemma 2.1 — Actual sink peeling

Suppose the graph of T is acyclic and K!=0. There is an i with
K_i!=0 and Ke_i=0. Set B=A/K_i. Then K_i is an actual radical
square-zero ideal of A, is left B-projective, and

\[
K_i\otimes_B K_i=0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The remaining kernel B -> C is the actual square-zero bimodule
sum of the K_h, h!=i, with the same stable projective types over C
and the graph obtained by removing row i.

#### Proof

Choose a sink at the end of a nonempty maximal directed path.
It has a nonzero incoming row, so K_i!=0. A sink has no outgoing
edges, so column i of T is zero. Actual projectivity in (1.2)
gives Ke_i=0.

Since K^2=0, the actions of A on each K_i factor through C.
Its actual C-bimodule stability thus makes it an actual A ideal;
it is contained in J(A) and its square is zero.

The quotient B still maps to C with kernel
K^{rem}=sum_{h!=i}K_h. The ordinary projective column has exact
sequence

\[
0\to K^{rem}e_i\to Be_i\to Ce_i\to0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Here K^{rem}e_i=0 by Ke_i=0. Therefore **as actual B modules**
Be_i=Ce_i, with the remaining kernel acting trivially.
The canonical B action on K_i also factors through C. Equation
(1.1) makes K_i a sum of these actual projective Be_i.

Since K_i e_i=0, tensoring its left projective decomposition gives
K_i tensor_B K_i equal to a sum of copies of K_i e_i, hence zero.
This proves every hypothesis of C074 at the **current** quotient
A -> B, without a section or bimodule perfectness.

The direct bimodule sum embeds its other summands into B.
Their C actions and projective types are unchanged, their products
are still zero, and row i alone is removed. QED.

### Theorem 2.2 — Termination and supported ordinary transfer

If T is acyclic, Lemma 2.1 constructs a finite tower

\[
A=A_0\longrightarrow A_1\longrightarrow\cdots
\longrightarrow A_q=C
\tag{2.3}
\]

where q=#{i:K_i!=0}<=v-1. Every step is an actual one-sided-projective,
tensor-square-zero ordinary ideal quotient satisfying C074.
It strictly decreases algebra dimension and the positive number
of remaining types by one.

Consequently C infinite + implies A infinite +. A hypothetical
bad A gives a bad C by an eligible descent at every step.
If gldim C=g is finite, gldim A<=g+q.

#### Proof

Apply Lemma 2.1 to the remaining actual kernel after each quotient.
The graph stays acyclic. Until K^{rem}=0, a maximal nonempty
path has a nonzero sink summand. Removing it decreases q by one;
there is no infinite iteration. An acyclic finite graph has a
source with zero incoming row, so at least one K_i was zero and
q<=v-1.

C074 applies to each actual step by the projectivity and zero
tensor square just proved. Its supported infinity implication
lifts backwards along the tower; its quotient-pd bound <=1
and all-source supported transfer pass a bad algebra forward.
The elementary finite-resolution bound adds at most one per
step, giving gldim A<=g+q. QED.

This is proved eligibility along a specific class of reductions,
not a graph-only inference of ordinary homological return.

## 3. All-size extension closure and a bound

### Theorem 3.1 — Perfectness is unnecessary for this whole extension class

Under (1.1), if C satisfies Universal Gap A, so does A.
No C^e-perfectness of K, bound on its one-sided right pd,
ordinary algebra section or initial tensor-square-zero assumption
is required.

#### Proof

If T has a cycle, C072's actual ordinary projective-column series
(I-tT)^(-1) and ordinary finite-length detection already make
A infinite +.

If T is acyclic, a bad A descends through the actual tower (2.3)
to a bad C. This contradicts the Gap-A property of C. QED.

The class theorem holds for every finite v. It closes higher-index
nonperfect kernels whose projective types are actual stable
bimodule summands. It does not force such a decomposition in every
one-sided-projective kernel, or an eligible ideal in every bad algebra.

### Quantitative supported-vanishing version

Assume all reverse-supported A-Tor vanishes for every n>=M, M>=1.
The cyclic case is impossible. If the terminal C has C061's ordinary

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gldim}A
\le(v-1)(M+2q)+q,\qquad q\le v-1.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The bound is conditional on (1.1), all-source/all-degree vanishing
and the terminal all-vertex sections. No universal finite-window

### Direct right-projective-type version

Alternatively assume a C-bimodule direct sum K=sum_i K_i with
K_i as an actual right C module in add(e_iC). Decompose e_jK
with multiplicities B(j,i) of e_iC and use edges j -> i.
A sink i with incoming edges has e_iK=0 and K_i!=0.
For B=A/K_i, the right projective e_iB maps isomorphically

The direct right version of C074 applies at each step. Removing
column i gives the same finite sink tower, ordinary supported
transfer and numerical bound. No opposite-side infinite-+ theorem
is used.

## 4. Actual higher-index nonperfect nonsplit calibration

Let A_7 have vertices 0,1,2, loop t at 0, arrows a:0 -> 1
and c:1 -> 2, relations t^2=0 and ca=0. Put b=at.
Its exact basis is

\[
e_0,e_1,e_2,t,a,b,c,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and its only nonzero radical product is at=b. It is finite,
connected, seven-dimensional and associative by this quiver
presentation over every field.

Take the actual square-zero ideal K=span{b,c}. The quotient C
is the four-dimensional triangular C_tri from C073 plus the
isolated vertex 2. Its canonical stable decomposition is

\[
K_1=kb=Ce_1\otimes_k R_0,\qquad
K_2=kc=Ce_2\otimes_k R_1,\qquad K_0=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Both left factors Ce_1,Ce_2 are one-dimensional projectives.
The right actions preserve each displayed actual subspace.
The graph is the two-edge chain 0 -> 1 -> 2.

R_0 has the explicit infinite right C resolution by e_0C
with all maps t. Also rad(e_1C)=ka=R_0, so R_1 has infinite
right pd. Hence K has infinite C^e-pd.

The only nonzero two-fold tensor word is K_2 tensor_C K_1,
because R_1 e_1=k. It equals Ce_2 tensor R_0. Every three-fold
word is zero because R_0 e_1=R_0 e_2=0. Actual left projectivity
therefore gives

\[
K^{\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}2}\ne0,\qquad
K^{\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}3}=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The whole ordinary quotient A_7 -> C has no algebra section:
after vertex alignment any lift a+lambda b has product b with
the unique lift of t, contradicting at=0 in C. Thus neither
C072's perfect-kernel isomorphism nor C074 as **one whole-kernel
step** applies.

Sink peeling nevertheless gives

\[
A_7\longrightarrow A_7/(c)
=A_{\rm tri}\times k e_2
\longrightarrow C_{\rm tri}\times k e_2=C.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The first kernel kc is projective on the left over the actual
six-dimensional intermediate quotient: its projective column
at vertex 2 is k e_2. Its tensor square is zero. The second
kernel kb is the actual nonperfect left-projective tensor-square-zero
kernel from C073. These are exactly the eligible steps of Lemma 2.1.

The original algebra has supported vertex-0 diagonal ordinary
Tor in every degree: a relative bar with both endpoints 0 can
contain only t letters and has zero differential. It is infinite +,
not a counterexample. All assertions above use literal basis
and module/tensor certificates; the unchanged triangular computation
is reused and no new bounded computation is needed.

### 4.2 Actual acyclic columns do not imply the bimodule decomposition

For a negative audit, take C to be the seven-dimensional
radical-square-zero algebra with vertices 0,1,2 and arrows
a:0 -> 1, b:1 -> 0, r:0 -> 2, s:2 -> 0.
Let

\[
{}_C K=X\oplus Y,\quad X=Ce_0,\quad Y=Ce_1,
\]

with bases x_0,x_a,x_r and y_1,y_b respectively. Give X right
vertex 2 and Y right vertex 0. Define the nonzero radical right
actions

\[
x_0 r=y_b,\qquad y_1 s=x_a.
\tag{4.5}
\]

They are C-left-linear: x_a r=x_r r=0 and y_b s=0 follow
from C radical-square-zero. Their composites are zero, so they
define an actual C-bimodule. The projective column graph is
the acyclic chain 2 -> 0 -> 1. Actual K^{tensor_C^L 2}
has column 2 equal to Ce_1 and is nonzero, while its third
tensor power is zero.

There is no stable bimodule decomposition (1.1). The right
vertex columns are the indecomposable left projectives
Ke_2=X=Ce_0 and Ke_0=Y=Ce_1. A bimodule projector onto the
Ce_0 type must be identity on X and zero on Y, since these
distinct columns each have only their stated indecomposable
projective type. But it cannot commute with x_0 r=y_b.

K is also nonperfect. As a right C module its vertex dimensions
are (2,0,3). A right projective with zero vertex-1 component
can only be a sum of e_2C, whose vertex-0 and vertex-2 dimensions
are equal; K is therefore nonprojective. All right C simples
have infinite pd, by the literal radical-square-zero two-cycle
bar returns. A nonprojective right module has a nonzero
semisimple first syzygy because J(C)^2=0. Hence K has infinite
right pd and infinite C^e-pd.

This is realizable by an ordinary **nonsplit** twelve-dimensional
algebra A=C plus K. In addition to the C actions and (4.5), set
the product of two C radical lifts to be zero except

\[
s r=y_b.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The complete nonzero radical products are
a x_0=x_a, r x_0=x_r, b y_1=y_b,
x_0 r=y_b, y_1 s=x_a, and s r=y_b.
All outputs are annihilated by every radical letter on both sides;
thus all triple radical products are zero, and this endpoint-consistent
table is associative over every field. K is its actual square-zero
radical ideal with the displayed canonical quotient actions.

There is no ordinary section A -> C. After vertex alignment, r
has its unique lift, while s has lifts s+lambda x_0.
The quotient relation rs=0 would require lambda=0 because
r(s+lambda x_0)=lambda x_r. The relation sr=0 would require
lambda=-1 because (s+lambda x_0)r=(1+lambda)y_b.
These cannot both hold over any field.

This example invalidates automatic projective-type splitting,
not Universal Gap A. Indeed A has a different ordinary retract
to the radical-square-zero a,b two-cycle plus isolated vertex 2:
kill r,s,K and keep a,b and all vertices. Its section keeps
that same subalgebra, whose products ab and ba are zero in A.
The ordinary bar retract gives the supported 0 diagonal in
every even degree. Thus A is already infinite +, and no global
irreducibility claim is made.

Both calibrations use explicit finite tables and module/tensor
certificates, not extrapolation from computations or graph cycles.

   factor through C. This ideal claim is proved before taking a quotient.
3. Choose a sink with an incoming edge, so K_i is nonzero.
   A merely isolated zero component does not decrease anything.
4. The zero outgoing column is a statement about actual Ke_i=0.
   It is not a resolution-term or Peirce-cycle surrogate.

   The isomorphism Be_i=Ce_i in (2.2) proves it; old quotient
   projectivity is not assumed to inherit automatically.

   not the original C. Its projective decomposition over B and
   actual K_i e_i=0 supply the calculation.
7. Row removal preserves the **actual** remaining C-bimodule summands.
   Both dimension and the number of remaining nonzero types decrease.
8. Cyclic closure uses the already-proved ordinary column formula;
   acyclic closure uses C074 and ordinary supported detection at
   every step. No singular, stable or opposite-+ transfer is substituted.
9. The persistent simple pair may change at each supported bridge.
   The all-source condition and threshold M+2q are retained.
10. The right argument uses actual right projective columns and the
    direct right C074 bridge. It is not a silent opposite transfer.
11. The calibration is higher-index, nonperfect and nonsplit as a whole,

12. This is an all-size class closure with proved repeated eligibility.
    General stable-type decomposition and general ideal existence
