# 19. New theorem — opposite cross module is necessarily singular

## Theorem 19.1 — Opposite Cross-Singularity Necessity

Assume:

\[
e=\sum_{\operatorname{pd}_D L_x=\infty}e_x,
\qquad
f=1-e,
\qquad
\Gamma=fDf,
\]

and:

\[
\operatorname{gldim}\Gamma=\infty.
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{pd}_\Gamma({}_\Gamma fDe)=\infty.
}
\tag{19.1}
\]

### Proof

Assume instead:

\[
\operatorname{pd}_\Gamma(fDe)<\infty.
\]

Take any \(j\in f\).

By definition of \(f\):

\[
\operatorname{pd}_D L_j<\infty.
\]

Choose a finite \(D\)-projective resolution of \(L_j\).

Every indecomposable term is some \(De_v\).

Apply the exact restriction functor \(f(-)\).

If \(v\in f\), then:

\[
fDe_v
=
\Gamma e_v
\]

is projective over \(\Gamma\).

If \(v\in e\), then:

\[
fDe_v
\]

is a direct summand of \(fDe\), hence has finite \(\Gamma\)-projective dimension by assumption.

Therefore the restricted finite resolution is a bounded complex of finite-pd \(\Gamma\)-modules resolving

\[
fL_j
=
S_j^\Gamma.
\]

Replacing each term by a finite projective resolution and totalizing yields:

\[
\operatorname{pd}_\Gamma S_j^\Gamma<\infty.
\]

This holds for every simple \(S_j^\Gamma\).

Since \(\Gamma\) is finite-dimensional with finitely many simples:

\[
\operatorname{gldim}\Gamma<\infty,
\]

contradiction.

\(\square\)
