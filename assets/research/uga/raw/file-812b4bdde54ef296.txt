### Theorem 1.1 — Ordinary supported return and strict descent

If retained y,z have e_yAe_z nonzero and Tor^B_n(R_z,L_y) nonzero for
infinitely many n, then A has a supported persistent ordinary pair with
the SAME LEFT source y: either (z,y) itself, or (f,y) for some f in F
with e_yAe_f nonzero. Degrees can shift by a bounded amount; a same-pair
high isomorphism is not asserted.

If A's ALL reverse-supported ordinary simple Tor vanish for n>M,
then B's supported ordinary simple Tor vanish for

\[
n>M+t.
\tag{1.4}
\]

LEFT infinite labels are EXACTLY restricted. For every finite LEFT M,
writing b=pd_B(eM), m=pd_A M when finite,

\[
b\le m+t,\qquad m\le b+d+1.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Finiteness of either implies finiteness of the other. Consequently
gd(B)<=gd(A)+t and gd(A)<=gd(B)+d+1 whenever finite, and gd(A) is
infinite iff gd(B) is infinite. Badness therefore descends from A to B,
with strict algebra-dimension and vertex decrease. The chosen LEFT
infinite count stays the same; RIGHT labels/counts may change.

For a checked tower, supported cutoff is at most the original M plus
the sum of its t's. Dimension prohibits infinitely many eligible moves.
Eligibility of later blocks must be checked; no arbitrary primitive

The direct RIGHT theorem uses right finite pds, the ordinary right Yoneda

It preserves the RIGHT source (the original Tor detector z), and may
