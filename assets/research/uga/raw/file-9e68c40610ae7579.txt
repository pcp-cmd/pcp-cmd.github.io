# Run — Projective columns give actual returns or a uniform screening bound

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Researcher:** one main Codex researcher  
**Evidence:** complete all-degree proofs and normal adversarial self-audit; not independently verified  
**Global status:** Universal Gap A remains OPEN. No novelty claim is made.

This continuation starts from T6. It changes the test module from an
individual quotient simple to an ordinary projective column of C.
An exact module triangle then computes its actual ordinary A-Tor.
For a one-sided projective two-stage coupler, a finite matrix of
actual projective multiplicities determines all these groups.

A cycle in this matrix produces a supported ordinary Tor tail,
even for a nonperfect C-bimodule. If the matrix is acyclic, the
derived tensor powers really vanish, bounding the screening page
uniformly in homological degree. Combined with finite C^e-pd,
this gives eventual surjectivity from A-Tor to C-Tor without
any regular injective bound. These are two complementary
closure mechanisms, not additional counterexample invariants.

## 1. Two-stage model and the ordinary test modules

For the actual local-trivial deletion e_xAe_x=k, reuse the
strictly augmented minimal retained model

\[
H=C\oplus M[1],\qquad C=A/Ae_xA,\quad M=H^{-1}(q).
\]

The only higher operation is the possible pure-input m_3=kappa;
negative products and mixed higher operations vanish by degree.
No assumption on kappa is made. Let q_0 be the number of retained
vertices. It is finite; all matrix sizes below are q_0.

Assume first that M is projective as a **left C-module**.
For each retained j, decompose the actual module column

\[
M e_j\cong\bigoplus_i(Ce_i)^{K_{ij}},
\qquad K_{ij}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

These are multiplicities of M itself, not of the terms in a
projective resolution of a nonprojective coefficient.
M e_j is a direct summand of the left-projective M, so (1.1)
is available. There is no right-projectivity or C^e-perfectness
assumption at this stage.

View Ce_j and M e_j as left H-modules through p:H -> C.
Degree forces the regular module's kernel M[1] to have exactly
this induced ordinary C action: m_2 is the C action, and every
higher action with a negative last argument has zero output.
Inclusion and projection therefore give a strict short exact
sequence of augmented A_infinity H-modules

\[
0\longrightarrow(M e_j)[1]\longrightarrow H e_j
\longrightarrow C e_j\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Its underlying graded sequence is split. It gives the actual
derived module triangle

\[
(M e_j)[1]\longrightarrow H e_j\longrightarrow C e_j
\longrightarrow(M e_j)[2].
\tag{1.3}
\]

The nonzero kappa is included in the regular H action and in
this extension. No algebra section or formality is being
substituted for the sequence. The standard strict-module
triangle conventions are those of
[Keller, Sections 4.2 and 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9910179).

For retained R_z, the established localisation comparison gives

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^H_n(R_z,Ce_j)
\cong\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,Ce_j),
\tag{1.4}
\]

where Ce_j on the right is inflated through the ordinary
quotient. This is ordinary Tor with an actual finite module.
Ce_j is not asserted to be A-projective.

## 2. Exact ordinary Tor recurrence

### Theorem 2.1 — Matrix powers compute the projective-column Tor

For all r>=0 and all retained z,j,

\[
\boxed{
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_{2r}(R_z,Ce_j)=(K^r)_{zj},\qquad
\operatorname{Tor}^A_{2r+1}(R_z,Ce_j)=0.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Tensor (1.3) with R_z over H. The term
R_z tensor_H^L H e_j is R_z e_j in degree zero.
Connectivity gives Tor_0(R_z,Ce_j)=k when z=j and zero otherwise,
and the long exact sequence gives Tor_1(R_z,Ce_j)=0.
For every n>=2 its remaining part is an actual isomorphism

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^H_n(R_z,Ce_j)
\cong\operatorname{Tor}^H_{n-2}(R_z,M e_j).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Use the ordinary C-linear isomorphism (1.1), which is also an
H-module isomorphism through p. Thus the matrices T_n of these
dimensions satisfy T_0=I, T_1=0, T_n=T_{n-2}K. Induction and
(1.4) prove (2.1). QED.

### Theorem 2.2 — A cycle gives a supported ordinary tail

If the directed graph of K has a directed cycle, A is infinite +,
without requiring C infinite + or an infinite-pd deleted source.

#### Proof

Choose a vertex j on a cycle of length ell. Then
(K^{r ell})_{jj}>0 for every r>=1. By the already proved
ordinary Tor formula, Tor^A_{2r ell}(R_j,Ce_j) is nonzero.

The finite composition factors of Ce_j have multiplicities
[Ce_j:L_y]=dim e_yCe_j. The ordinary finite-length Tor inequality
therefore gives

\[
0<(K^{r\ell})_{jj}
\le\sum_y\dim(e_yCe_j)\,
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_{2r\ell}(R_j,L_y).
\tag{2.3}
\]

There are finitely many y. One fixed y occurs in infinitely
many of these degrees, with e_yCe_j!=0. This also gives
e_yAe_j!=0. Hence (R_j,L_y) is an ordinary supported
persistent pair. QED.

The graph is used only after the chain-level triangle has
computed the actual Tor groups. A support cycle for an
arbitrary nonprojective coupler does not satisfy this proof.

### Corollary 2.3 — Invertible couplers cannot hide all ordinary returns

If M is a Morita self-equivalence C-bimodule, its action permutes
the indecomposable projectives. K is a permutation matrix.
Theorem 2.2 makes A infinite +, independent of kappa and
regardless of whether M has finite C^e-pd.

This closes a nonperfect-coupler class as well. It includes
twisted regular bimodules; freeness on each side was insufficient
for restoring the quotient-edge image in T5, but the new
ordinary projective-column returns are present.

## 3. The complementary case: actual tensor nilpotence

If A is not infinite + and M is left-projective, Theorem 2.2
forces K to be acyclic. A q_0-vertex acyclic graph has no
length-q_0 path, so K^{q_0}=0.

### Lemma 3.1 — Matrix nilpotence is actual derived tensor nilpotence here

For every p>=1, the ordinary tensor power satisfies

\[
(M^{\otimes_C p})e_j
\cong\bigoplus_i(Ce_i)^{(K^p)_{ij}}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

It remains left-projective, and it also represents the derived
tensor power. In particular

\[
\boxed{M^{\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}q_0}=0\quad\text{if }K\text{ is acyclic}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Tensor (1.1) on the left with M. Since
M tensor_C Ce_i=M e_i, the resulting projective multiplicities
are K^2. Iterate to get (3.1). At each step the factor on the
right is left C-projective, so no higher tensor homology
appears. This proves the derived assertion and (3.2). QED.

This is not a nilpotence claim based on the correspondence
types in a finite resolution. One-sided projectivity of the
actual coupler is essential for the exact recurrence.

### Theorem 3.2 — The screening page is uniformly bounded

If K is acyclic, every ordinary quotient-edge Tor class which
dies in the existing negative-weight spectral sequence dies
by a page r<=q_0-1, in its convention where m_3 raises weight
by one.

#### Proof

For the binary bar differential b_2, the weight-p column has
homology given by

\[
R_z\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}
M^{\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}p}
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}L_y,
\tag{3.3}
\]

shifted by 2p. This is the existing two-stage multibar
description, with p the number of negative letters.
Lemma 3.1 makes every first-page column p>=q_0 zero.
All subsequent pages retain those zeros. A page-r
differential from weight zero has target weight r, so it
vanishes when r>=q_0. QED.

The bound is uniform in total homological degree for this
finite algebra. It is a **negative-weight** page bound; no
excursion bound for the local-singular Target-B filtration
is inferred.

Consequently the entire unbounded-weight supported-screening
subbranch is excluded for one-sided projective two-stage
couplers: cycles give actual ordinary returns, and the
acyclic case has the uniform bound above. The nonperfect
acyclic bounded-weight case is still open.

## 4. Combining the bound with the existing screening corridor

Assume additionally D=pd_{C^e} M<infinity.

### Lemma 4.1 — Positive columns have a finite degree corridor

For p>=1, the homology in (3.3) is concentrated in degrees
0<=h<=pD before its 2p shift.

#### Proof

Take a length-D projective C-bimodule resolution P of M.
Each tensor product of elementary projective bimodules is
again projective, by the elementary factorisation used in T5.
The bounded complex P^{tensor_C p} therefore represents the
derived tensor power, with projective length at most pD.
Lemma 3.1 identifies it with the ordinary M^p.

Tensor its terms with R_z: they become right C-projective.
Tensoring finally with L_y is computed by that same length-pD
complex, giving the asserted range. QED.

This reuses and checks the finite-C^e-pd Screening-Corridor
asset. Finite pd on the two separate sides is not substituted.

### Theorem 4.2 — Eventual ordinary surjectivity in the acyclic case

If K is acyclic, then for every retained pair the natural map

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,L_y)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Tor}^C_n(R_z,L_y)\text{ is surjective for }
n>(D+2)(q_0-1)+1.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

A nonzero page-r death from a weight-zero class of degree n
needs a positive-weight target of total degree n-1=2r+h.
By Theorem 3.2, 1<=r<=q_0-1, and by Lemma 4.1,
0<=h<=rD. Hence every possible death satisfies

\[
n\le(D+2)r+1\le(D+2)(q_0-1)+1.
\]

Above this bound every zero-weight class survives. There
are no incoming differentials to weight zero. Degreewise
finite convergence identifies that edge survival with the
image of the actual bar projection, yielding (4.1).
The localisation comparison is ordinary A-Tor. QED.

### Corollary 4.3 — A complete one-sided-projective perfect branch closes

If M is projective on one side and has finite C^e-pd, then
C infinite + implies A infinite +. No regular injective
dimension bound on C is needed.

For a left-projective M, if K has a cycle use Theorem 2.2.
Otherwise use (4.1) to lift C's fixed supported infinite tail.
Reverse support lifts separately through C.

Thus a globally vertex-minimal counterexample cannot have
such a local-trivial deletion at an infinite-pd vertex,
by C058 and global minimality. This extends the projective
case of C062 and covers perfect couplers outside T6's
one-Gorenstein quotient class.

The assertion for a right-projective M is proved directly
in the next section, without opposite-side transfer.

## 5. Explicit right-module version

If M is right C-projective, write

\[
e_iM\cong\bigoplus_j(e_jC)^{B_{ij}}.
\]

The strict right H-module sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow(e_iM)[1]\longrightarrow e_iH
\longrightarrow e_iC\longrightarrow0
\]

gives, after tensoring with L_y,

\[
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_{2r}(e_iC,L_y)=(B^r)_{iy},
\qquad\operatorname{Tor}^A_{2r+1}(e_iC,L_y)=0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Here T_n=B T_{n-2}, with T_0=I. A diagonal cycle term tests
the actual right module e_iC against L_i. Its right composition
factors R_z have multiplicities dim e_iCe_z. Thus the same
finite-length inequality selects a fixed pair (R_z,L_i)
with e_iCe_z!=0 and infinitely many ordinary Tor groups.

If B is acyclic, all right-projective tensor columns of
M^p have multiplicities B^p. The factor on the left is
right-projective at each tensor step, so M^{tensor^L q_0}=0.
The same multibar argument gives the page bound q_0-1,
and finite C^e-pd gives (4.1).

This calculation uses actual right modules and ordinary Tor.
It does not assume or invoke infinite-+ invariance under
opposite passage.

## 6. Single-negative-layer extension

The triangle argument also applies if the retained model has
exactly C and a single negative layer M[a], a>=1, and no
other negative cohomology. Degree forces all negative
products and mixed higher operations to vanish; the only
possible pure operation is m_{a+2}.

The module triangle is M e_j[a] -> H e_j -> Ce_j
-> M e_j[a+1]. For left-projective M it gives

\[
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_{(a+1)r}(R_z,Ce_j)=(K^r)_{zj},
\]

with all other nonnegative degrees zero. Cycles still
give supported ordinary tails.

For acyclic K, the first page has at most q_0-1 negative
letters. In the original negative-weight convention
wt=a times this count, a death page has weight shift
at most a(q_0-1). If D=pd_{C^e} M is finite, its positive
target has total degree (a+1)p+h with h<=pD. Thus the
ordinary comparison is surjective for

\[
n>(a+1+D)(q_0-1)+1.
\]

This retains the single-layer hypothesis. A general
many-layer model has mixed operations and no such strict
kernel module of one degree; the same recurrence is not
asserted for it.

## 10. Continuation: an ordinary bridge for unbounded odd negative layers

The cycle mechanism extends to an actual many-layer case. This
extension does not inherit the preceding single-layer page bound.

Assume every positive even negative-cohomology group is zero,
and every M_d=H^{-d}(q) for odd d is left C-projective. There
is no bound on d, homogeneous generation, operation arity or
the mixed higher operations. Write the actual columns as

\[
M_d e_j\cong\bigoplus_i(Ce_i)^{K_d(i,j)}
\qquad(d\text{ odd}).
\]

### Theorem 10.1 — Full ordinary Tor formula with projective test columns

For every retained z,j, the formal matrix Poincare series is

\[
\boxed{
\left(\sum_{n\ge0}
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,Ce_j)t^n\right)_{zj}
=\left(I-\sum_{\substack{d\ge1\\d\ {\rm odd}}}
t^{d+1}K_d\right)^{-1}.}
\tag{10.1}
\]

All odd-degree groups in this formula are zero. The inverse
is interpreted coefficientwise as sum of matrix powers;
only finitely many factors and degrees contribute to any
fixed coefficient. No rationality assertion is made.

#### Proof

Use the existing negative-weight filtration on the actual
bar module computing R_z tensor_H^L Ce_j. Its left test
module Ce_j is pulled back through the strict projection
p and has no higher module actions. Its binary first page
decomposes by the sequence d_1,...,d_p of negative letters.
Negative-negative binary products vanish by the odd-only
cohomology hypothesis. The associated complex is

\[
R_z\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}M_{d_1}
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}\cdots
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}M_{d_p}
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}Ce_j,
\tag{10.2}
\]

shifted by sum(d_i+1). The weight-zero term is simply
R_z e_j in degree zero.

Every factor M_d on the right at the next tensor step
is left-projective, as is the final Ce_j. Thus (10.2)
is its ordinary tensor in degree zero. Decomposing the
actual projective columns shows its dimension is

\[
(K_{d_1}\cdots K_{d_p})_{zj}.
\]

Every shift sum(d_i+1) is even. The entire first page
for this projective-column test complex therefore has
only even total degrees. Each later differential lowers
total degree by one, so every such differential is zero.
The negative-weight filtration is finite in every total
degree, giving actual homology rather than a merely formal
associated-graded claim.

Summing the dimensions over all finite sequences and
using the established ordinary localisation comparison
proves (10.1). QED.

Only the bar complexes with these C-projective test
modules degenerate here. The quotient-simple screening
spectral sequence need not degenerate. This distinction
permits the non-formal mixed operations in the hypothesis.

### Corollary 10.2 — Weighted cycles force ordinary supported returns

Make a finite graph whose edge j -> i is present if
K_d(i,j)>0 for at least one odd d. If it has a directed
cycle, A is infinite +.

#### Proof

For each edge on a fixed cycle choose one actual odd
degree d. Repeating that cycle supplies a positive
diagonal term in (10.1) at every positive multiple of
the fixed even degree L=sum(d+1) along the cycle.
Hence Tor^A_{rL}(R_j,Ce_j) is nonzero for all r>=1.
Apply the ordinary finite-length inequality (2.3) to
select a fixed composition factor L_y with e_yCe_j!=0
and a persistent ordinary pair (R_j,L_y). QED.

This closes a stated **many-layer, unbounded-cohomology**
class, including nonperfect C-bimodule layers. Pure and
mixed higher operations cannot screen its projective-column
returns because the first-page concentration was proved
for the actual complex.

### Direct right-projective version

If instead every odd M_d is right C-projective, decompose

This is a right-module calculation, not opposite transfer.
