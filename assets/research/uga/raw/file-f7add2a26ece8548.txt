# §0 Mathematical verdict

**Not solved.** No verified fact, proof, or counterexample exists in this project; the central blocker is the full converse from eventual Hochschild-homology vanishing to finite global dimension.

**Closed components.** None: the fact graph is empty.

**Viable proof architecture.** Run two symmetric routes without assuming either outcome: derive homological smoothness from eventual vanishing, or construct a finite-dimensional complex algebra for which eventual vanishing coexists with a nonzero singularity category.

**Main blocker.** There is no verified bridge from the Hochschild homology of the diagonal bimodule to the projective dimensions of all simple modules, and there is no verified counterexample satisfying every hypothesis.

**Highest-priority unresolved bridge.** Decide, with a complete proof or a fully checked construction, whether a non-perfect diagonal/singularity category can be invisible to Hochschild homology in all sufficiently high degrees.

**Method failure vs. proposition failure.** There is presently evidence for neither method failure nor proposition failure; both positive and negative routes remain UNKNOWN.

**Calibration caveat.** Do not read UNKNOWN as evidence for either truth value; it records only the empty verified state.

**Current best proof skeleton.**

1. Fix the standard finite-dimensional complex-algebra formulation and its quantifiers.
2. Express finite global dimension through projective dimensions of simple modules and/or perfection of the diagonal.
3. Express eventual Hochschild-homology vanishing through an appropriate Hochschild chain model.
4. Identify the exact information lost when diagonal homology is passed to Hochschild homology.
5. Either prove that this loss cannot hide a non-perfect diagonal in the finite-dimensional complex setting,
6. or construct an algebra in which it does hide one.
7. Verify all finiteness, field, grading, and degree hypotheses independently.
8. Submit the resulting implication or counterexample through the independent verifier.

**Central missing lemma.** For every finite-dimensional complex algebra \(A\), determine whether the condition that there exists \(N\) with \(HH_n(A)=0\) for every \(n\ge N\) forces every finite-dimensional simple right \(A\)-module to have finite projective dimension. This is non-trivial because Hochschild homology is an invariant of the diagonal bimodule after a trace-like operation and need not visibly retain every module-theoretic obstruction.

# §2 Interface contract table

### Exact formulation → research target — make every hypothesis type-check

**Input required.** An ordinary finite-dimensional associative unital algebra over \(\mathbb C\), standard Hochschild homology, and eventual vanishing in all degrees beyond one bound.

**Output claimed.** A precise statement whose positive conclusion is finite left/right global dimension and whose negative resolution is one algebra satisfying all input hypotheses with infinite global dimension.

**Available facts.** None.

**Missing verification on the actual model.** (1) Fix conventions; (2) verify equivalence of the selected global-dimension formulations under these hypotheses; (3) distinguish ordinary from dg or topological variants.

**Failure mode if ignored.** A construction outside the ordinary finite-dimensional complex setting, or a proof using a stronger vanishing hypothesis, would not decide the stated conjecture.

**Status.** UNKNOWN

### Eventual vanishing → module-theoretic finiteness — positive route

**Input required.** A finite-dimensional complex algebra \(A\) and a bound \(N\) such that \(HH_n(A)=0\) for all \(n\ge N\).

**Output claimed.** Every simple \(A\)-module has finite projective dimension, hence \(\operatorname{gldim}(A)<\infty\).

**Available facts.** None.

**Missing verification on the actual model.** (1) A bridge from trace-level homology to the diagonal resolution; (2) a bridge from the diagonal resolution to all simples; (3) uniform finiteness.

**Failure mode if ignored.** Vanishing of a derived invariant may be mistaken for vanishing of the category or object from which it was computed.

**Status.** UNKNOWN

### Candidate algebra → verified counterexample — negative route

**Input required.** An explicitly specified ordinary finite-dimensional complex algebra \(A\).

**Output claimed.** A complete computation or theorem application proving \(HH_n(A)=0\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), together with a complete proof that \(\operatorname{gldim}(A)=\infty\).

**Available facts.** None.

**Missing verification on the actual model.** (1) Finite-dimensionality; (2) ordinary-algebra status; (3) the full required range of Hochschild vanishing; (4) an infinite-global-dimension witness; (5) compatibility of every imported theorem.

**Failure mode if ignored.** A dg, infinite-dimensional, topological, or only partially vanishing example would be irrelevant; an unverified categorical equivalence would leave the construction incomplete.

**Status.** UNKNOWN

# §3 Dangerous heuristic lines and strategies not to pursue

- “Eventual Hochschild-homology vanishing means the diagonal bimodule is perfect.” **Status: Not automatic.** This is precisely the unproved converse mechanism; no signed fact currently supports it.
- “A nonzero singularity category must have nonzero Hochschild homology in arbitrarily high degrees.” **Status: Conditional only.** A trace-type invariant can in principle fail to detect a nonzero category; no detection theorem is verified here.
- “Enough positive special cases prove the general conjecture.” **Status: Mathematically incorrect as stated.** Special families can guide a proof but cannot exclude a counterexample outside them.
- “A plausible categorical construction is already a counterexample.” **Status: Method shortcut, not a proof step.** Every equivalence and every finiteness/vanishing property must be checked on the final ordinary algebra.

**Strategies not to pursue.**

- Do not infer the answer from the title or abstract of any paper: reproduce the proof through verifier-checkable claims.
- Do not use dg or topological counterexamples without an explicit verified passage to an ordinary finite-dimensional complex algebra.
- Do not confuse Hochschild cohomology counterexamples with the homology conjecture.
- Do not accept finite total dimension, boundedness, and eventual vanishing as interchangeable without checking finite-dimensionality in each degree.
- Do not reveal direct-resolution literature to Phase-I workers before their independent record is frozen.

# §4 Missing bridge lemmas

### Bridge 1 — trace detection or its failure

**Target statement.** For finite-dimensional complex algebras, either prove that eventual vanishing of Hochschild homology detects perfection of the diagonal, or construct and verify a non-perfect diagonal whose Hochschild homology vanishes eventually.

**Prerequisites.** Precise chain/categorical models and all finiteness hypotheses.

**Existing facts to use.** None.

**Missing checks.** (1) Identify a valid detection theorem, or (2) build an explicit failure and verify that it descends to the required algebraic setting.

**Closure criterion.** A complete implication proof closes the positive route; a fully verified finite-dimensional ordinary algebra closes the negative route. This bridge is independent and parallelizable in its two directions.

### Bridge 2 — infinite global dimension witness

**Target statement.** For any candidate negative example \(A\), exhibit a simple module of infinite projective dimension or an equivalent fully verified obstruction to finite global dimension.

**Prerequisites.** An explicit finite-dimensional complex algebra and a valid module/categorical model.

**Existing facts to use.** None.

**Missing checks.** Construct the witness and prove it survives every equivalence or quotient used in the candidate construction.

**Closure criterion.** The bridge closes only when infinite global dimension follows by a complete argument on the final algebra. It depends on a candidate example but can be adversarially checked in parallel.

### Bridge 3 — eventual vanishing computation

**Target statement.** For any candidate negative example \(A\), prove that one bound \(N\) satisfies \(HH_n(A)=0\) for every \(n\ge N\).

**Prerequisites.** A valid Hochschild complex or an applicable invariance/localization theorem with all hypotheses matched.

**Existing facts to use.** None.

**Missing checks.** Control all degrees beyond one bound and rule out a merely sparse vanishing pattern.

**Closure criterion.** The bridge closes only with an all-high-degree computation or a theorem whose hypotheses are verified on the final algebra. It depends on a candidate example and is parallelizable with Bridge 2.
