# Universal Gap A — Universal Two-Sided Finite-Head Collision
## Removal of the eventual-periodicity hypothesis

**Date:** 2026-10-02  
**Status:** PROJECT-DERIVED THEOREM / READY FOR INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION  
**Scope:** elementary finite-dimensional algebras; no eventual periodicity assumed.

---

# 0. Main result

Let \(A=kQ/I\) be an elementary finite-dimensional algebra which is **not** of infinite \(+\) global dimension.

Fix a vertex \(x\) whose local corner

\[
\Gamma_x:=e_xAe_x
\]

is nontrivial:

\[
\Gamma_x\neq k.
\]

Then there exist vertices \(y,z\neq x\) such that

\[
e_zAe_x\neq0,\qquad e_xAe_y\neq0
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tor}^{\Gamma_x}_m
\bigl(e_zAe_x,\ e_xAe_y\bigr)\neq0
\quad\text{for infinitely many }m.
}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tor}^{\Gamma_x}_m(Ae_x,e_xA)\neq0
\quad\text{for infinitely many }m.
}
\]

Thus every nontrivial local corner of a hypothetical Gap-A failure is
**infinitely non-stratifying**.

No eventual bimodule periodicity is used.

# 1. Literature inputs

We use only the following external facts.

## 1.1 CLMS definition of infinite \(+\)

Cibils–Lanzilotta–Marcos–Solotar define \(A\) to have infinite \(+\) global dimension when there exist vertices \((x,y)\) such that

\[
yAx\neq0
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_*(k_x,{}_yk)
\]

is nonzero in infinitely many degrees.

They prove that this is equivalent to infinite \(\tau\)-Hochschild homology.

## 1.2 Chain-space / syzygy criterion

For an elementary algebra, write \(C\) for the Cartan matrix and \(C^{(n)}\) for the Ext matrix. The \(n\)-th minimal Hochschild chain-space dimension is

\[
b_n(A)=\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(C^{(n)}C\bigr).
\]

Armenta's Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
A\text{ infinite }+
\iff
b_n(A)\neq0\text{ for infinitely many }n.
\]

## 1.3 One-way law

If \(A\) is not infinite \(+\), there is \(N\) such that for every vertex \(x\) and every \(n\ge N\),

\[
T_n(x)\subseteq Z(x),
\]

where

\[
T_n(x)=\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{top}\Omega_A^n(S_x),
\qquad
Z(x)=\{y:e_xAe_y=0\}.
\]

Equivalently, for every \(n\ge N\),

\[
e_xP_n(S_x)=0.
\]

# 2. Opposite-invariance of infinite \(+\)

This is proved directly from the chain-space formula, without using Armenta Proposition 4.7.

For the opposite algebra:

\[
C(A^{op})=C(A)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Duality between finite-dimensional left and right modules gives

\[
C^{(n)}(A^{op})
=
C^{(n)}(A)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_n(A^{op})
&=
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
C^{(n)}(A)^{\mathsf T}
C(A)^{\mathsf T}
\right)\\
&=
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
(C(A)C^{(n)}(A))^{\mathsf T}
\right)\\
&=
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
C(A)C^{(n)}(A)
\right)\\
&=
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
C^{(n)}(A)C(A)
\right)\\
&=
b_n(A).
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
A\text{ infinite }+
\iff
A^{op}\text{ infinite }+.
}
\]

In particular,

\[
A\text{ not infinite }+
\Longrightarrow
A^{op}\text{ not infinite }+.
\]

This permits the one-way law to be applied independently to the left and right simple resolutions at the same vertex.

### Source discrepancy

Armenta v1 Proposition 4.7 instead states

\[
A\text{ infinite co}+
\iff
A^{op}\text{ infinite }+.
\]

Its displayed Tor-flip formula should be rechecked against the existential vertex indexing in CLMS Definition 5.4.

The present theorem does **not** use Proposition 4.7; it uses the independent chain-space calculation above.

# 3. Left finite head at a nontrivial local corner

Fix \(x\), and put

\[
\Gamma=e_xAe_x\neq k.
\]

Take the minimal left \(A\)-projective resolution

\[
\cdots\to P_2\to P_1\to P_0\to S_x\to0.
\]

Since \(A\) is not infinite \(+\), the one-way law gives some \(N_L\) such that

\[
e_xP_n=0
\qquad(n\ge N_L).
\]

The functor

\[
e_x(-):A\text{-mod}\to\Gamma\text{-mod}
\]

is exact. Applying it to the resolution gives a finite exact sequence

\[
0\to L_{N_L-1}\to\cdots\to L_1\to L_0\to k_x\to0,
\]

where

\[
L_i:=e_xP_i.
\]

If

\[
P_i\simeq\bigoplus_y(Ae_y)^{\beta_{iy}},
\]

then

\[
L_i\simeq
\bigoplus_y(e_xAe_y)^{\beta_{iy}}
\]

as left \(\Gamma\)-modules.

Thus

\[
k_x\in
\operatorname{thick}_{D(\Gamma)}
\{L_0,\ldots,L_{N_L-1}\}.
\]

---

# 4. Right finite head

By Section 2,

\[
A^{op}\text{ is not infinite }+.
\]

Apply the one-way law to the simple at \(x\) over \(A^{op}\), equivalently to the simple right \(A\)-module \(k_x\).

Let

\[
\cdots\to Q_2\to Q_1\to Q_0\to k_x\to0
\]

be its minimal right \(A\)-projective resolution.

There is \(N_R\) such that

\[
Q_n e_x=0
\qquad(n\ge N_R).
\]

Applying the exact functor

\[
(-)e_x:\text{mod-}A\to\text{mod-}\Gamma
\]

gives

\[
0\to R_{N_R-1}\to\cdots\to R_1\to R_0\to k_x\to0,
\]

where

\[
R_j:=Q_je_x.
\]

If

\[
Q_j\simeq\bigoplus_z(e_zA)^{\gamma_{jz}},
\]

then

\[
R_j\simeq
\bigoplus_z(e_zAe_x)^{\gamma_{jz}}
\]

as right \(\Gamma\)-modules.

Hence

\[
k_x\in
\operatorname{thick}_{D(\Gamma^{op})}
\{R_0,\ldots,R_{N_R-1}\}.
\]

---

# 5. Local simple has unbounded self-Tor

Because \(e_x\) is primitive, \(\Gamma\) is a finite-dimensional local algebra.

Since

\[
\Gamma\neq k,
\]

its unique simple \(k_x\) has infinite projective dimension.

Indeed, if the minimal free resolution of \(k_x\) terminated,

\[
0\to\Gamma^{b_d}\to\cdots\to\Gamma^{b_0}\to k_x\to0,
\]

the Euler dimension identity would give

\[
1
=
\left(\sum_i(-1)^ib_i\right)\dim_k\Gamma,
\]

forcing

\[
\dim_k\Gamma=1,
\]

contrary to \(\Gamma\neq k\).

Minimality therefore gives a nonzero free term in every degree, and tensoring with the right simple kills all minimal differentials. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tor}^{\Gamma}_m(k_x,k_x)\neq0
\qquad\forall m\ge0.
}
\]

Thus

\[
k_x\otimes_\Gamma^{\mathbf L}k_x
\]

is unbounded.

---

# 6. Finite-head collision

Assume for contradiction that every pair of head terms has bounded derived tensor:

\[
R_j\otimes_\Gamma^{\mathbf L}L_i
\in D^b(k)
\]

for every

\[
0\le i<N_L,\qquad0\le j<N_R.
\]

Since \(k_x\) is obtained from the finite family \(\{L_i\}\) by finitely many cones, for each fixed \(R_j\),

\[
R_j\otimes_\Gamma^{\mathbf L}k_x
\]

is bounded.

Since \(k_x\) is also obtained from the finite family \(\{R_j\}\) by finitely many cones, it follows that

\[
k_x\otimes_\Gamma^{\mathbf L}k_x
\]

is bounded.

This contradicts Section 5.

Therefore there are \(i,j\) for which

\[
R_j\otimes_\Gamma^{\mathbf L}L_i
\]

has unbounded homology.

Using the finite direct-sum decompositions in Sections 3–4, some fixed primitive summands satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tor}^{\Gamma}_m
(e_zAe_x,e_xAe_y)
\neq0
}
\]

for infinitely many \(m\).

The cases \(y=x\) or \(z=x\) cannot give unbounded Tor because

\[
e_xAe_x=\Gamma
\]

is projective over itself. Hence

\[
y,z\neq x.
\]

This proves the theorem.

# 7. Consequences

## 7.1 Infinite local projective dimensions

The fixed pair satisfies

\[
\operatorname{pd}_{\Gamma}(e_xAe_y)=\infty
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{pd}_{\Gamma^{op}}(e_zAe_x)=\infty.
\]

Otherwise Tor would vanish above the finite projective dimension.

## 7.2 Infinite non-stratification

Since

\[
Ae_x=\bigoplus_z e_zAe_x,
\qquad
e_xA=\bigoplus_y e_xAe_y,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tor}^{e_xAe_x}_m
(Ae_x,e_xA)\neq0
\quad\text{i.o.}
}
\]

for every vertex \(x\) of a non-\(+\) algebra with

\[
e_xAe_x\neq k.
\]

Thus the vertex ideal \(Ae_xA\) is not merely non-stratifying: its derived multiplication defect has unbounded homology.

# 8. What this does and does not solve

This theorem is genuinely universal-scope:

- no bound on the number of vertices;
- no eventual periodicity;
- no Gorenstein hypothesis;
- no finite-generation hypothesis;
- no graph-cycle-to-homology inference.

It says that a hypothetical Gap-A failure has only two structural possibilities at each relevant vertex:

### Local-trivial branch

\[
e_xAe_x=k.
\]

### Local-singular branch

\[
e_xAe_x\neq k
\]

and then a fixed two-sided mixed linker

\[
e_zAe_x
\otimes_{e_xAe_x}^{\mathbf L}
e_xAe_y
\]

has nonzero homology in infinitely many degrees.

This does **not** yet prove Gap A.

The remaining local-singular problem is a genuine derived-necklace/no-hiding problem:

\[
\text{unbounded mixed linker}
\Longrightarrow ?
\text{ordinary Peirce-visible simple return}.
\]

The remaining local-trivial problem is essentially a tiling/support-return problem.

# 9. Route fingerprint

**PROOF_OBJECT:** finite left/right heads obtained by applying \(e_x(-)\) and \((- )e_x\) to minimal simple resolutions.

**MECHANISM_FAMILY:** two-sided local-corner collision via derived tensor.

**QUANTIFIER_STRATEGY:** fixed vertex \(x\), finite head on both sides, then finite direct-sum extraction of fixed \(y,z\).

**ROOT_OBSTRUCTION:** ordinary high syzygies avoid \(x\), while the nontrivial local simple \(k_x\) has infinite self-Tor.

**OLD_ROUTE_DELTA:** removes the old eventual-bimodule-periodicity assumption completely.

**NEW_MONOTONE_OR_RIGIDITY:** finite cone generation of \(k_x\) from both heads plus unbounded local self-Tor.

**CANNOT_IMPLY:** unbounded mixed-link Tor does not by itself imply an ambient simple-simple persistent Tor/Ext pair; Auslander-type screening is a calibration against that inference.

**DUPLICATE_CHECK:** this is the old finite-head collision mechanism with a materially stronger hypothesis set: arbitrary finite-dimensional non-\(+\) algebra rather than the eventual-periodic hard branch.
