## 1. Ordinary data and the exact socle condition

Use I_L, I_R, H_zy, W_z, T_y and D(y,z)=dim e_yAe_z as in T16--T18.
All algebras are finite-dimensional elementary over any field.
F_L is the complement of I_L; its simple pds have a finite common
bound d. A left module with ONLY F_L composition factors has pd<=d,
by ordinary finite-length extensions. This forward implication is
valid; its converse is not used.

The LEFT socle separation condition is

\[
z\in I_R(A)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{soc}({}_AAe_z)
\text{ has composition factors only in }I_L(A).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently, none of these left projectives has a nonzero submodule
whose composition factors are all in F_L. Indeed every nonzero
finite-length F_L-only submodule has a simple F_L socle, which lies
in the socle of Ae_z. Conversely an F_L simple in that socle is
such a submodule. This condition is about composition-factor support,
NOT about excluding every finite-pd submodule: a projective itself
has finite pd and can have infinite-pd composition factors.

The stronger easily stated sufficient condition that the whole LEFT
regular socle has only I_L factors implies (1.1). Neither condition
is inferred from finite dimension or the number three alone.

## 2. Reusable ordinary bridge for one infinite-factor carrier in an actual block

### Lemma 2.1 — An actual recurrent projective block with at most one infinite factor gives visible Tor

Fix an infinite-pd left simple L_x. Consider a connected component
K of the ACTUAL Peirce graph on its persistent projective labels
T_x. Distinct components have projective Hom zero in both directions.
Suppose

\[
Y_K=\{j\in I_L:\text{ some }q\in K\text{ has }D(j,q)>0\}
\quad\text{has size at most one.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then the empty case is impossible, and the singleton case gives a
fixed supported ordinary Tor tail for A.

#### Proof

Reuse the actual-block construction in Peirce-Elimination-Theorem,
Section 3. After the last occurrence of a nonpersistent label, the
left resolution uses only T_x. Its differentials are literally
block diagonal. For s sufficiently large the module

\[
X_K=\operatorname{coker}(P_{s+1}^K\longrightarrow P_s^K)
\]

has the actual exact minimal projective resolution supplied by that
block. Every q in K appears infinitely often in this tail, so
Tor_n(R_q,X_K) persists. Also X_K is a quotient of P_s^K; its infinite-pd
simple composition factors are contained in Y_K.

If Y_K is empty, X_K has pd<=d, contrary to that actual recurrence.
If Y_K={j}, choose q in K with D(j,q)>0. Ordinary finite-length
Tor detection gives, for n>d,

\[
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_q,X_K)
\le [X_K:L_j]\,\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_q,L_j).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Every other factor is in F_L and has zero Tor there. The left side
is nonzero infinitely often because q is recurrent in the ACTUAL
minimal block. Thus the fixed pair (R_q,L_j) persists and D(j,q)>0.
The label q is chosen inside the same recurrent block as its physical
carrier; this is the required alignment. QED.

There is a direct right version with actual right projective blocks,
I_R composition factors, and the unchanged physical pair D(y,z).
No graph cycle has been used to infer recurrence. The graph here
only identifies literal zero Hom blocks of a resolution whose
ordinary recurrence already exists.

## 3. Three-source class closure by actual socle separation

### Theorem 3.1 — Three left infinite simples under (1.1) force visible ordinary Tor

Suppose |I_L(A)|=3 and (1.1) holds. Then A is infinite +.
More generally |I_L|<=2 already satisfies Gap A by C080, with no
socle assumption. Thus the stated class closes at every finite
total vertex number and over every field.

#### Proof

Assume A is bad. C080's ordinary singleton-detector lemma shows
every nonempty W_z has at least two labels. For z in I_R it is
contained in the three-element I_L. No supported infinite Tor
means W_z and the physical infinite-factor support

\[
S_z=\{j\in I_L:D(j,z)>0\}
\tag{3.1}
\]

are disjoint. Therefore |S_z|<=1. Condition (1.1) ensures S_z is
NONEMPTY: Ae_z is a nonzero finite-length projective, so its socle
is nonzero and has a factor in I_L. Write S_z={c(z)}. All of its
infinite-pd left simple factors have that one label c(z).

Now take z,w in I_R with c(z)!=c(w). For an actual A-linear map
f:Ae_z->Ae_w, all I_L components of im f vanish: at c(z) the target
component is zero, and at every other I_L vertex the source component
is zero. Its image has only F_L composition factors. It is a
submodule of Ae_w, so (1.1) forces im f=0. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_A(Ae_z,Ae_w)=0
\quad\text{whenever }c(z)\ne c(w),\qquad z,w\in I_R.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This holds in BOTH directions because the condition holds for each
target. These are actual zero maps, not maps merely zero in a stable
quotient.

Choose an infinite left L_x and its actual high minimal resolution.
All sufficiently high labels lie in T_x, which is contained in I_R.
Equation (3.2) makes every Peirce component K of T_x homogeneous
for c(z). Therefore Y_K in Lemma 2.1 is the singleton {c(z)}.
There is a recurrent label in at least one such component, since
L_x has infinite pd. Lemma 2.1 supplies a supported ordinary Tor
tail, contrary to badness. QED.

Equivalently one can group projectives directly by c(z). The high
minimal resolution is a literal direct sum of exact minimal blocks;
the block of a recurrent q has only c(q) as an infinite-pd factor
and ordinary detection forces Tor(R_q,L_{c(q)}) to persist. Its
support D(c(q),q)>0 is built into (3.1). This explains concretely
why the finite-factor image obstruction is killed rather than
renamed.

### Theorem 3.2 — Direct right version

Suppose |I_R|=3, and for EVERY y in I_L the RIGHT socle of e_yA
has factors only in I_R. Then A is infinite +.

#### Proof

Assume A is bad. Every nonempty T_y has at least two labels, by
the direct left singleton-detector bridge of C080. It lies in the
three-element I_R. Its physical right-factor support

\[
S'_y=\{z\in I_R:D(y,z)>0\}
\]

is disjoint from T_y, hence has size at most one; the right socle
condition makes it nonempty. Write S'_y={r(y)}. Any actual right
projective map e_yA->e_wA with different r values has image with
only finite-pd RIGHT simple factors. The target's right socle
condition forces that image zero. All high right resolutions
therefore split into actual blocks with a unique infinite right
composition factor r(y).

In a block where e_yA is recurrent, the actual right tail module X
has Tor_n(X,L_y) nonzero infinitely often. Direct ordinary devissage
in the RIGHT argument, after the finite common bound for the other
right simples, forces Tor_n(R_{r(y)},L_y) nonzero infinitely often.
The physical support D(y,r(y))>0 is from S'_y. This is the same
pair, directly proved. No opposite-infinite-+ theorem and no
equality of I_L with I_R is used. QED.

### Corollary 3.3 — Three common infinite labels close without a socle hypothesis

If I_L(A)=I_R(A)=I has size three, A is infinite +, regardless of
the total number of finite-pd vertices. The equality of the sets
is an EXPLICIT hypothesis, not an opposite-side transfer.

To prove this, assume A bad. For each z in I, the row W_z lies in I,
has at least two labels, and misses z because D(z,z)>0. Hence
W_z=I minus {z}. No visible return now gives D(j,z)=0 for every
other j in I. Thus the actual persistent projectives Ae_z, z in I_R,
have pairwise Hom zero and each has exactly its own label z as its
infinite left-factor carrier. The high left resolution is literally
block diagonal by single projective labels. Lemma 2.1 gives supported
Tor, a contradiction. No socle separation is needed in this case.
This reuses the ordinary Peirce singleton-block mechanism; it does
not assert arbitrary algebras have equal infinite-side label sets.

## 4. Boundary: the finite-factor coupling that the theorem actually removes

For three left infinite simples in a bad A, every persistent left
projective has at most one infinite left factor type by (3.1).
Actual maps between different nonempty types, and maps involving
a projective with empty I_L support, can have only F_L composition
factors in their images. Such images have pd<=d. Nevertheless
finite pd alone does NOT make them zero and does not split the
ordinary resolution. A submodule can have finite pd without the
composition restriction; that still weaker shortcut is also invalid.

Condition (1.1) makes these particular images zero by their simple
socles. That is the load-bearing step, not an assertion that maps
factoring through perfect objects can be discarded in ordinary Tor.
Without (1.1), the cross maps can join the real blocks, and the
present proof stops at (3.2). The exact ordinary issue is now an
actual finite-factor submodule of a persistent projective, rather
than unspecified higher nonformality.

This does not establish a badness-preserving quotient by their sum.
Even when that sum has bounded ambient left pd, new quotient labels
and an ordinary reverse-support comparison must be checked; T16--T18
and C076's separate supported migration gap remain in force.
The theorem supplies no uniform degree cutoff or finite-window test.

## 5. Explicit connected calibration: socle separation is not automatic

Take the radical-square-zero algebra with six vertices
x1,x2,x3,f1,f2,f3, three loops t_i at x_i, arrows a_i:x_i->f_i,
and b1:f1->f2, b2:f2->f3. Its basis is the six identities and
these eight radical elements; EVERY product of two radical elements
is zero. Dimension is fourteen. It is connected and elementary
over every field.

The literal radicals of the LEFT projective covers give

\[
\Omega L_{x_i}=L_{x_i}\oplus L_{f_i},\qquad
\Omega L_{f1}=L_{f2},\quad
\Omega L_{f2}=L_{f3},\quad L_{f3}\text{ projective}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence I_L={x1,x2,x3} and the f pds are 2,1,0. The actual RIGHT
covers have Omega R_{x_i}=R_{x_i}; the f kernels contain R_{x_i}
(and the previous R_f where present). Thus I_R is all six vertices.
Each Ae_{x_i} has in its left socle the finite-pd factor L_{f_i},
so (1.1) fails exactly at persistent right labels x_i.

This is already infinite +: the actual right identity Omega R_x=R_x
gives Tor_n(R_x,L_x)=k in EVERY degree, with nonzero diagonal Peirce
support. It verifies nonautomaticity even for a connected algebra
with exactly three left infinite sources; it is not a bad-algebra
counterexample, a refutation of Theorem 3.1, or evidence of ordinary
screening in a bad A. Infinity follows from literal simple radicals,
not from the support graph. No computation is needed for these
complete one-line cover descriptions.

## 6. Normal adversarial self-audit

1. **Which side is finite?** F_L consists of left finite-pd simples.
   Its uniform bound kills Tor(R_q,L_f) for every right q. The right
   proof uses its separately defined finite right complement.
2. **Socle condition on which projectives?** For the left proof it
   applies to Ae_z with z in I_R, because these are the high left
   projective detector labels. The condition is NOT stated only for
   z in I_L, and the two index sets are not equated.
3. **Missing physical carrier?** It is nonempty because the socle
   of EACH such nonzero projective contains an I_L factor; the
   count-three/no-+ argument alone gives only an upper bound.
4. **Cross-type maps only stably zero?** No. The entire image has
   F_L composition factors and is an actual target submodule.
   Its nonzero simple socle would contradict (1.1), so the map is
   literally zero in the module category.
5. **Exactness after blocking?** Both directions of all cross Hom
   vanish. Therefore the actual high differential is block diagonal,
   and exactness at every high term holds component by component.
   A merely triangular or stable splitting is insufficient.
6. **Graph implies homological return?** No. Recurrence is supplied
   by q in the ACTUAL persistent minimal resolution. Peirce zero
   only proves block decomposition; the actual block resolution
   and ordinary devissage supply the supported return.
7. **Could the selected detector have the wrong physical source?**
   q is chosen from a block with c(q)=j, D(j,q)>0. The block tail
   has only L_j as an infinite left factor, so devissage selects
   this same j for Tor(R_q,L_j).
8. **Finite-pd factors used backwards?** No. Only a module with all
   finite-pd simple factors is bounded. Neither finite pd of a
   module nor projectivity is used to infer finite pd of its factors.
9. **Three replaced by arbitrary p?** No. |W_z|>=2 implies a unique
   possible physical carrier only when |I_L|=3. With four or more
   sources the present proof can have several infinite carrier
   labels in one projective and does not give the conclusion.
10. **Socle hypothesis automatic or inherited?** Neither. Section 5
    is a literal connected negative calibration. No quotient is
    taken in this proof, so no inheritance claim is needed.
11. **New quantification or verification?** No universal cutoff,
    diagonal witness for the positive theorem, window bound or
    independent-verifier status is asserted. The actual Section 5
    calibration has a diagonal witness, a separate fact.
12. **Old branch forgotten?** General finite-factor coupling at
    three sources, four-or-more-infinite-source cases, C076 physical
    support transfer, Target A bounded screening, local-trivial and
    supported-weight cases and higher-output Target B remain OPEN.
13. **Common-label corollary silently transfers sides?** No. It assumes
    I_L=I_R of size three and uses the actual diagonal Peirce entries.
    Finite-pd vertices elsewhere are allowed. General equality is
    false as the distinct actual label sets in T16--T18 demonstrate.
