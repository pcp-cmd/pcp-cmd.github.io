## 1. Setting and ordinary comparison

Let A be elementary finite-dimensional over k. Delete an idempotent E
which is a sum of labelled primitive vertices; a single primitive vertex
is included. Set f=1-E, C=A/AEA, and use a strictly unital augmented
minimal retained model

\[
H=C\oplus\bigoplus_{d\ge1}M_d[d],\qquad M_d=H^{-d}(q).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Each cohomological degree is finite-dimensional. No bound on d is imposed.
The projection p:H -> C is strict: binary pure-C products are those of C,
while a higher product or any product with a negative input has negative
output degree. Test modules Ce_j are pulled back through p with their
ordinary C action and no higher module actions.

The ordinary derived-localisation comparison used in C065--C066 gives

\[
R_z\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}Ce_j
\simeq R_z\otimes_H^{\mathbf L}Ce_j.
\tag{1.2}
\]

These are retained ordinary A modules. The comparison is with the derived
quotient, not an assertion that A -> C is a homological epimorphism.
For the block version, the same localisation inverts 1-E; it does not
require E to be primitive. The full strict model is
A<h>/(hE=Eh=h), dh=E, followed by its retained corner. The conventional
inputs are Proposition 9.2, Proposition 9.6 and Corollary 4.29 of
[Braun--Chuang--Lazarev, Derived localisation of algebras and modules](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.01146).
Over a field the left-properness hypothesis for that strict model holds.

Assume every actual M_d is left C-projective. Its actual columns have
well-defined multiplicities

\[
M_de_j\cong\bigoplus_i(Ce_i)^{K_d(i,j)}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

There is a finite vertex set but possibly infinitely many weighted edges.
Put an edge j -> i of weight d+1 whenever K_d(i,j)>0. Multiplicity affects
word counts but not the period defined below.

## 2. A weighted component has a single endpoint residue

Let V be a cyclic strongly connected component of the actual graph.
Define its period

\[
g_V=\gcd\{\,w(\gamma):\gamma\text{ is a nonempty directed
closed walk in }V\,\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Every edge weight is a positive integer at least two; the displayed gcd
is a positive integer even if the set of edge weights is infinite.
It is the gcd of **closed-walk totals**, not the gcd of individual edges,
and not the weight of one selected cycle.

### Lemma 2.1 — Endpoint residue

For j,z in V, all directed paths j -> z have the same weight modulo g_V.
There are potentials phi_i in Z/g_V with

\[
w(j\longrightarrow z)\equiv\phi_z-\phi_j\pmod {g_V}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The empty path has weight zero. A path between vertices of V cannot leave
V and return.

#### Proof

Choose a base vertex v and a path v -> i for every i. Any two paths
v -> i become closed walks after appending the same path i -> v.
The difference of their weights is therefore divisible by g_V, which
makes phi_i well-defined. Comparing a path v -> j followed by any path
j -> z with the chosen path v -> z gives (2.2).

If a path j -> z passes through h, then j reaches h and h reaches z.
Because z reaches j inside V, h and j are mutually reachable, so h lies
in V. This proves the last assertion. QED.

## 3. The actual ordinary Tor formula

### Theorem 3.1 — Periodic component bridge

Under the actual left-projectivity hypothesis (1.3), suppose g_V>=2.
For all z,j in V,

\[
\boxed{
\left(\sum_{n\ge0}\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_z,Ce_j)t^n\right)_{z,j\in V}
=
\left(I-\sum_{d\ge1}t^{d+1}K_d|_V\right)^{-1}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The inverse means the coefficientwise sum of matrix powers. No rationality,
finite homogeneous generation, finite bimodule pd, formality, negative-degree
ceiling or higher-operation arity bound is asserted or required.

#### Proof

Use the existing negative-weight filtration on the reduced two-sided
H-bar with endpoints R_z and Ce_j. A C radical letter has weight zero
and contributes one to total homological degree. A letter of M_d has
weight d and contributes d+1. Binary bar operations preserve negative
weight, while an operation m_r, r>=3, increases it by r-2.

For a fixed weight, first compute the binary differential b_2. Here
negative-negative products need not vanish. Introduce the auxiliary
increasing filtration by the number p of negative letters **only on
this binary complex**. Products/actions with C preserve p;
negative-negative products decrease p by one. The associated C-block
multibar for a sequence d_1,...,d_p computes

\[
R_z\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}M_{d_1}
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}\cdots
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}M_{d_p}
\otimes_C^{\mathbf L}Ce_j,
\tag{3.2}
\]

shifted by sum(d_i+1). For p=0 it is R_z e_j in degree zero.

Every tensor step on the right has an actual left-projective factor.
More explicitly, the final Ce_j is projective; if X is a finite sum
of Ce_i, then M_d tensor_C X is a sum of actual projective columns
M_de_i. Induction shows the derived tensor word to the right of R_z
is its ordinary tensor, is left-projective, and has no higher homology
after applying R_z tensor_C -. Its dimension is

\[
(K_{d_1}\cdots K_{d_p})_{zj}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus every nonzero associated homology term with endpoints z,j in V
comes from an actual weighted path j -> z. Lemma 2.1 shows that its
total degree n satisfies

\[
n\equiv\phi_z-\phi_j\pmod {g_V}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

All differentials in the auxiliary spectral sequence lower total degree
by one and preserve endpoints. Since g_V>=2, both source and target
cannot lie in the sole residue (3.4). Every such differential is zero.
The binary homology therefore has precisely the weighted-word dimensions
(3.3), despite possibly nonzero binary negative products.

Now return to the negative-weight spectral sequence for the **full**
A_infinity bar. Its binary first page remains concentrated in (3.4).
Every later differential again lowers total degree by one and preserves
endpoints, so it is zero. This includes the effect of pure-C higher
operations and mixed higher operations.

There is no convergence extrapolation: at total degree n, each bar
letter contributes at least one, each negative letter at least two,
and every d is at most n-1. Length, weight and the auxiliary count
are bounded in that degree, and all participating spaces are finite.
Both filtrations converge degreewise to actual homology. Higher-page
vanishing preserves dimensions, without claiming a canonical splitting
of the filtered vector space.

Finally, all paths between vertices of V remain in V. Sum (3.3) over
all finite negative-letter sequences and use (1.2). This gives (3.1).
QED.

Only the projective-column test complexes in the theorem collapse.
No collapse of the quotient-simple screening sequence, ordinary algebra
section, general screening-page bound or local-singular excursion bound
has been proved.

### Corollary 3.2 — Ordinary supported return and branch exclusion

If such a periodic cyclic component exists, A is infinite +.
This conclusion does not require C to be infinite + or the deleted
simple to have infinite pd.

#### Proof

Choose a nonempty closed walk at j in V, selecting an actual degree
for each of its edges, and let L be its total weight. Repeating it gives
a positive diagonal coefficient of (3.1) in each degree rL, r>=1.
Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^A_{rL}(R_j,Ce_j)\ne0.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Apply the ordinary finite-length inequality

\[
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_j,Ce_j)
\le\sum_y [Ce_j:L_y]\,
\dim\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(R_j,L_y).
\tag{3.6}
\]

If every simple factor on the right had eventually zero Tor, the left
side would too. Finitely many factors therefore give one fixed y
returning infinitely often. Since C is elementary,
[Ce_j:L_y]=dim e_yCe_j. Its positive multiplicity gives
e_yCe_j!=0, hence e_yAe_j!=0. The resulting ordinary pair (R_j,L_y)
has exactly the required reverse Peirce support. QED.

The selected simple pair need not be diagonal. The passage from a graph
cycle to (3.5) uses the proved actual spectral-sequence formula, not
support recurrence alone.

### Direct right-projective version

If every actual M_d is instead right C-projective, decompose

\[
e_iM_d\cong\bigoplus_j(e_jC)^{B_d(i,j)}.
\]

Use edges i -> j of weight d+1, and a cyclic component with the same
closed-walk gcd condition. Compute e_iC tensor_H^L L_y directly:
every actual tensor word on the left is right-projective, its
degree-zero homology has dimension (B_{d_1}...B_{d_p})_{iy}, and the
same two filtrations concentrate it in one endpoint residue.
The component series is (I-sum_d t^{d+1}B_d|_V)^(-1).

A repeated closed walk at i gives Tor^A_{rL}(e_iC,L_i)!=0. Its right
composition factors select one fixed R_z with e_iCe_z!=0 and persistent
Tor^A_n(R_z,L_i). This is reverse-supported in A. This proof uses the
right-module bar itself and no opposite-side infinite-+ transfer.

### Relation to the previous closure classes

If every negative degree is odd, every edge weight is even, so each
cyclic component has g_V>=2. C066 is recovered on such components.
For one negative layer M[a], all weights equal a+1; its cyclic
components likewise have period at least a+1. The cyclic part of C065
is included. The present theorem additionally closes actual mixed-parity
periodic components, including cases where the individual edge weights
have gcd one.

## 4. An actual mixed-parity calibration

This calibration is a **nonprimitive block deletion** of an ordinary
finite algebra. It does not claim a primitive deletion realizes this
particular two-layer example.

Let A be the radical-square-zero algebra of the oriented cycle

\[
1\xrightarrow{p}x\xrightarrow{c}0\xrightarrow{q}a
\xrightarrow{\gamma}b\xrightarrow{\delta}1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

There are five primitive idempotents and these five arrows; every
radical product is zero, so dim_k A=10. Delete
E=e_x+e_a+e_b. Then C=k e_0 times k e_1.
The deleted corner Gamma=EAE is the disjoint union of x and the
hereditary line a -> b.

In algebraic product order, U=fAE has boundary arrows c,delta and
V=EAf has boundary arrows p,q. The normalized retained Gamma bar
has exactly the passages

\[
u=c h_x p\in e_0M_1e_1,\qquad
v=\delta h_b\gamma h_a q\in e_1M_2e_0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

There are no others: no internal arrow is possible at x; at a,b only
the single gamma exists and cannot be repeated. Every normalized
passage differential is zero, since it multiplies radical arrows.
Every join VU is zero as well. Thus the retained homology has only
M_1=ku and M_2=kv, with all negative binary products zero.

There are no nonunit C letters, since C equals its vertex algebra.
Degree and composability forbid all higher negative outputs: u,u is
not composable; every other binary negative product is too negative;
an r>=3 product has degree at most 2-2r<-2. Hence this actual minimal
model is formal square-zero and every layer is projective on both sides.

Its two actual edges have weights two and three. The gcd of the
individual weights is one, but every nonempty closed walk has weight
5r, so g_V=5. The theorem gives

\[
T(t)=\frac{1}{1-t^5}
\begin{pmatrix}1&t^2\\t^3&1\end{pmatrix},
\tag{4.3}
\]

with rows R_0,R_1 and columns Ce_0=L_0,Ce_1=L_1.

There is also a separate all-degree ambient proof. The ordinary
simple-pair normalized bar has zero differential because J(A)^2=0;
its basis is the directed paths of length n in the five-cycle.
Exactly one such path starts at each vertex. Among retained endpoints,
the diagonal paths have lengths 0 mod 5, paths 1 -> 0 have lengths
2 mod 5, and paths 0 -> 1 have lengths 3 mod 5. These are precisely
(4.3), over every field. Each simple has a periodic minimal resolution
following that cycle, with period five. A is infinite +, not a Gap-A
counterexample or an unbounded-screening realization.

\[
\sum_n\dim\operatorname{Tor}^{A5}_n(R_1,L_1)t^n=1+t^2.

The old actual simple resolution P_1 <- P_2 <- P_1 proves (5.2).

\[
\left(1-\sum_{d\ge1}t^{d+1}\right)^{-1}

## 7. Normal adversarial audit

1. **Actual module:** (1.3) uses actual projective negative homology,
   not a projective term in its resolution. C068's counterexample
   remains valid and the Long01 reverse implication is unchanged.
2. **One stated side:** every negative layer must be projective on the
   same chosen side. Alternating projective sides is not the hypothesis.
3. **All weighted closed walks:** a single closed walk of length L>=2
   is insufficient. Additional weighted walks can make the gcd one.
   Nor may one use gcd of individual edge weights: (4.1) has weights
   two and three but closed-walk period five.
4. **Component scope:** the formula is asserted for both endpoints
   inside the qualifying SCC, not for all retained pairs. The proof
   that a weighted path cannot leave and re-enter is necessary.
5. **Binary products:** they are allowed. The negative-letter count
   is applied only to b_2; it is not asserted to filter the full
   A_infinity differential. The full argument returns to negative weight.
6. **Residue mechanism:** degree-minus-one differentials vanish only
   after the actual C-block homology is proved to have one residue.
   A naked support graph or arbitrary degree labels do not supply it.
7. **Convergence:** both filtrations are finite in each actual total
   degree. No uniform negative ceiling is silently introduced.
8. **Ordinary survival:** the test modules are ordinary inflated C
   projectives; (1.2) and actual bar convergence produce ordinary
   A-Tor. No singular, stable or Tate comparison is substituted.
9. **Visibility:** (3.6) gives a fixed simple pair with e_yAe_j!=0.
   It need not be diagonal or any preselected persistent pair.
10. **Calibration scope:** Section 4 deletes a three-vertex block;

    theorem. It is already infinite +.
11. **Aperiodic obstruction:** actual projectivity, perfectness and

12. **Global scope:** this excludes a stated periodic class in any

## 8. Route choice and the remaining global question

The existing N-Vertex-Main-Route.md already shows that associative finite
generation of the actual Yoneda algebra would close the global problem,
but its required generation statement remains open. The reused Saorin
seven-dimensional example has zero positive simple self-Ext, persistent
supported off-diagonal Ext and an infinitely generated square-zero
positive Yoneda ideal. Requiring a diagonal witness would lose it.

The conventional A_infinity generation result reviewed here supplies
generation from degrees zero and one by higher operations, rather than
associative finite generation. Its constructive proof uses a finite
composition series in the first middle module; it does not prohibit
unbounded iterated operation trees. This review yields no new closure
claim. See [Jasso, On a theorem of Keller on Yoneda algebras of simple modules](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.14004)
and [Saorin, On the vanishing of self extensions over Artin algebras](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0306043).

The present checkpoint instead proves an ordinary closure mechanism.
C065's one-layer and C066's odd-many-layer cyclic cases join the more
general periodic-component theorem. Genuinely aperiodic actual components,
nonprojective layers, nonperfect acyclic bounded screening and general
unbounded-degree higher-output screening still require a new bridge,
strict descent or terminating bound. T9's ordinary radical dimension
descent remains a separate valid global route, whose universal
reduction-existence step is unresolved. Target A, general local-trivial
and supported-weight branches remain on the map.
